## 2. Section 1.1: Finite Sums (`01-finite-sums.md`)

**Opening frame** (~120 words). *Sets and Functions*, Chapter 3 defined \( \sum \), \( \prod \) and powers of real numbers by recursion and proved three formulas with them (@fd2:prp-sum-first-n, @fd2:prp-geometric-sum, @fd2:thm-bernoulli). Analysis rearranges such sums on every page, and the algebra chapters of this volume need the same sums with terms in ℤ/n, in a field, in ℂ and among polynomials. This section states the rules once, for any commutative ring, and teaches the one computational move that turns a sum into two terms: telescoping.

**Subsections and blocks, in order.**

1. `## Sums and powers in any commutative ring`. Hook (~80 words): fd2's recursions use only a 0, a 1, + and ·, so they run in any commutative ring (@fd3:def-commutative-ring-and-field); state the index-from-0 form \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k \) (empty sum \( \sum_{k=0}^{-1} = 0 \), the recursion appends the last term) and products likewise; general bounds by the shift remark after @fd2:def-sum-product.
   - **Moved:** the "Powers" paragraph and `prp-power-laws-commutative-ring` verbatim from F4.1 §01 (statement (a)–(d), Idea, proof), with one sentence replacing the ℂ-specific remarks: "For \( R = \nR \) these are @fd2:prp-power-laws (a)–(c); the proofs are the same inductions and use only the ring axioms."
   - **Natural multiples** (prose + fold). Recall fd3's \( n \cdot a \) (*Number Systems*, Section 2.3, "Multiples"). In ℝ, \( n \cdot a = na \) (one sentence: \( n \cdot a = (n \cdot 1)a \) by (e) below and \( n \cdot 1 \) is the real number \( n \), @fd3:def-naturals-in-ordered-field).
   - **`lem-natural-multiples`** `[Natural Multiples in a Commutative Ring]`: "Let \( R \) be a commutative ring, \( a, b \in R \) and \( m, k \in \nN \). (a) \( (m + k) \cdot a = m \cdot a + k \cdot a \). (b) \( m \cdot (a + b) = m \cdot a + m \cdot b \). (c) \( (m \cdot a)b = m \cdot (ab) \). (d) \( (mk) \cdot a = m \cdot (k \cdot a) \). (e) \( m \cdot a = (m \cdot 1)a \)." *Main line:* statement and the sentence "each is one induction on the recursion for \( m \cdot a \)". *Fold* `title="The inductions for natural multiples"`: proofs of (a)–(d) are those of F5.2 `lem-integer-multiples` (i)–(iv) (moved verbatim; (a) on \( k \), (b), (c), (d) on \( m \)); (e) from (c) with \( a = 1 \), (F6), (F7).
   - **Moved, changed:** `lem-ring-finite-sums` `[Finite Sums in a Commutative Ring]`, statement verbatim except (c): "\( \sum_{k=0}^{n} m \cdot a_k = m \cdot \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k \) for every \( m \in \nN \)." Proof: (a), (b), (d), (f) verbatim; (c) is (b) with \( c = m \cdot 1 \) by `lem-natural-multiples` (e); (e) by induction on \( n \): \( -(s + a_{n+1}) = (-s) + (-a_{n+1}) \) (uniqueness of negatives, @fd3:prp-ring-algebra). Main line keeps statement + "In words" + Idea; the proof goes into a fold `title="Proof of the sum rules"`. (Nothing in the book cites (c) for negative \( m \); F5.2's `lem-integer-multiples` (d) supplies negative multiples where Ch4 needs them.)
   - **Quick check:** "In ℝ, compute \( \sum_{k=0}^{3} (2k - 1) \), \( \sum_{k=4}^{3} k^2 \) and \( \prod_{k=1}^{0} 7 \)." *Solution:* \( -1 + 1 + 3 + 5 = 8 \); \( 0 \) (empty sum); \( 1 \) (empty product).

2. `## Reindexing and comparing`.
   - **`prp-sum-reindex`** `[Reindexing a Finite Sum]`: "Let \( R \) be a commutative ring, \( n \in \nN \) and \( a_0, \dots, a_n \in R \). (a) *(Reversal.)* \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_{n-k} \). (b) *(Splitting.)* For every integer \( m \) with \( -1 \le m \le n \), \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k = \sum_{k=0}^{m} a_k + \sum_{k=m+1}^{n} a_k \)." Idea (3 lines): (b) by induction on \( n \ge m \), each step one split of the last term; (a) by induction on \( n \) **for all lists at once**, splitting the first term with `lem-ring-finite-sums` (f). Proof: (b) base \( n = m \): second sum empty. Step: split the last term of both the left side and \( \sum_{k=m+1}^{n+1} \). (a) base \( a_0 = a_0 \). Step: \( \sum_{k=0}^{n+1} a_{n+1-k} = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_{n+1-k} + a_0 \); apply the hypothesis to the list \( c_j \coloneqq a_{j+1} \) (\( 0 \le j \le n \)): \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} c_{n-k} = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_{k+1} \); then `lem-ring-finite-sums` (f).
   - **`exm-odd-sum-reversal`** `[Odd numbers by reversal]`: "Show \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} (2k + 1) = (n + 1)^2 \)." *Solution:* call the sum \( S \). By reversal, \( S = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (2(n - k) + 1) \). Adding termwise (`lem-ring-finite-sums` (a)), \( 2S = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (2n + 2) = (n + 1)(2n + 2) \) (a constant summed over \( n + 1 \) indices is \( (n + 1) \cdot c \): one-line induction, written out), so \( S = (n + 1)^2 \). Aftermath: reversal pairs the first term with the last.
   - **`prp-sum-order`** `[Comparing Sums Termwise]`: "Let \( F \) be an ordered field, \( n \in \nN \) and \( a_k, b_k \in F \) for \( 0 \le k \le n \). (a) If \( a_k \le b_k \) for every \( k \), then \( \sum a_k \le \sum b_k \); if moreover \( a_j < b_j \) for some \( j \), the inequality is strict. (b) If \( a_k \ge 0 \) for every \( k \), then \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k \ge \sum_{k=0}^{m} a_k \) for every \( -1 \le m \le n \), and \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k \ge a_j \) for every \( j \le n \). (c) If \( a_k \le M \) for every \( k \), then \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k \le (n + 1)M \). (d) *(Triangle inequality for sums.)* \( \abs{\sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k} \le \sum_{k=0}^{n} \abs{a_k} \)." All sums run over \( 0 \le k \le n \). Proof: (a) induction, @fd3:prp-ordered-ring-rules (b) at each step (strictness carried); (b) split (`prp-sum-reindex` (b)) and the tail is \( \ge 0 \) by (a) against the zero list; the single term: split around \( j \) twice, both outer parts \( \ge 0 \); (c) is (a) with \( b_k = M \); (d) induction with @fd3:prp-absolute-value-ordered-field (e). Aftermath: (b) "drop non-negative terms" and (c) "replace each term by the largest" are the moves of 1.4; @fd2:prp-finite-max supplies an \( M \).
   - **Warning:** "\( \sum a_k b_k \) is not \( (\sum a_k)(\sum b_k) \), and \( \sum 1/a_k \) is not \( 1/\sum a_k \): with \( n = 1 \), \( a_0 = a_1 = b_0 = b_1 = 1 \), the first is \( 2 \) and the second \( 4 \); and \( 1/1 + 1/1 = 2 \ne 1/2 \)."
   - **`prp-double-sum`** `[Double Sums over a Rectangle]`: "Let \( R \) be a commutative ring, \( m, n \in \nN \) and \( a_{ij} \in R \) for \( 0 \le i \le m \), \( 0 \le j \le n \). Then \( \sum_{i=0}^{m} \sum_{j=0}^{n} a_{ij} = \sum_{j=0}^{n} \sum_{i=0}^{m} a_{ij} \). In particular \( \bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{m} x_i\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{n} y_j\bigr) = \sum_{i=0}^{m} \sum_{j=0}^{n} x_i y_j \)." Proof: induction on \( m \), step = `lem-ring-finite-sums` (a) applied to the inner sums; the product form by `lem-ring-finite-sums` (b) twice. One sentence aftermath: the analysis volumes need it for double series (*Analysis on the Real Line*; prose only).

3. `## Telescoping`. Hook (~90 words, a goal stated first): compute \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k(k+1)} \). Listing \( \frac12, \frac16, \frac1{12}, \frac1{20} \) and the partial sums \( \frac12, \frac23, \frac34, \frac45 \) suggests \( 1 - \frac1{n+1} \); induction would confirm a guess, but we want a way to **find** the value.
   - **`[Scratch work]`** for `exm-telescoping-reciprocals` (≤ 20 lines): try to write each term as a difference of consecutive values, \( \frac1{k(k+1)} = b_k - b_{k+1} \); the factors \( k, k+1 \) suggest \( b_k = \frac1k \); check \( \frac1k - \frac1{k+1} = \frac{(k+1) - k}{k(k+1)} \). Givens/Goal table:

     | Givens | Goal |
     |---|---|
     | \( a_k = \frac{1}{k(k+1)} \) for \( 1 \le k \le n \) | a closed form for \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k \) |
     | \( a_k = \frac1k - \frac1{k+1} \) (checked by @fd3:lem-field-fraction-rules (c)) | \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} \bigl(\frac1k - \frac1{k+1}\bigr) \) |
     | the gap: every \( \frac1{k+1} \) with \( k < n \) is cancelled by the next term | \( 1 - \frac1{n+1} \): needs a theorem that does the cancelling |

   - **`thm-telescoping`** `[Telescoping Sums and Products]`: "Let \( R \) be a commutative ring, \( n \in \nN \) and \( b_0, \dots, b_{n+1} \in R \). (a) \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} (b_{k+1} - b_k) = b_{n+1} - b_0 \). (b) If \( R \) is a field and every \( b_k \ne 0 \), then \( \prod_{k=0}^{n} \frac{b_{k+1}}{b_k} = \frac{b_{n+1}}{b_0} \)." Proof (≤ 6 lines): (a) induction on \( n \): base \( b_1 - b_0 \); step adds \( b_{n+2} - b_{n+1} \) to \( b_{n+1} - b_0 \). (b) same with products; @fd3:lem-field-fraction-rules (b).
   - **`exm-telescoping-reciprocals`** `[A telescoping sum]` — the hook's sum: solution in three lines with \( b_k = -\frac1{k+1} \) after the shift to index from 0, or directly with the general bounds; value \( 1 - \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{n}{n+1} \).
   - **`exm-method-telescoping`** `[Method: telescoping]`. **Template.** To find \( \sum a_k \), write \( a_k = b_{k+1} - b_k \) (or \( b_k - b_{k+1} \)) for a sequence \( b \) you choose; then the sum is the last \( b \) minus the first (@thm-telescoping). To *verify* a guessed formula \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k = F(n) \), check \( F(n) - F(n-1) = a_n \) and \( F(0) = a_0 \): that is telescoping with \( b_k = F(k-1) \). **When to use it.** Quotients whose denominator is a product of shifted factors; differences of powers or roots; any sum whose partial sums you can guess. **The usual failure.** A difference \( b_{k+2} - b_k \) that skips a step: it leaves **two** terms at each end, not one.
   - **`lem-difference-of-powers`** (moved; statement verbatim). Short Idea citing the box: the product \( (z - w) \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} z^k w^{n-1-k} \) multiplies out to \( \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} (z^{k+1} w^{n-1-k} - z^k w^{n-k}) \). **Proof changed:** with \( b_k \coloneqq z^k w^{n-k} \) (\( 0 \le k \le n \)), `lem-ring-finite-sums` (b) and (a) with (F9) give the displayed sum of \( b_{k+1} - b_k \) over \( 0 \le k \le n - 1 \) (using \( z \cdot z^k = z^{k+1} \), \( w \cdot w^{n-1-k} = w^{n-k} \) by `prp-power-laws-commutative-ring` (a)); @thm-telescoping (a) with \( n - 1 \) in place of \( n \) gives \( b_n - b_0 = z^n - w^n \). Particular case \( w = 1 \) as before. Aftermath: with \( z = r \), \( w = 1 \) in ℝ this is @fd2:prp-geometric-sum read backwards; Section 1.5 uses it for roots.
   - **`exm-telescoping-squares`** `[The sum of the squares]`: "Find \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^2 \)." *Solution:* \( (k + 1)^3 - k^3 = 3k^2 + 3k + 1 \) (expand). Sum over \( 1 \le k \le n \), telescoping: \( (n + 1)^3 - 1 = 3\sum k^2 + 3 \cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2} + n \) (@fd2:prp-sum-first-n). Solve: \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^2 = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} \). Check \( n = 3 \): \( 14 = \frac{3 \cdot 4 \cdot 7}{6} \).
   - **Non-example by minimal change** (fold `title="When the cancellation skips"`): \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} (b_{k+2} - b_k) = b_{n+2} + b_{n+1} - b_1 - b_0 \) (split into two telescoping sums by `lem-ring-finite-sums` (a)); for \( b_k = \frac1{k+1} \): \( n = 0 \) gives \( \frac13 - 1 \).
   - **Quick check:** "Which \( b_k \) makes \( \frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k+1)} \) telescope?" *Solution:* \( \frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k+1)} = \frac12\bigl(\frac1{2k-1} - \frac1{2k+1}\bigr) \), so \( b_k = -\frac{1}{2(2k-1)} \) gives \( a_k = b_{k+1} - b_k \).
   - **Telescoping products** (two sentences + example in prose): \( \prod_{k=2}^{n} \bigl(1 - \frac1{k^2}\bigr) = \prod \frac{k-1}{k} \cdot \prod \frac{k+1}{k} = \frac1n \cdot \frac{n+1}{2} = \frac{n+1}{2n} \) for \( n \ge 2 \) (products of products split as sums do; one line by induction, ledgered).
   - **Looking ahead** box: "Chapter 4 uses these sums with terms in a field, and Chapter 9 proves the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with them."
4. **Exercises.**
   - **A1** (`exr-finite-sums-a1`). (a) Compute \( \sum_{k=5}^{4} k \) and \( \prod_{k=1}^{3} (k + 1) \). (b) True or false: \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k b_k = (\sum a_k)(\sum b_k) \). (c) Rewrite \( \sum_{k=2}^{6} k^2 \) with index from 0. (d) Which method finds \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{2}{k(k+2)} \)? *Solution.* (a) \( 0 \); \( 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 24 \). (b) False: \( n = 1 \), all terms 1: \( 2 \ne 4 \). (c) \( \sum_{j=0}^{4} (j + 2)^2 \) (shift by 2). (d) Telescoping with a skip: \( \frac{2}{k(k+2)} = \frac1k - \frac1{k+2} \).
   - **B1** (`-b1`, `[B1: Telescoping computations]`). Find (a) \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k+1)} \); (b) \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k \cdot k! \). *Solution.* (a) By the Quick check, \( \frac12 \bigl(1 - \frac{1}{2n+1}\bigr) = \frac{n}{2n+1} \). (b) \( k \cdot k! = (k + 1)! - k! \), so the sum is \( (n + 1)! - 1 \). Check \( n = 2 \): \( 1 + 4 = 5 = 6 - 1 \).
   - **B2** (`-b2`, `[B2: Prove or disprove]`). For all \( n \ge 1 \) and reals \( a_k \): (a) \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k + 1) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k + n \). (b) \( \bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k\bigr)^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k^2 \). (c) \( \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_{n-k} b_k = \sum_{k=0}^{n} a_k b_{n-k} \). (d) \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k(k+2)} = \frac12 \bigl(1 - \frac1{n+2}\bigr) \). *Solution.* (a) True: `lem-ring-finite-sums` (a) and a constant sum. (b) False: \( n = 2 \), \( a = (1, 1) \): \( 4 \ne 2 \). (c) True: reversal applied to \( c_k \coloneqq a_k b_{n-k} \). (d) False, although it holds at \( n = 1 \) (both \( \frac13 \)): at \( n = 2 \) the left side is \( \frac{11}{24} \), the right \( \frac38 \). The correct value, by the skipping telescope, is \( \frac12\bigl(\frac32 - \frac1{n+1} - \frac1{n+2}\bigr) \).

## 3. Section 1.2: The Binomial Theorem (`02-the-binomial-theorem.md`)

**Opening frame** (~110 words): \( (1 + x)^n \) is the most used polynomial in analysis (lower bounds for powers, Section 1.4) and the source of Fermat's little theorem in algebra (Chapter 4). Its expansion in every commutative ring is proved here, once. Coefficients are defined by Pascal's rule and never by division, which keeps the theorem valid where \( p! = 0 \).

1. **Moved verbatim:** `## Binomial coefficients` from F5.2 §06: `def-binomial-coefficients`, the three facts, the table rows 0–6, `prp-binomial-absorption` (Idea, proof). The remark "The formula … we neither prove nor use it" is **replaced** by item 2.
2. **`prp-binomial-factorial`** `[Binomial Coefficients and Factorials]`: "For \( n, k \in \nN \) with \( k \le n \): (a) \( \binom{n}{k} \, k! \, (n - k)! = n! \); (b) \( \binom{n}{k} = \binom{n}{n-k} \). Hence in ℝ, \( \binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!} \)." Idea (3 lines): induct on \( n \) for all \( k \le n \); the absorption identity turns \( (j + 1)! \) on the left into \( j! \) and a factor \( n + 1 \). Proof: base \( n = 0 \). Step, \( k = 0 \): \( 1 \cdot 1 \cdot (n + 1)! \). \( k = j + 1 \le n + 1 \): \( \binom{n+1}{j+1}(j+1)!(n-j)! = \bigl[(j+1)\binom{n+1}{j+1}\bigr] j!(n-j)! = (n+1)\binom{n}{j} j!(n-j)! = (n+1) n! \) by @prp-binomial-absorption and the hypothesis at \( j \le n \); \( (n+1) - (j+1) = n - j \). (b): both \( \binom{n}{k} \) and \( \binom{n}{n-k} \) times the non-zero natural number \( k!(n-k)! \) give \( n! \) (by (a) at \( k \) and at \( n - k \)); cancel in ℚ (@fd3:lem-field-fraction-rules (d)). Aftermath: the formula is a fact about ℕ, so it holds in ℝ; division by \( k!(n-k)! \) is legal there, not in \( \nF_p \) (Chapter 4, prose).

## 4. Section 1.3: Inequalities for Sums and Products (`03-inequalities-for-sums-and-products.md`)

**Opening frame** (~110 words): Bernoulli's inequality (@fd2:thm-bernoulli) compares a power with a linear function. This section sharpens it to a strict inequality, extends it from powers to products of different factors, and uses it to prove the inequality between the arithmetic and geometric means, the tool for bounding a product by a power. Cauchy–Schwarz waits for vectors (Chapter 9).

1. **`lem-bernoulli-strict`** `[Strict Bernoulli Inequality]`: "Let \( x \in \nR \) with \( x \ge -1 \) and \( x \ne 0 \). Then \( (1 + x)^n > 1 + nx \) for every integer \( n \ge 2 \)." Idea (2 lines): as in @fd2:thm-bernoulli; the extra term \( nx^2 \) is now positive. Proof: @fd2:thm-induction with base 2. \( n = 2 \): \( 1 + 2x + x^2 > 1 + 2x \) since \( x^2 > 0 \) (@fd3:prp-ordered-ring-rules (e)). Step: \( 1 + x \ge 0 \), so \( (1 + x)^{n+1} \ge (1 + nx)(1 + x) = 1 + (n+1)x + nx^2 > 1 + (n+1)x \). (Multiplying a strict inequality by \( 1 + x \ge 0 \) gives \( \ge \); the strictness comes from \( nx^2 > 0 \). The drafter states this.)
2. **`prp-weierstrass-product`** `[Products of Factors Below One]`: "Let \( n \in \nN \) and \( a_1, \dots, a_n \in \nR \) with \( 0 \le a_k \le 1 \). Then \( \prod_{k=1}^{n} (1 - a_k) \ge 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k \)." Hook: Bernoulli with \( x = -a \) is the case of equal factors. Proof: induction from \( n = 0 \) (\( 1 \ge 1 \)); step multiplies the hypothesis by \( 1 - a_{n+1} \ge 0 \): \( \ge (1 - s)(1 - a_{n+1}) = 1 - s - a_{n+1} + s a_{n+1} \ge 1 - (s + a_{n+1}) \), as \( s a_{n+1} \ge 0 \) (\( s = \sum_{k \le n} a_k \ge 0 \) by @prp-sum-order (a)). Aftermath: the analysis volumes use it for infinite products (prose).
   - **Quick check:** "Bound \( 0.99 \cdot 0.98 \cdot 0.97 \cdot 0.96 \) below by the proposition." *Solution:* \( a = (0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.04) \); \( \ge 1 - 0.10 = 0.90 \) (actual value about \( 0.9035 \)).
3. `## The arithmetic and geometric means`. Hook (~100 words): a rectangle of perimeter 4 has area at most 1; in symbols, \( ab \le \bigl(\frac{a+b}{2}\bigr)^2 \), because the difference is \( \bigl(\frac{a-b}{2}\bigr)^2 \). For \( n \) numbers, a square is not enough.
   - **`thm-am-gm`** `[Arithmetic and Geometric Means]`: "Let \( n \ge 1 \) be an integer, \( a_1, \dots, a_n \in \nR \) with every \( a_k \ge 0 \), and \( A \coloneqq \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k \). Then (a) \( a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n \le A^n \); (b) equality holds **if and only if** \( a_1 = a_2 = \dots = a_n \)." (Roots are not yet available; the root form is `cor-am-gm-roots`, 1.5.)
   - *Try it first (ten minutes): prove (a) for \( n = 3 \), or explain what goes wrong when you add a third number to the case \( n = 2 \).*
   - **`[Scratch work]`** (≤ 20 lines): the naive induction multiplies \( a_1 \cdots a_n \le A_n^n \) by \( a_{n+1} \) and then needs \( A_n^n a_{n+1} \le A_{n+1}^{n+1} \), a comparison of means of different lengths. Divide by \( A_n^{n+1} \) (assume all \( a_k > 0 \)): the goal becomes \( \bigl(\frac{A_{n+1}}{A_n}\bigr)^{n+1} \ge \frac{a_{n+1}}{A_n} \), a power against something linear — Bernoulli's shape. With \( x \coloneqq \frac{A_{n+1}}{A_n} - 1 \), \( (n+1)x = \frac{a_{n+1} - A_n}{A_n} \) because \( (n+1)A_{n+1} = nA_n + a_{n+1} \). Givens/Goal table:

     | Givens | Goal |
     |---|---|
     | \( a_k > 0 \); \( a_1 \cdots a_n \le A_n^n \) | \( a_1 \cdots a_{n+1} \le A_{n+1}^{n+1} \) |
     | multiply by \( a_{n+1} > 0 \) | enough: \( A_n^n a_{n+1} \le A_{n+1}^{n+1} \) |
     | \( x \coloneqq A_{n+1}/A_n - 1 > -1 \); Bernoulli with exponent \( n + 1 \) | \( (1 + x)^{n+1} \ge 1 + (n+1)x = a_{n+1}/A_n \): the gap closes |

   - **Proof** (~200 words). (a) If some \( a_j = 0 \), the product is \( 0 \le A^n \) (\( A \ge 0 \), @fd2:prp-power-laws (e) or \( 0^n \)). Let all \( a_k > 0 \); write \( A_m \) for the mean of the first \( m \). Induction on \( n \) (@fd2:thm-induction, base 1: \( a_1 = A_1 \)). Step as in the table: \( A_n > 0 \); \( x > -1 \) as \( A_{n+1} > 0 \); @fd2:thm-bernoulli; multiply by \( A_n^{n+1} > 0 \) (with \( (A_{n+1}/A_n)^{n+1} A_n^{n+1} = A_{n+1}^{n+1} \), @prp-power-laws-commutative-ring (c)); then \( A_{n+1}^{n+1} \ge A_n^n a_{n+1} \ge a_1 \cdots a_n a_{n+1} \). (b) (⇐) all equal to \( a \): both sides \( a^n \). (⇒) contrapositive: suppose not all equal. If some \( a_j = 0 \), some \( a_i > 0 \), so \( A > 0 \) and \( 0 < A^n \). If all positive, induct on \( n \ge 2 \) the statement "not all equal ⇒ strict": in the step, either \( a_1, \dots, a_n \) are not all equal (strict by hypothesis in the second inequality) or they are all equal to \( A_n \) and \( a_{n+1} \ne A_n \), so \( x \ne 0 \) and @lem-bernoulli-strict makes the first inequality strict. Base \( n = 2 \): \( A^2 - a_1 a_2 = \bigl(\frac{a_1 - a_2}{2}\bigr)^2 > 0 \). **Self-explanation prompt** at the end of the Idea: "*Before reading on: where will \( x > -1 \) be needed, and why is it true?*"

## 5. Section 1.4: Estimates for Limits (`04-estimates-for-limits.md`)

**Opening frame** (~120 words): *Number Systems*, Chapter 4 defined \( a_n \to a \) in an ordered field (@fd3:def-sequence-limit-ordered-field) and proved the limit laws. In ℝ, a proof that \( a_n \to a \) is an inequality \( \abs{a_n - a} < \varepsilon \) to be forced for large \( n \), and the work is to replace \( \abs{a_n - a} \) by something simpler. This section names the moves, so that *Analysis on the Real Line* can cite them.

1. **`exm-first-estimate`** `[A first estimate]` (worked in full; faded triple, stage 1): "Show \( \frac{n + 3}{2n^2 - 1} \to 0 \) in ℝ."
   - **`[Scratch work]`** (≤ 18 lines): want \( \frac{n+3}{2n^2 - 1} < \varepsilon \); solving exactly is a quadratic inequality; instead enlarge: numerator \( n + 3 \le 2n \) when \( n \ge 3 \); denominator \( 2n^2 - 1 \ge n^2 \) when \( n \ge 1 \); so the term is \( \le \frac{2n}{n^2} = \frac2n \), and \( \frac2n < \varepsilon \) once \( n > \frac2\varepsilon \). Givens/Goal table:

     | Givens | Goal |
     |---|---|
     | \( \varepsilon > 0 \) arbitrary | \( N \) with \( \abs{a_n - 0} < \varepsilon \) for all \( n \ge N \) |
     | for \( n \ge 3 \): \( 0 < a_n \le \frac2n \) | enough: \( \frac2n < \varepsilon \) |
     | Archimedes: \( N_1 > \frac2\varepsilon \) | \( N \coloneqq \max\{3, N_1\} \): the gap closes |

   - *Proof* (forward, constant first): let \( \varepsilon > 0 \); by @fd3:def-archimedean-ordered-field (ℝ Archimedean, @fd3:thm-cof-complete-archimedean) choose \( N_1 \in \nN \) with \( N_1 > 2/\varepsilon \), depending on \( \varepsilon \); \( N \coloneqq \max\{3, N_1\} \). For \( n \ge N \): \( 2n^2 - 1 \ge n^2 > 0 \), \( n + 3 \le 2n \), so \( 0 < a_n \le \frac2n \le \frac2N < \varepsilon \) (@fd3:prp-reciprocal-order).
2. **`exm-method-simpler-bound`** `[Method: bound by something simpler]`. **Template.** To force \( \abs{a_n - a} < \varepsilon \): (1) find \( b_n \) with \( \abs{a_n - a} \le b_n \) for \( n \ge N_0 \), and \( b_n \) so simple (\( C/n \), \( C/\sqrt n \), \( Cq^n \)) that \( b_n < \varepsilon \) can be solved; (2) solve it, getting \( N_1 \) from \( \varepsilon \); (3) in the proof, set \( N \coloneqq \max\{N_0, N_1\} \) first and justify last. **When to use it.** Whenever \( \abs{a_n - a} < \varepsilon \) cannot be solved for \( n \) directly. **The usual failure.** Bounding in the wrong direction (making a numerator smaller), and an \( N \) that depends on \( n \).
3. **`exm-method-dominant-term`** `[Method: keep the dominant term]`. **Template.** In a quotient of sums, keep the term that grows fastest and absorb the rest: a lower-order term is at most a fixed fraction of the dominant one for \( n \ge N_0 \) (\( 5 \le \frac12 n^2 \) once \( n \ge 4 \)), so \( n^2 - 5 \ge \frac12 n^2 \). Then the quotient is bounded by a quotient of single powers. **When to use it.** Rational expressions in \( n \), and \( \sqrt n \), \( \log \)-free polynomial growth. **The usual failure.** Subtracting in the denominator without a range: \( n^2 - 5 \ge \frac12 n^2 \) is false for \( n \le 3 \).
   - **`exm-dominant-term`** `[A limit that is not zero]`: "Show \( \frac{2n^2 + 3n}{n^2 - 5} \to 2 \)." *Solution:* \( \abs{a_n - 2} = \frac{3n + 10}{n^2 - 5} \) for \( n \ge 3 \); for \( n \ge 4 \): \( 3n + 10 \le 6n \) (needs \( n \ge \frac{10}{3} \)) and \( n^2 - 5 \ge \frac12 n^2 \); so \( \le \frac{12}{n} \). \( N \coloneqq \max\{4, N_1\} \) with \( N_1 > \frac{12}{\varepsilon} \).
   - **Quick check:** "For which \( n \) is \( n^2 - 7n \ge \frac12 n^2 \)?" *Solution:* iff \( \frac12 n^2 \ge 7n \) iff \( n \ge 14 \) (for \( n \ge 1 \)), or \( n = 0 \).
4. `## Powers`. Bernoulli and the binomial bounds turn powers into simple bounds.
   - **`prp-power-null`** `[Geometric Sequences Tend to Zero]`: "Let \( r \in \nR \) with \( \abs{r} < 1 \). (a) If \( r \ne 0 \) and \( h \coloneqq \frac{1}{\abs{r}} - 1 \), then \( h > 0 \) and \( \abs{r}^n < \frac{1}{nh} \) for every \( n \ge 1 \). (b) \( r^n \to 0 \)." Proof: \( \frac1{\abs r} > 1 \) (@fd3:prp-reciprocal-order); @fd2:thm-bernoulli: \( (1 + h)^n \ge 1 + nh > nh \); invert. (b) \( r = 0 \): \( 0^n = 0 \) for \( n \ge 1 \); else the method with \( b_n = \frac{1}{nh} \), \( \abs{r^n} = \abs{r}^n \) (@fd3:prp-absolute-value-ordered-field (b), induction).
   - **`prp-linear-over-power-null`** `[Powers Beat n]`: "Let \( r > 1 \) and \( h \coloneqq r - 1 \). Then \( \frac{n}{r^n} \le \frac{4}{n h^2} \) for every \( n \ge 2 \), and \( \frac{n}{r^n} \to 0 \)." Proof: @cor-binomial-real (b) with \( k = 2 \): \( r^n \ge \frac{n(n-1)}{2}h^2 \ge \frac{n^2}{4} h^2 \) (\( n - 1 \ge \frac{n}{2} \) for \( n \ge 2 \)); divide; the method.
   - **`exm-geometric-comparison`** `[Factorials beat powers of two]`: "Show \( \frac{1}{n!} \le \frac{1}{2^{n-1}} \) for \( n \ge 1 \), hence \( \frac{1}{n!} \to 0 \)." *Solution:* \( n! = \prod_{k=1}^{n} k \ge \prod_{k=2}^{n} 2 = 2^{n-1} \) (@fd2:def-sum-product, termwise products of positives, induction); then @prp-power-null with \( r = \frac12 \) and the comparison \( 0 < \frac1{n!} \le (\frac12)^{n-1} \).
   - **Warning:** "\( \frac1{n^2} \le \varepsilon \) is not \( \frac1{n^2} < \varepsilon \): the definition asks for strict inequality, and the choice \( N = 1/\varepsilon \) (when an integer) can give equality at \( n = N = 1 \), \( \varepsilon = 1 \)."

2. `## Integer powers`. **`def-integer-power`** `[Integer Powers]`: "Let \( F \) be a field, \( a \in F \), \( a \ne 0 \), \( n \in \nN \). \( a^{-n} \coloneqq (a^{-1})^n \)."
