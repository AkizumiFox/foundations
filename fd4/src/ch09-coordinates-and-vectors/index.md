# Coordinates and Vectors in \( \nR^n \)

Chapter 3 built the complex plane as pairs of real numbers and gave it a length, the modulus, with a triangle inequality. Analysis needs the same for lists of any length. A point of \( \nR^n \) is its list of coordinates; it can be added to another and stretched by a real number; one more operation, the dot product, takes two points to a number; and from the dot product come the length, the distance, the right angle, and the one inequality, Cauchy–Schwarz, from which everything else follows. This chapter builds that structure from the real numbers and proves every rule. It uses no linear algebra and no limits, and it says nothing about angles until the last section, where the cosine of the angle between two vectors is defined as a quotient.

Calculus on \( \nR^n \) is built on this: a ball is the set of points at distance less than \( r \) from a center, convergence of vectors is convergence of distances, and the triangle inequality of Section 9.5 is the engine of every estimate. The linear algebra volumes take the same operations as axioms and prove the same rules for an abstract vector space.

## Roadmap

- **Points and Coordinates.** The space \( \nR^n \) of \( n \)-tuples of reals, with equality coordinate by coordinate (@def-real-n-space); subsets of the plane described by conditions on coordinates; and boxes (@def-box-rn).
- **Adding Points and Scaling Them.** Addition and scaling coordinate by coordinate (@def-vector-operations-rn), proved by the method "work coordinatewise" to obey the laws of a vector space (@prp-vector-space-laws-rn); the standard vectors and the expansion of a vector in them (@def-standard-basis-rn, @prp-standard-basis-expansion); parallel vectors; midpoints and parallelograms.
- **The Dot Product.** The dot product \( \inner{x}{y} = x_1 y_1 + \dots + x_n y_n \) (@def-dot-product-rn) and its laws (@prp-dot-product-laws-rn); orthogonal vectors and a test for orthogonality (@def-orthogonal-rn, @prp-orthogonal-test-rn); and the set of points orthogonal to a given vector as one linear equation.
- **Length and Distance.** The norm and the distance (@def-norm-rn, @def-distance-rn) with their basic properties (@prp-norm-basic-rn, @prp-distance-basic-rn); \( \nR^2 \) as the complex plane, with the norm equal to the modulus (@prp-plane-is-complex-plane); Pythagoras (@prp-pythagoras-rn) and the move "expand the square"; the parallelogram law and polarization (@prp-parallelogram-law-rn, @prp-polarization-rn); and the comparison of the norm with the coordinates, which shows that a ball sits between two boxes (@prp-norm-coordinate-bounds, @cor-balls-and-boxes-rn).
- **Cauchy–Schwarz and the Triangle Inequality.** The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with its equality case (@thm-cauchy-schwarz-rn), proved by the method "normalize", and its form for sums of real numbers (@cor-cauchy-schwarz-sums); the triangle inequality for vectors and its equality case (@thm-triangle-inequality-rn) and for finite sums (@cor-triangle-finite-sums-rn); and the bound of the length by the sum of the coordinates (@cor-norm-sum-bound).
- **Projection onto a Line and the Cosine of an Angle.** The projection of \( x \) onto \( y \) (@def-projection-rn), the method "subtract the projection", and the theorem that the projection is the closest point of the line (@thm-projection-closest-point); the distance from a point to a line through the origin; the cosine of the angle between two vectors, which lies between \( -1 \) and \( 1 \) (@def-angle-cosine-rn, @prp-angle-cosine-properties); cosines computed; and four review exercises, on a monotone function, a homomorphism of groups, the plane read as \( \nC \), and a pair of sums.

## Named moves

- **Work coordinatewise** (@exm-method-coordinatewise). To prove an equation between vectors, fix a coordinate \( k \), compute the \( k \)-th coordinate of each side, prove the equation in \( \nR \) naming the law used, and conclude that the vectors agree in every place. Named in Section 9.2.
- **Expand the square** (@exm-method-expand-the-square). Lengths are roots of sums, and their squares are dot products: compare squares, write \( \norm{v}^2 = \inner{v}{v} \), expand by linearity and symmetry, and return to lengths by comparing squares. Named in Section 9.4; it proves the Pythagorean theorem and the parallelogram law.
- **Normalize** (@exm-method-normalize). If a statement is homogeneous in \( x \) and in \( y \), settle \( x = 0 \) and \( y = 0 \) separately and prove it for the unit vectors \( x/\norm{x} \) and \( y/\norm{y} \). Named in Section 9.5, where it proves Cauchy–Schwarz.
- **Subtract the projection** (@exm-method-subtract-the-projection). Given \( x \) and \( y \ne 0 \), split \( x \) into its projection onto \( y \) and the remainder, which is orthogonal to \( y \). Named in Section 9.6, where it proves the closest-point theorem.

## Threads

- **Same statement, three places.** The triangle inequality for the plane is proved in Section 3.3 for \( \nC \) and again in Section 9.5 for every \( \nR^n \), by a different route. Cauchy–Schwarz is stated here only for \( \nR^n \) and for finite sums, and the linear algebra volumes prove it for abstract inner product spaces.
- **Chapter 10 on distances.** Hyperplanes \( \inner{a}{x} = c \), nearest points on lines and hyperplanes, and betweenness stated by distances all come from the dot product and the projection of this chapter.
- **The angle.** Section 9.6 defines only the cosine of the angle between two vectors. An angle \( \theta \) with that cosine needs the function \( \cos \): for \( n = 2 \) Chapter 3 supplies it through polar form, and for general \( n \) the analysis volumes do.

## Summary

\( \nR^n \) is the set of lists of \( n \) reals, with addition and scaling coordinate by coordinate obeying the laws of a vector space (@def-real-n-space, @prp-vector-space-laws-rn). The dot product is symmetric, linear in each place and positive on non-zero vectors (@prp-dot-product-laws-rn). The norm \( \norm{x} = \sqrt{\inner{x}{x}} \) and the distance \( \norm{x - y} \) obey Pythagoras, the parallelogram law and polarization (@prp-pythagoras-rn, @prp-parallelogram-law-rn, @prp-polarization-rn). Cauchy–Schwarz, \( \abs{\inner{x}{y}} \le \norm{x}\norm{y} \), holds with equality exactly for parallel vectors (@thm-cauchy-schwarz-rn), and it gives the triangle inequality (@thm-triangle-inequality-rn). The projection of \( x \) onto \( y \) is the closest point of the line \( \nR y \) (@thm-projection-closest-point), and the quotient \( \inner{x}{y}/(\norm{x}\norm{y}) \) lies in \( [-1, 1] \) (@prp-angle-cosine-properties).

What is open: the angle itself, which needs the function \( \cos \); lines, hyperplanes and convex sets, which Chapter 10 builds from this chapter; and distance and convergence for abstract spaces, which the analysis volumes take up.

## Terms

- **box**: the product \( [a_1, b_1] \times \dots \times [a_n, b_n] \) of closed intervals (@def-box-rn).
- **cosine of the angle**: \( \inner{x}{y}/(\norm{x}\norm{y}) \) for non-zero \( x \), \( y \) (@def-angle-cosine-rn).
- **distance**: \( \norm{x - y} \) (@def-distance-rn).
- **dot product**: \( \inner{x}{y} = \sum_k x_k y_k \) (@def-dot-product-rn).
- **norm**, **length**: \( \norm{x} = \sqrt{\inner{x}{x}} \) (@def-norm-rn).
- **orthogonal vectors**: vectors with dot product \( 0 \) (@def-orthogonal-rn).
- **parallel vectors**: vectors one of which is a real multiple of the other (@def-parallel-rn).
- **projection onto a line**: \( \operatorname{proj}_y x = \frac{\inner{x}{y}}{\norm{y}^2}\, y \) (@def-projection-rn).
- **standard vectors**: the vectors \( e_1, \dots, e_n \) with a single coordinate \( 1 \) (@def-standard-basis-rn).
- **unit vector**: a vector of norm \( 1 \) (@def-unit-vector-rn).
