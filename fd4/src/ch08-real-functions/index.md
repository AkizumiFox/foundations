# Real Functions and Their Graphs

Analysis studies functions of a real variable, and it studies them with a short list of questions. Where does the function increase? What is the largest value it takes on a set? Does it have an inverse? How does it behave under the floor, which makes it jump? This chapter fixes the objects and answers every such question that does not need a limit. A real function is a rule with a domain and a graph; functions are added, multiplied and composed; a monotone function is injective and has a monotone inverse; the values of a function on a set have a supremum, and there are rules for comparing suprema; the integer part becomes a function that jumps; and the exponential, the logarithm and real powers are built from three borrowed facts. The last section collects the functions on which later definitions of continuity and integral will be tested, proves what the tools of the chapter allow, and leaves the rest as questions.

Part III begins here. The three chapters of Part III (8, 9 and 10) are the tools that *Analysis on the Real Line* assumes on its first page. They use the algebra of Parts I and II sparingly, mostly for polynomials, as the list below shows.

## Roadmap

- **Real Functions and Their Graphs.** A real function with its domain and its graph (@def-real-function), and the vertical line test (@prp-graph-vertical-line); sums, products, quotients and compositions (@def-function-algebra); polynomial and rational functions; how the graph moves when the formula changes (@prp-graph-transformations); and even and odd functions, with the splitting of every function on a symmetric domain into an even and an odd part (@prp-even-odd-decomposition).
- **Monotone Functions and Inverse Functions.** Increasing, decreasing and strictly monotone functions (@def-monotone-function), proved by factoring out \( y - x \); rules for building them (@prp-monotone-algebra); strictly monotone functions reflect the order (@prp-strictly-monotone-order-reflecting); the inverse of a strictly monotone function exists and is monotone in the same direction (@thm-monotone-inverse); roots as inverses of powers, with odd roots defined for every real number (@exm-odd-roots); and a strictly increasing function that jumps.
- **Bounded Functions and Their Suprema.** The infimum by reflection and the epsilon test for a supremum (@prp-real-infimum, @prp-real-sup-approximation); bounded functions, and the supremum and infimum of a function on a set (@def-bounded-function, @def-sup-of-function); the method "bound every value, then find a witness"; rules for comparing suprema (@thm-sup-function-rules); the spread of values (@prp-sup-oscillation); polynomials of positive degree are unbounded on \( \nR \) (@exm-polynomial-unbounded); and iterated suprema (@prp-iterated-sup).
- **Floor, Distance to the Integers, and Step Functions.** The floor, ceiling and fractional part (@def-floor-ceiling) with their rules (@prp-floor-rules); the distance to the nearest integer (@def-distance-to-integer, @prp-distance-to-integer); periodic functions; and step functions, closed under the operations of Section 8.1 (@def-step-function, @prp-step-function-algebra).
- **The Exponential, the Logarithm, and Real Powers.** The box of three borrowed facts; the exponential is determined by two of them (@thm-exp-unique); Euler's number and the logarithm with its rules (@def-euler-number, @def-logarithm, @prp-log-rules); real powers \( a^x \), consistent with the roots and integer powers of Chapter 1 (@def-real-power, @prp-real-power-consistent) and with the laws of powers (@prp-real-power-laws); and the exponential beats every power (@prp-exp-beats-powers).
- **A Table of Strange Functions.** Irrationals are dense (@lem-irrationals-dense); the Dirichlet function and Thomae's function (@def-dirichlet-function, @def-thomae-function); an involution that is monotone nowhere, a staircase along the diagonal, a zigzag that never settles, an unbounded function on a closed interval, and a supremum never reached; and a table with a question for each function that later volumes answer.

## Named moves

- **Read a graph as a set of pairs** (@exm-method-graph-pairs). To move a graph by a bijection of the plane, fix a point \( (x, y) \) and unwind "\( (x, y) \in \operatorname{graph} g \)" into "\( x \in D_g \) and \( y = g(x) \)". Named in Section 8.1.
- **Factor out \( y - x \)** (@exm-method-factor-difference). To show that \( f \) is strictly increasing, write \( f(y) - f(x) = (y - x)\,Q(x, y) \) and show \( Q > 0 \) for \( x < y \). Named in Section 8.2.
- **Bound every value, then find a witness** (@exm-method-sup-two-halves). To prove \( \sup_A f = M \), show \( f(x) \le M \) for every \( x \in A \), then for each \( \varepsilon > 0 \) produce \( x \in A \) with \( f(x) > M - \varepsilon \). Named in Section 8.3.
- **Pin the floor by its inequality** (@exm-method-floor-inequality). To show \( \floor{A} = m \), check that \( m \) is an integer with \( m \le A < m + 1 \), or that \( j \le A \iff j \le m \) for every integer \( j \). Named in Section 8.4.
- **Squeeze, then close the gap by Archimedes** (@exm-method-archimedes-squeeze). To prove \( \sup S = L \), bound the set by \( L \) and find elements within \( C/n \) of it, with \( C \) fixed before \( n \) is chosen. Named in Section 8.5, where it proves the two-sided bound for the exponential.

## Threads

- **Order and inverses.** The inverse of a strictly monotone function gives roots as functions in Section 8.2. *Analysis on the Real Line* proves that a continuous injective function on an interval is strictly monotone, and Section 8.2 shows why both hypotheses are needed.
- **Suprema of functions.** The rules of Section 8.3 are used again for the spread of values, which *Analysis on the Real Line* calls the oscillation and measures integrability with.
- **Functions that resist drawing.** The functions of Section 8.6, together with the distance to the integers of Section 8.4, are where continuity, differentiability and the integral are tested in the analysis volumes: a function continuous everywhere and differentiable nowhere is built from tents of the form \( \dist(2^k x, \nZ)/2^k \), and the zigzag of Section 8.6 is replaced by the sine once that exists.
- **The exponential.** The exponential, borrowed in Section 8.5, is constructed in *Analysis on the Real Line*, and the sine and cosine borrowed in Section 3.5 are constructed there as well.

## Summary

A real function is a set of pairs with one value over each input, and the operations on functions are operations on values (@def-real-function, @def-function-algebra). A strictly monotone function is injective, reflects the order, and has a strictly monotone inverse (@thm-monotone-inverse). The values of a function on a set have a supremum and an infimum when they are bounded, found by bounding every value and then producing a witness, and the rules of @thm-sup-function-rules compare them. The floor makes the integer part a function (@def-floor-ceiling), and step functions are built from it. From three facts about the exponential, the exponential is unique (@thm-exp-unique), the logarithm is its inverse, and \( a^x \) is defined for every positive \( a \) and real \( x \), agreeing with the roots of Chapter 1 and obeying the laws of powers (@prp-real-power-consistent, @prp-real-power-laws).

What is open: continuity, limits of functions and the integral, which *Analysis on the Real Line* defines and tests on the functions of Section 8.6, and the three borrowed facts about the exponential.

## Terms

- **bounded function**: a function whose values on a set \( A \) satisfy \( \abs{f(x)} \le M \) for one \( M \) (@def-bounded-function).
- **ceiling**, **floor**, **fractional part**: \( \ceil{x} \), \( \floor{x} \) and \( x - \floor{x} \) (@def-floor-ceiling).
- **Dirichlet function**, **Thomae's function**: the indicator of the rationals, and the function that is \( 1/q \) at \( p/q \) in lowest terms and \( 0 \) at irrational numbers (@def-dirichlet-function, @def-thomae-function).
- **even function**, **odd function**: \( f(-x) = f(x) \) and \( f(-x) = -f(x) \) (@def-even-odd-function).
- **graph**: the set \( \{(x, f(x)) : x \in D\} \) (@def-real-function).
- **logarithm**: the inverse of the exponential, \( \log \colon (0, \infty) \to \nR \) (@def-logarithm).
- **monotone function**: an increasing or decreasing function, strictly or not (@def-monotone-function).
- **periodic function**: a function that has a period \( p \ne 0 \), with \( f(x + p) = f(x) \) for every \( x \) of its domain (@def-periodic-function).
- **real power**: \( a^x \) for \( a > 0 \) and \( x \in \nR \), defined through the exponential (@def-real-power).
- **step function**: a function on \( [a, b] \) that is constant on each open piece of a partition \( a = x_0 < \dots < x_n = b \) (@def-step-function).
- **supremum of a function**: the supremum of its values on a set, \( \sup_A f \) (@def-sup-of-function).
