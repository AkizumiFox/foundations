# Chapter 1 — S2a statement referee report

**Blueprint:** `authoring/blueprints/ch01.md` (as of 2026-09-28, before this review's edits; the pre-edit copy was diffed against `check_blueprint.py` output, see §4).
**Referee brief:** PIPELINE S2a, plus: derive every sequent claimed derivable; justify every "needs RAA"; check exercise answers, marking schemes, number facts, the search method, the rule-system decisions, pedagogy order, and overlap with Huth & Ryan §1.1–1.2 (examples and exercises, not ideas).

**Method.**
- **Derivations.** Every sequent the blueprint calls derivable (and every replacement proposed below) was written as a fitch-book `nd` block and checked with `tools/check_fitch.py`. The file is `authoring/reviews/ch01-S2-derivations.md`: 76 blocks, 0 errors. Three blocks are marked `% expect-invalid` (the scope errors of the find-the-error items) and are correctly rejected. Derived-rule lines (LEM, DS, CP, ¬¬E) are `\by{…}` and their shape is not machine-checked; every such rule is itself derived from primitives in the same file. References below of the form "D27" point to that file.
- **"Needs RAA" claims.** Each one was checked by a brute-force search for Kripke counter-models (rooted frames with up to 4 worlds, all monotone valuations) and a truth table for classical validity. A sequent that is classically valid but has a Kripke counter-model is not derivable from the primitive rules without RAA: those rules are exactly intuitionistic natural deduction, which is sound for Kripke semantics. Every "no RAA" claim is backed by an RAA-free derivation in the D-file.
- **Numbers.** \(3 \mid n^2 \Rightarrow 3 \mid n\) was checked for \(|n| < 1000\). The counterexamples to \(4 \mid n^2 \Rightarrow 4 \mid n\) are \(n = 2, 6, 10, \dots\), so the witness \(n = 2\) is correct. The √3 and √6 routes were checked by hand; the √6 route needs only parity (as in the pilot). No \(2^{11} - 1\) fact appears in this chapter.
- **Huth & Ryan.** Every sequent in the blueprint was compared with H&R §1.2's examples (1.5–1.24), its list of provable equivalences (§1.2.4), and Exercises 1.2 (items 1–9).
