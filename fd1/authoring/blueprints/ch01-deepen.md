# Deepening blueprint: F1.1 Connectives and Derivations (fd1 Chapter 1), pilot D (stage S1)

**What this is.** A **delta** blueprint (stage S1) that deepens the delivered, reviewed Chapter 1 of *Logic and Proof* inside its fixed arrangement. The author likes the arrangement of the R2 chapter and finds its content and teaching too thin; this file says exactly what is added, section by section, and nothing else. It is complete on its own: a drafter executes it with this file, the current `fd1/src/ch01-connectives-and-derivations/`, `ch01-revision.md` (with its S3/S7 deviation notes) for everything this file does not change, `authoring/STYLE.md` (§12, and §12.8 and §12.13 as amended by D1), `DRAFTING.md` (including `\purpose`, `\taut` and the Diagrams templates) and `NOTATION.md`. Line numbers are those of the delivered files (last changed at commit `a0f50d8`; repository HEAD `3d73051`), written `01:263` for `01-statements-and-connectives.md` line 263, and so on.

**Inputs.** The deepening plan (`plan.md`, §3.0 volume-wide devices and §3.1 Chapter 1, as corrected by its *Critic review*), the final whole-book layout (`layout-final.md`, Layout B with grafts: eight chapters in three Parts), and the gap matrix (`gap-matrix.md`), which names the books whose **technique** each addition borrows. Every example, drill, exercise, figure and answer below is the book's own, in mathematical dress, school mathematics first. No text, example, exercise, sequent list or figure is taken from any of the twelve books or closely paraphrased (plan, binding rule 3).

**Binding rules for this deepening.**
1. **No label is lost, renamed or moved.** All 89 labels now in the chapter keep their names, their section and the content of every cited block (§3). Eighteen labels are new (§3.2). An uncited `exr-` item may change content only where §3.1 says so.
2. **The arrangement is fixed.** The index and the six sections keep their files, titles, order, headings and one new thing each. Additions go **inside** the existing subsections, or into new `##` subsections at the places named; no existing heading is renamed (heading ids may be cited).
3. **Reviewed content stays.** Statements and proofs of cited `def-`, `prp-` and `exm-` blocks are verbatim, except the wording changes listed for S2a (§9). Moving a passage verbatim into a second-reading fold is not a change.
4. **The new numbering.** Every pointer from Chapter 1 to a later chapter is written in the final layout's numbering: Chapter 2 negation and classical rules, Chapter 3 finding derivations, Chapter 4 truth, soundness and completeness, Chapter 5 the quantifier rules, Chapter 6 working with quantifiers, Chapter 7 models and theories, Chapter 8 prose (§6). The D2 sweep does not touch ch01 files while the pilot is in flight (layout §1.3).
5. **Do not pre-empt the later chapters.** §7 lists what Chapters 2–8 hold. Chapter 1 never uses the words *consistent*, *inconsistent*, *satisfiable*, *row*, *valid* (outside the one existing fold sentence) or *sound* (outside one new fold sentence) in their logical senses, never writes ⊨, draws no truth table and no search tree, and never asserts that something is **not derivable** except as a forward claim in a Looking-ahead box (layout G10).
6. **Derive-or-break lists hold only settled claims.** Chapter 1's rules are the positive fragment without ⊥: some classically valid statements built from ∧, →, ∨ (for instance \( ((P \to Q) \to P) \to P \), \( (P \to Q) \vee (Q \to P) \), and \( P \to (Q \vee R) \vdash (P \to Q) \vee (P \to R) \)) can be neither derived with these rules nor broken. In 1.5, where \( \rulename{\vee E} \) is not yet available, a list holds only claims derivable with \( \rulename{\wedge I} \), \( \rulename{\wedge E} \), \( \rulename{{\to}E} \), \( \rulename{{\to}I} \), \( \rulename{R} \), \( \rulename{\vee I} \), or breakable.    1. "Let \( x \) be a real number. 'If \( x > 3 \), then \( x^2 > 9 \)' is false at \( x = 1 \), since \( x > 3 \) fails there." — *Answer:* No. An implication is broken only when its hypothesis holds and its conclusion fails; at \( x = 1 \) it promises nothing (Section 1.1, "When is an implication broken?").
   2. "Let \( n \) be an integer. '\( n \) is divisible by \( 4 \) only if \( n \) is even' says that every even number is divisible by \( 4 \)." — *Answer:* No. "\( P \) only if \( Q \)" is \( P \to Q \): divisible by \( 4 \) implies even, which is true; the reading in the question is the converse, false at \( n = 2 \) (Section 1.1).
   3. "In a proof that begins 'Suppose \( x > 2 \)' and derives \( x^2 > 4 \), the line \( x^2 > 4 \) may be used anywhere later in the proof." — *Answer:* No. It holds only inside the box the supposition opened; outside it, only "if \( x > 2 \), then \( x^2 > 4 \)" may be used, and \( x^2 > 4 \) fails at \( x = 0 \) (Section 1.4).
   4. "'\( 5 \ge 0 \)' is false, because \( 5 \ne 0 \)." — *Answer:* No. \( 5 \ge 0 \) is \( (5 > 0) \vee (5 = 0) \), and an inclusive "or" needs one true side (Section 1.5).

The form "from \( P \vee Q \) and \( P \), conclude not \( Q \)" is called affirming a disjunct, and it is bad (Sections 1.1 and 1.6).
