## 1. The volume-wide devices, as applied to Chapter 4

Chapter 4 has **no rule sections**. So the devices that STYLE scopes to rule sections (rule card, rule-instance gallery, snapshots, Givens/Goal table) do not apply, as R3 §1 already said. The devices that do apply, and where:

D1. **Purpose-annotated assumptions** (§12.13.1). Every derivation of the chapter prints "asm, for →I" and so on, inferred by the engine. Chapter 4 says one sentence about it, in 4.7's two-level box: the annotation is part of the object (the derivation), and the soundness proof reads it as the rule whose case applies. The one hand-written `\purpose` is in 4.7 B4's scaffold.

D2. **No galleries.** There is no new rule to instantiate. The one rule-like device the chapter introduces, TAUT (4.8), gets a **"Not allowed" block** (D3), because its misuse is the error a reader will make.

D3. **"Not allowed" block** (§12.13.3). One, `[Not allowed: TAUT]`, in 4.8 (four wrong steps, each tagged with the clause of `def-rule-taut` it breaks). The WRONG-beside-RIGHT format is used in 4.7's **rule-by-rule meaning table** (layout G7): for →I, ∨E, ¬I and RAA the WRONG variant of the rule is shown with the derivation it would license and the refuting row that convicts it.

D4. **The counterexample set** (§12.13.4, NOTATION). Named in 1.1 (`def-counterexample-set`), made syntactic in 3.2 (Γ, ¬φ inconsistent), and here made semantic and then searched: 4.5 states that Γ ⊨ ψ exactly when the counterexample set Γ, ¬ψ is unsatisfiable (05:80–84, kept, now named by the term), 4.6 searches it systematically, 4.7 certifies every earlier break (the **retroactive paragraph**, G5), and 4.8 closes the circle (consistent ⇔ satisfiable). Every derive-or-refute solution of the chapter says "the counterexample set … is made true by the row …".

D5. **Derive-or-refute items** (§12.13.4). From 4.7 on, the means of breaking is "a refuting row, which proves non-derivability by @cor-refuting-row". Items with the verdict withheld: 4.6 B1 (valid or refuted, by search; semantic only), 4.7 B3 (derive or refute), 4.8 B5 (consistent or not). The STYLE §12.13.4 sentence still carries pre-layout numbering ("from Section 3.6"); in the final layout it reads "a refuting row once Section 4.7 has proved soundness" (coordinator, §3.3).

D6. **Drill banks of 12** (§12.8; Chapter 4 is not Chapter 1 or 2). Every section 4.1–4.10 has an A2 bank of **12 parts** in a ramp from recognition to construction (non-rule sections use the ramp: recognize, compute one step, judge, then construct), with one folded solution answering every part in one or two lines. R3 planned 46 parts in six banks; this file keeps every one of them, splits R3's 3.2 bank between 4.2 and 4.3, and adds 74 parts. 4.9 and 4.10 get banks too (full track only).

D7. **Chapter close** (§12.13.5). `index.md` ends with `## Summary`, whose closing paragraph, under no special name, says what is proved and what is open (plan §3.0.8; Critic small 7: not "Interlude"), and `## Terms`. It gains the "Minimum for fd2" line (G14).

D8. **"Used later" marker** (§12.13.6). Paid: 1.4 C4 (`exr-proving-an-implication-c4`, ", used later") is recalled by label in 4.7's "The context of a line" (§4.7 item 3). Opened: none in this chapter.

D9. **Pictures where the structure is spatial** (DRAFTING, Diagrams). R3's five figures (the build-up figure 4.1; the check-mark figure and the depth profile 4.3; the picture for `lem-context-inclusion` (b) 4.7; the case-split trees 4.8) are kept. New: **rows as regions** (4.5, plain TikZ circles in a rectangle, no fills); **two search trees** and **one satisfiability tree** (4.6, `tree` template); a **flowchart** of the search (4.6, `flowchart` template, its own text, not DRAFTING's example); **a polarity tree** (4.9, the parse tree of one formula with each node marked + or −, plain `tikzpicture` with `level distance` as in 01:193). Each figure's node texts are given in the section plans; S3 writes the `tikzpicture` in full in a raw LaTeX block, no colours, no white fills.

D10. **The induction checklist** (plan §3.3, 3.2; layout 4.2). A boxed checklist introduced in 4.2 and **reused** in 4.3 (structural induction), 4.7 (induction on the lines of a derivation, in the two-level box) and 7.3 (seam, §6).

D11. **Not adopted, with reasons.** (a) Plan §3.0.9's inline "(Why?)" prompt per worked example: STYLE §12.13 does not carry it, and the chapter's Quick-check density meets §12.7 (as in `ch01-deepen.md` D11). (b) The plan's Looking-ahead box on 2ⁿ growth in 4.8: the existing warning 08:513–515 already makes the scale concrete, and the box is spent on TAUT's forward pointer (§4.8 item 10). (c) The plan's "finish the proof: two cases of the soundness proof left to the reader" [Si]: the frozen exit check 4.7 C2 already asks for three cases written from memory; a second item would duplicate it. (d) The plan's `prp-reduction-to-unsatisfiability`: the layout lists no such label, and the reductions already stand unlabelled at 05:38–42 and 05:80–84; 4.5 completes them in a table (§4.5 item 8), without a label.

### 4.6 Section 4.6: Hunting for a Refuting Row (`06-refuting-row-search.md`, new)

**Purpose.** Turn "working backwards from F" into a method that never stalls: search the counterexample set systematically, record the search as a tree, and prove that the search decides every entailment and every question of satisfiability. One new thing: **the search tree**.

**Placement and seams.** Between 4.5 (which reduced every semantic question to unsatisfiability of a list) and 4.7 (soundness). It answers 3.2's withheld verdicts **semantically** only (G10: no "not derivable" here); 4.7 turns its refuting rows into non-derivability and names the move "test the subgoal" (G9); 4.8's C5 turns its closed trees into derivations (C2). `exm-backwards-from-f` (4.4) is the forced case and is cited, not repeated. The twin in Chapter 7 is 7.2's counter-model search (layout §1.2).

**Budget:** main line ≤ 4,600 (expected ~4,500); folds ≤ 900 (F4.6-a ~650, F4.6-b ~150). One Looking-ahead box, in-chapter (item 8).

**New order of material.**
1. **Opening frame** (~170 words): Section 4.5 reduced every question about rows to one, whether a list is unsatisfiable. A table answers it by brute force, \( 2^n \) rows for \( n \) atoms. Working backwards from F (@exm-backwards-from-f) is faster: assume the conclusion false and follow what is forced; but it stalls as soon as a value leaves a choice, as a false conjunction or a true disjunction does. This section turns working backwards into a method that never stalls: forced steps first, and when nothing is forced, split into cases and follow every case. On paper the method is a tree. A branch that closes is a case with no row; a branch that stays open hands us a row. The answers are about truth, "valid" or "here is a refuting row"; what they say about derivations is the next section's business.
2. **`## The rules of the search`** (~650 words) [technique: Sm, Ho, LB — the tree rules; readers derive them from the tables first (Ho)].
   - Setup (~120 words): to decide \( \Gamma \models \psi \), look for one row making every formula of the counterexample set \( \Gamma, \neg\psi \) true (05:80); to decide whether a list \( \Delta \) is satisfiable, look for one row making all of \( \Delta \) true. Requirements are kept as formulas: "\( \varphi \) must be true" is written \( \varphi \), and "\( \varphi \) must be false" is written \( \neg\varphi \).
   - **Quick check (new, before the table; the reader derives the rules):** "From the tables of (V3)–(V5): in how many ways can \( \varphi \wedge \psi \) be true? be false? In how many ways can \( \varphi \to \psi \) be false? be true?" *Solution:* \( \varphi \wedge \psi \) is true in one way (both true) and false in two (\( \varphi \) false, or \( \psi \) false; the row with both false is covered by either). \( \varphi \to \psi \) is false in one way (\( \varphi \) true, \( \psi \) false) and true in two (\( \varphi \) false, or \( \psi \) true).
   - **The table of search rules** (a pipe table; columns *On the branch* | *Its requirement, read off* | *Adds*):
     one way: \( \varphi \wedge \psi \) | (V3) | \( \varphi \), \( \psi \); \( \neg(\varphi \vee \psi) \) | (V4) | \( \neg\varphi \), \( \neg\psi \); \( \neg(\varphi \to \psi) \) | (V5) | \( \varphi \), \( \neg\psi \); \( \neg\neg\varphi \) | (V2) twice | \( \varphi \).
     two ways: \( \varphi \vee \psi \) | (V4) | \( \varphi \) **or** \( \psi \); \( \neg(\varphi \wedge \psi) \) | (V3) | \( \neg\varphi \) or \( \neg\psi \); \( \varphi \to \psi \) | (V5) | \( \neg\varphi \) or \( \psi \); \( \varphi \leftrightarrow \psi \) | (V6) | \( \varphi, \psi \) or \( \neg\varphi, \neg\psi \); \( \neg(\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi) \) | (V6) | \( \varphi, \neg\psi \) or \( \neg\varphi, \psi \).
     Then three sentences: an atom, a negated atom, and \( \neg\bot \) (true in every row) need nothing; a branch holding some formula and its negation, or holding \( \bot \), describes no row and is **closed**; the one-way rules are exactly the forced steps of working backwards, and the two-way rules are the places where it stalled.
   - One sentence on overlap: the two ways of a split need not exclude each other (a row may make both \( \varphi \) and \( \psi \) true); they need only cover every row that makes the formula true.
3. **`## The search, drawn as a tree`** (~900 words with the two examples).
   - **`def-search-tree`** `[Search Tree]`: "Let \( \Delta \) be a finite list of formulas. A **search tree** for \( \Delta \) is built in stages. It begins as one node, the **root**, carrying the formulas of \( \Delta \). At each stage, choose an open branch and a formula \( \chi \) on it that is not an atom, a negated atom, \( \bot \) or \( \neg\bot \), and that is not yet **used** on that branch; then, below the last node of **every** open branch through the node carrying \( \chi \), add \( \chi \)'s rule: one new node with the formulas of its one way, or two new nodes side by side with the formulas of its two ways. On each of those branches, \( \chi \) (and every formula equal to it) is now used. A **branch** is a path from the root down to a node with nothing below it, and its formulas are those on its nodes. A branch is **closed** if it holds some formula together with its negation, or holds \( \bot \); otherwise it is **open**. An open branch is **finished** if every formula on it, other than an atom, a negated atom or \( \neg\bot \), is used on it."
   - In words (~60): a search tree is a record of a backwards computation with every choice followed; a closed branch is a case with no row, a finished open branch a case that has survived every requirement. Drawing conventions (the `tree` template): a used formula is ticked (\( \checkmark \)), a closed branch ends in ×, an open finished branch in ○.
   - **`exm-refuting-row-search-closed`** `[A tree that closes]`: "Decide whether \( p \to (q \vee r), \ q \to s, \ r \to s \models p \to s \)." *Solution.* The root carries the counterexample set: the three premises and \( \neg(p \to s) \). Stage 1, the one-way rule for \( \neg(p \to s) \): add \( p \), \( \neg s \). Stage 2, \( p \to (q \vee r) \): \( \neg p \) or \( q \vee r \); the branch \( \neg p \) closes against \( p \). Stage 3, \( q \vee r \): \( q \) or \( r \). Stage 4, on the branch \( q \): \( q \to s \) gives \( \neg q \) (closes against \( q \)) or \( s \) (closes against \( \neg s \)); on the branch \( r \): \( r \to s \) gives \( \neg r \) or \( s \), both closed. Every branch is closed: no row makes the counterexample set true, so the entailment holds. Figure (the `tree` template; S3 writes it in full and sets sibling distances):

       \node {$p \to (q \vee r)$ \ticked \\ $q \to s$ \ticked \\ $r \to s$ \ticked \\ $\neg(p \to s)$ \ticked \\ $p$ \\ $\neg s$}

     (The tick on \( q \to s \) and \( r \to s \) means "used on the branches where it was used"; the caption says so: "A tick marks a formula used on every open branch below it; \( r \to s \) was used only below \( r \), \( q \to s \) only below \( q \).") *Aftermath:* five closed branches replace a table of \( 2^4 = 16 \) rows; read as a proof by cases, the tree says: \( p \) is forced, so \( q \vee r \); in the case \( q \), \( s \) follows, against \( \neg s \); in the case \( r \), likewise.
   - **`exm-refuting-row-search-open`** `[A branch that stays open]`: "Decide whether \( p \to (q \vee r), \ q \to s \models p \to s \)." *Solution.* The same first three stages. On the branch \( q \), \( q \to s \) closes both ways, as before. On the branch \( r \), \( q \to s \) gives \( \neg q \) or \( s \); the side \( s \) closes, the side \( \neg q \) does not. That branch carries \( p \to (q \vee r) \) (used), \( q \to s \) (used), \( \neg(p \to s) \) (used), \( p \), \( \neg s \), \( q \vee r \) (used), \( r \), \( \neg q \): it is open and finished. Its row: the atoms on it, \( p \) and \( r \), true; the negated atoms, \( q \) and \( s \), false. Check against the root: \( p \to (q \vee r) \) is \( \mathrm{T} \to \mathrm{T} \), true; \( q \to s \) has a false hypothesis, true; \( p \to s \) is \( \mathrm{T} \to \mathrm{F} \), false. So this row refutes the claim. Figure: as above without \( r \to s \) at the root, the \( r \) node's children \( \neg q \) \openbranch and \( s \) \closedbranch. *Aftermath:* the premise \( r \to s \) of the first example is exactly what closed this branch; and the search may stop at the first finished open branch, since one refuting row settles the question.

4. **`## What a closed tree proves`** (~600 words with the statement).
   - Two observations, in prose (~200 words): *each rule passes a row on*: if a row makes every formula on a branch true and a rule is applied there, the row makes true all the formulas of at least one new node (each rule was read off its table); so a row making the root true runs down some branch to its end with every formula true there. A closed branch holds \( \varphi \) and \( \neg\varphi \), or \( \bot \), which no row makes true. *An open finished branch hands over a row*: make its atoms true and all other atoms false; every formula on the branch is then true, from the shortest up.
   - **`prp-search-decides`** `[The Search Decides]`: "Let \( \Delta \) be a finite list of formulas. (a) Every search tree for \( \Delta \) can be continued until each of its branches is closed or finished, and it has only finitely many stages. (b) If every branch of a search tree for \( \Delta \) is closed, then \( \Delta \) is unsatisfiable. (c) If a search tree for \( \Delta \) has an open finished branch \( B \), then the valuation \( v_B \) that makes the atoms on \( B \) true and every other atom false makes every formula on \( B \) true, in particular every formula of \( \Delta \). So, for a list \( \Gamma \) and a formula \( \psi \): a search tree for \( \Gamma, \neg\psi \), continued as in (a), either closes, and then \( \Gamma \models \psi \), or has an open finished branch, whose row refutes \( \Gamma \models \psi \)."
   - In words (~50): the search always ends, a closed tree proves unsatisfiability, an open finished branch exhibits a satisfying row; and since (b) and (c) cannot both apply to finished trees of one list, every order of moves gives the same verdict.
   - *Idea* (~60): (a) every new formula is shorter than the one whose rule added it, and is a subformula of a root formula or the negation of one; (b) and (c) are the two observations, made exact by induction (on the stages for (b), on the length of a formula for (c)). One sentence: "The proof is in the fold below." [technique: Si — a failed search, with every choice followed, is itself a proof]
   - **Fold F4.6-a** `title="The proof: why the search decides"` (~650 words), a `.proof` inside the fold: (a) Let \( N \) be the number of formulas that are subformulas of formulas of \( \Delta \), or negations of such. Every formula ever added is one of these (each rule adds immediate parts of the used formula, or negations of them, or, for \( \neg\neg\varphi \), the formula \( \varphi \)), and each is used at most once on a branch, so a branch has at most \( N \) stages below the root; a stage adds at most two nodes below each branch, so the tree never has more than \( 2^N \) branches; hence only finitely many stages, and when no stage is possible every branch is closed or finished. (b) *Claim:* at every stage, if a valuation \( v \) makes every formula of \( \Delta \) true, some branch has all its formulas true under \( v \). At the start this is the root. If it holds before a stage and the stage applies \( \chi \)'s rule on branches through \( \chi \)'s node, a branch \( B \) all true under \( v \) either does not pass through that node (unchanged) or does, and then \( v \) makes \( \chi \) true, and by the clause of @def-truth-value from which the rule was read, \( v \) makes true every formula of one new node: one rule at a time (the nine rows of the table, each one line). By induction on the stages the claim holds for the finished tree; a closed branch holds \( \varphi \) and \( \neg\varphi \) or \( \bot \), which \( v \) cannot make all true; so if every branch is closed, no such \( v \) exists. (c) By @thm-strong-induction on the length of a formula \( \chi \) on \( B \): an atom is true by the definition of \( v_B \); \( \bot \) is not on the open branch \( B \); \( \neg\bot \) is true; a negated atom \( \neg p \): \( p \) is not on \( B \) (else \( B \) is closed), so \( v_B(p) = \mathrm{F} \) and \( \neg p \) is true; any other \( \chi \) is used on \( B \), so \( B \) passes through a node with the formulas of one of \( \chi \)'s ways, each shorter than \( \chi \) and true by the hypothesis, and by its clause \( \chi \) is true. The last sentence of the statement: apply (b) or (c) to \( \Delta = \Gamma, \neg\psi \), with 05:80.

5. **Fold F4.8-k** (new) `title="Why two definitions of 'follows' are enough"` (~200 words; after item 4) [technique: Co — Kreisel's squeezing argument]: before Chapter 1, "\( \psi \) follows from \( \Gamma \)" had only an informal meaning: whatever a careful mathematician accepts as a valid inference, by any means. If \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \), then \( \psi \) follows informally: each rule is an inference every mathematician accepts, and a chain of accepted inferences is accepted. ### 4.10 Section 4.10: Compactness and Maximal Consistent Sets (`10-compactness.md`, new, optional)

**Purpose.** Allow infinitely many premises, prove completeness for them by a second route (a maximal consistent collection and its row), and deduce compactness: a collection is satisfiable as soon as each finite part is.

**Placement and status.** Optional: nothing outside this section depends on it, and its results carry no `.optional` marker of their own, as in 7.3 (§2, "Files and numbering"). It may cite anything of Chapters 1–4. Its first paragraph says it is optional. Its twin is 7.6 (layout §1.2).

**Budget:** main line ≤ 4,500 (expected ~4,400); folds ≤ 900 (F4.10-a enumeration ~250, F4.10-b the properties of maximal collections ~450, F4.10-c a remark ~100). Looking ahead (item 8).

1. **Opening** (~200 words): "This section is optional, and nothing outside it depends on it." Then: every list of premises so far was finite, and the proof of completeness needed it (Step 1, 08:409: finitely many atoms). A statement about infinitely many objects (one atom for each natural number, say) can have infinitely many premises. This section defines \( \vdash \) and \( \models \) for such collections, proves completeness again by a different route, and deduces the compactness theorem. The word "collection" keeps its everyday sense; nothing here needs more.
2. **`## Infinitely many premises`** (~380 words).
   - **`def-infinite-entailment`** `[Derivability and Entailment from Infinitely Many Premises]` (Critic small 1): "Let \( \Gamma \) be a collection of formulas, finite or infinite, and \( \psi \) a formula. \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \) if \( \psi \) is derivable from some finite list of members of \( \Gamma \); \( \Gamma \) is **consistent** if \( \Gamma \nvdash \bot \). \( \Gamma \models \psi \) if every valuation that makes every member of \( \Gamma \) true makes \( \psi \) true; \( \Gamma \) is **satisfiable** if some valuation makes every member of \( \Gamma \) true."
   - In words (~120): a derivation has finitely many lines, so it can use only finitely many premises; that is why \( \vdash \) goes through finite lists, while \( \models \) looks at all of \( \Gamma \) at once. For a finite list the new definitions agree with the old: a derivation from part of a list is a derivation from the list (@lem-weakening-cut (a)), and the other three are word for word those of @def-valid-argument, @def-inconsistent-set and @def-satisfiable.
   - One example (~100): \( \Gamma = \{p_1\} \cup \{p_n \to p_{n+1} : n \ge 1\} \). \( \Gamma \vdash p_3 \), from the finite list \( p_1, \ p_1 \to p_2, \ p_2 \to p_3 \) by two \( \rulename{{\to}E} \) lines; and \( \Gamma \models p_n \) for every \( n \), since a valuation making \( \Gamma \) true makes every \( p_n \) true (by @ax-induction on \( n \)).
3. **`## Listing all formulas`** (~180 words main; proof folded).
   - **`lem-formula-enumeration`** `[The Formulas Can Be Listed]`: "There is a list \( \varphi_0, \varphi_1, \varphi_2, \dots \) of formulas in which every formula occurs."
   - In words; one sentence: the atoms are listed already (\( p, q, r, p_1, p_2, \dots \)), and each formula is finite. **Fold F4.10-a** `title="How to list every formula"` (~250 words): write the atoms in a list \( a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots \). For each \( n \ge 1 \), the strings of length at most \( n \) over the \( n + 8 \) symbols \( a_1, \dots, a_n, \bot, \neg, \wedge, \vee, \to, \leftrightarrow, (, ) \) are finitely many, so the formulas among them are finitely many. List those for \( n = 1 \), then those for \( n = 2 \) not listed before, and so on. A formula of length \( \ell \) whose atoms are among \( a_1, \dots, a_m \) is listed by stage \( \max\{\ell, m\} \).
4. **`## Lindenbaum's construction`** (~650 words).
   - *Maximal consistent* (in prose, bold, before the lemma): a collection \( \Delta \) is **maximal consistent** if it is consistent and, for every formula \( \varphi \), \( \varphi \) is in \( \Delta \) or \( \neg\varphi \) is.
   - The key step (in prose with its proof, ~120 words): if \( \Delta \) is consistent, then \( \Delta \) with \( \varphi \) added, or \( \Delta \) with \( \neg\varphi \) added, is consistent. For if both were inconsistent, there would be finite lists \( \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \) of members of \( \Delta \) with \( \Delta_1, \varphi \vdash \bot \) and \( \Delta_2, \neg\varphi \vdash \bot \); by weakening both hold with \( \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \); \( \rulename{\neg I} \) gives \( \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \vdash \neg\varphi \) and \( \rulename{RAA} \) gives \( \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \vdash \varphi \), and \( \rulename{\neg E} \) with chaining gives \( \Delta_1, \Delta_2 \vdash \bot \): \( \Delta \) would be inconsistent.
   - **`lem-lindenbaum`** `[Lindenbaum's Lemma]`: "Every consistent collection of formulas is contained in a maximal consistent collection."
   - *Idea* (~40): go through the list of all formulas, adding each one or its negation, whichever keeps the collection consistent.
   - *Proof* (main line, ~250 words): let \( \Gamma \) be consistent and \( \varphi_0, \varphi_1, \dots \) the list of @lem-formula-enumeration. Define \( \Delta_0 \coloneqq \Gamma \), and \( \Delta_{n+1} \coloneqq \Delta_n \) with \( \varphi_n \) added if that is consistent, and \( \Delta_n \) with \( \neg\varphi_n \) added otherwise (a definition by recursion on \( n \), each stage from the one before). By the key step and @ax-induction, every \( \Delta_n \) is consistent. Let \( \Delta \) consist of the formulas that belong to some \( \Delta_n \). \( \Delta \) contains \( \Gamma \). It is consistent: a derivation of \( \bot \) from \( \Delta \) uses finitely many members, each in some \( \Delta_n \), hence all in the largest of those \( \Delta_n \) (the \( \Delta_n \) grow), which would be inconsistent. It is maximal: every formula is some \( \varphi_n \), and \( \varphi_n \) or \( \neg\varphi_n \) is in \( \Delta_{n+1} \).
   - **Quick check (new):** "Start from \( \Gamma = \{p \vee q, \neg p\} \) with a list beginning \( p, q \). What are \( \Delta_1 \) and \( \Delta_2 \)?" *Solution:* \( \Gamma \) with \( p \) is inconsistent (\( p \), \( \neg p \)), so \( \Delta_1 \) adds \( \neg p \), already there. \( \Delta_1 \) with \( q \) is satisfiable (\( p = \mathrm{F} \), \( q = \mathrm{T} \)), hence consistent (Section 4.7), so \( \Delta_2 \) adds \( q \).
5. **`## A row from a maximal consistent collection`** (~520 words).
   - **`lem-maximal-consistent`** `[Maximal Consistent Collections]`: "Let \( \Delta \) be maximal consistent. (a) If \( \Delta \vdash \varphi \), then \( \varphi \in \Delta \). (b) For every \( \varphi \), exactly one of \( \varphi \), \( \neg\varphi \) is in \( \Delta \); and \( \bot \notin \Delta \). (c) \( \varphi \wedge \psi \in \Delta \) iff \( \varphi \in \Delta \) and \( \psi \in \Delta \); \( \varphi \vee \psi \in \Delta \) iff \( \varphi \in \Delta \) or \( \psi \in \Delta \); \( \varphi \to \psi \in \Delta \) iff \( \varphi \notin \Delta \) or \( \psi \in \Delta \); \( \varphi \leftrightarrow \psi \in \Delta \) iff \( \varphi \) and \( \psi \) are both in \( \Delta \) or both not in \( \Delta \)." In words (~40): a maximal consistent collection behaves like the set of formulas true in one row. *Proof* in **fold F4.10-b** `title="The proof: a maximal collection decides everything"` (~450 words): (a) if \( \varphi \notin \Delta \), then \( \neg\varphi \in \Delta \), and with \( \Delta \vdash \varphi \), \( \rulename{\neg E} \) gives \( \Delta \vdash \bot \). (b) Both in would give \( \bot \) by \( \rulename{\neg E} \); one is in by maximality; \( \bot \in \Delta \) would make \( \Delta \) inconsistent. (c) Each clause from (a), (b) and a derivable sequent: \( \wedge \) by \( \rulename{\wedge E} \), \( \rulename{\wedge I} \); \( \vee \): from right to left by \( \rulename{\vee I} \) and (a); from left to right, if neither \( \varphi \) nor \( \psi \) is in \( \Delta \), then \( \neg\varphi, \neg\psi \in \Delta \), and \( (\vee 3) \) of @lem-connective-rows gives \( \Delta \vdash \neg(\varphi \vee \psi) \), so \( \neg(\varphi \vee \psi) \in \Delta \), against (b). \( \to \): if \( \varphi \to \psi \) and \( \varphi \) are in \( \Delta \), then \( \psi \) by \( \rulename{{\to}E} \) and (a); if \( \varphi \notin \Delta \), then \( \neg\varphi \in \Delta \) and \( ({\to}1) \) gives \( \varphi \to \psi \); if \( \psi \in \Delta \), \( ({\to}2) \) does. \( \leftrightarrow \): likewise with \( ({\leftrightarrow}1) \)–\( ({\leftrightarrow}4) \) and \( \rulename{{\leftrightarrow}E} \).
   - **`lem-truth-lemma`** `[Truth Lemma]`: "Let \( \Delta \) be maximal consistent, and let \( v_\Delta \) be the valuation that makes an atom \( p \) true exactly when \( p \in \Delta \). Then for every formula \( \varphi \): \( v_\Delta(\varphi) = \mathrm{T} \) iff \( \varphi \in \Delta \)."
   - *Proof* (main line, ~170 words): by @thm-structural-induction. Atoms: the definition. \( \bot \): \( v_\Delta(\bot) = \mathrm{F} \) and \( \bot \notin \Delta \). \( \neg\varphi \): \( v_\Delta(\neg\varphi) = \mathrm{T} \) iff \( v_\Delta(\varphi) = \mathrm{F} \) (V2) iff \( \varphi \notin \Delta \) (hypothesis) iff \( \neg\varphi \in \Delta \) (@lem-maximal-consistent (b)). \( \varphi \circ \psi \): the clause of @def-truth-value for \( \circ \) and the clause of @lem-maximal-consistent (c) for \( \circ \) say the same thing, by the hypothesis for \( \varphi \) and \( \psi \).
6. **`## Completeness again`** (~420 words).
   - **`thm-completeness-infinite`** `[Completeness for Infinitely Many Premises]`: "For every collection \( \Gamma \) of formulas, finite or infinite, and every formula \( \psi \): if \( \Gamma \models \psi \), then \( \Gamma \vdash \psi \). Equivalently, every consistent collection is satisfiable."
   - *Proof* (~130 words): suppose \( \Gamma \nvdash \psi \). Then \( \Gamma \) with \( \neg\psi \) added is consistent: if a finite list \( \Gamma_0 \) of members of \( \Gamma \), with \( \neg\psi \), derived \( \bot \), then an \( \rulename{RAA} \) box would give \( \Gamma_0 \vdash \psi \). By @lem-lindenbaum it lies in a maximal consistent \( \Delta \), and by @lem-truth-lemma \( v_\Delta \) makes every member of \( \Delta \) true, in particular every member of \( \Gamma \) and \( \neg\psi \): so \( \Gamma \not\models \psi \). The equivalence: \( \Gamma \) is satisfiable iff \( \Gamma \not\models \bot \), and consistent iff \( \Gamma \nvdash \bot \).
   - **The trade-off** (~150 words; bold lead-in `**Two proofs, two prices.**`) [technique: vD, Si, En — gluing rows against extending to a maximal object]: Section 4.8 glued the rows of a truth table into one derivation, a construction one can carry out (of length about \( 2^n \)) for finitely many premises. This section never writes a derivation: it shows that if none exists, a row exists, by a construction that goes through infinitely many formulas one at a time and cannot be finished by hand. The first proof is constructive and limited to finite lists; the second reaches infinite collections and produces no derivation. The second is the route that extends beyond propositional logic.

7. **`## Compactness`** (~600 words).
   - **`thm-compactness`** `[Compactness]`: "A collection \( \Gamma \) of formulas is satisfiable if and only if every finite list of members of \( \Gamma \) is satisfiable. Consequently, \( \Gamma \models \psi \) if and only if some finite list of members of \( \Gamma \) entails \( \psi \)."
   - *Proof* (~120 words): "only if" is clear. "If": every finite list of members is satisfiable, hence consistent (Section 4.7), so \( \Gamma \) is consistent (a derivation of \( \bot \) uses a finite list), hence satisfiable (@thm-completeness-infinite). For the second sentence: \( \Gamma \models \psi \) iff \( \Gamma \) with \( \neg\psi \) is unsatisfiable, iff (first sentence) some finite list of its members is unsatisfiable, iff some finite list \( \Gamma_0 \) of members of \( \Gamma \) has \( \Gamma_0, \neg\psi \) unsatisfiable (adding \( \neg\psi \) to a list keeps it unsatisfiable), iff \( \Gamma_0 \models \psi \) (05:80).
   - **`exm-compactness-chain`** `[Infinitely many premises, finitely many used]`: "Let \( \Gamma = \{p_1\} \cup \{p_n \to p_{n+1} : n \ge 1\} \). (a) Show \( \Gamma \models p_{100} \), and find a finite list of members of \( \Gamma \) that entails \( p_{100} \). (b) Show that a finite list of members of \( \Gamma \) that leaves out \( p_{50} \to p_{51} \) does not entail \( p_{100} \)." *Solution.* (a) A valuation making \( \Gamma \) true makes \( p_1 \) true and passes truth from \( p_n \) to \( p_{n+1} \), so it makes every \( p_n \) true (@ax-induction). The hundred formulas \( p_1, \ p_1 \to p_2, \dots, p_{99} \to p_{100} \) already entail \( p_{100} \). (b) The valuation making \( p_1, \dots, p_{50} \) true and every other atom false makes every member of \( \Gamma \) except \( p_{50} \to p_{51} \) true (each other implication has a true conclusion or a false hypothesis), and makes \( p_{100} \) false. *Aftermath:* compactness says this is no accident: an entailment from infinitely many premises always rests on finitely many.
   - **Warning** (~70): compactness promises one valuation for the whole collection when each finite part has one; the valuations of the finite parts may all differ, and the theorem does not say how to find the single one. Its proof went through a maximal consistent collection, which nobody writes down.
