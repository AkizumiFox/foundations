**Purpose.**
- Analysis lives in quantifiers: "for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \) there is \( N \) such that for every \( n \ge N \) …". Propositional logic cannot see inside such a sentence (Chapter 3 §03's warning). This chapter extends the language (terms, predicates, quantifiers), the calculus (\( \forall \)E, \( \forall \)I, \( \exists \)I, \( \exists \)E, =I, =E, with their side conditions) and the semantics (models), and, in the optional §12, proves the extended calculus sound.
- Its practical goals are Stage 0's (curriculum (a) and (e)): formalize, negate and **order** quantifiers; know exactly why a \( \delta \) may not depend on an \( x \) introduced later (the eigenvariable condition), and why a witness obtained by \( \exists \)E may not be treated as arbitrary (the fresh-name condition).
- Its centrepiece is decision 2's worked example: "uniformly continuous \( \Rightarrow \) continuous" as a full derivation, and the rule-by-rule reason the converse fails (§10).
- Rhetoric is sketched only ("let \( x \) be arbitrary", "choose \( N \) such that") in each rule section's "What the rule sounds like" paragraph; the rule / phrase / Lean table begun in Chapters 1–2 is **completed** at the end of §07; the full dictionary and the prose register are Chapter 5.

**Signature moves** (`index.md`):
- **Instantiate at the term you need** (\( \forall \)E): read the term off the goal.
- **Let \( x \) be arbitrary** (\( \forall \)I): a fresh name, about which nothing is assumed.
- **Postpone the witness** (\( \exists \)I): choose the term only when an available line shows it.
- **Name the witness early** (\( \exists \)E): "choose \( x_0 \) with …", open the box at once, and never generalize over it.
- **Fix, obtain, choose** (the analysis pattern): \( \forall \)I, then \( \forall \)E and \( \exists \)E on the hypothesis, then \( \exists \)I with a term built from what is available.
- **Negate quantifier by quantifier.** Push \( \neg \) inward, flipping \( \forall \leftrightarrow \exists \) and keeping bounds.
- **Refute with a model.** A structure in which the premises are true and the conclusion false.
- **Read the dependency off the order.** A variable may depend on the variables quantified to its left, and only on them.

**The search method, extended.** Chapter 2 §06 gave the propositional method (goal-directed and premise-directed steps, RAA when stuck). Each rule section here adds one step, and §08 prints the extended method as one `::: {.algorithm}` block (our own formulation):
- **Premise \( \forall x\, \varphi \)** (§03): do not instantiate blindly; instantiate at the term the goal (or another premise) mentions.
- **Goal \( \forall x\, \varphi \)** (§04): open a flagged box with a fresh \( x_0 \); the new goal is \( \varphi[x_0/x] \).
- **Goal \( \exists x\, \varphi \)** (§05): postpone; work on the premises until a term \( t \) with \( \varphi[t/x] \) available appears, then \( \exists \)I.
- **Premise \( \exists x\, \varphi \)** (§06): open the \( \exists \)E box **early**, before any \( \forall \)I box whose fresh name must not meet the witness, and let its last line be the current goal (which must not mention the witness).
- **An equation** (§07): =E with a \( \varphi \) chosen so that one substitution turns an available line into the goal.

## 01-predicates-and-quantifiers.md — Predicates and Quantifiers

*Migrates:* old §01's hook, informal definitions, `def-bounded-quantifier` with the one-domain convention, `exm-quantifier-order-sum`, the warnings and the swap quick check; old B2 and B3 (now B1, B2). Old §01's translation examples and its other exercises move to §08 (translation now comes after the rules).

**The one new thing:** reading a quantified sentence over a domain. No rule yet.

- **Hook.** An argument that is obviously correct and that propositional logic calls invalid: "every positive real has a square root; 2 is positive; so 2 has a square root" becomes \( p, q \therefore r \) with three unrelated atoms, refuted by a row (Chapter 3, cited: `cor-refuting-row`). The missing structure is *inside* the atoms. The section ends by promising that §03 derives this argument in four lines.
- **Informal definitions** (made exact in §02): predicate (a statement with holes, the open sentence of Chapter 1 §01), variable, **domain of discourse**, the quantifiers \( \forall \) and \( \exists \), with readings ("for every", "for all", "there is", "for some"). Clash check: \( P, Q \) of Chapters 1–2 were statements; here \( P(x) \) is a predicate symbol applied to a term.
- **Truth over a domain, informally.** \( \forall x\, \varphi \) is true when every element of the domain makes \( \varphi \) true; \( \exists x\, \varphi \) when at least one does. The same sentence changes truth value with the domain: \( \forall x\, (x \ge 0) \) over ℕ, ℤ; \( \exists x\, (x^2 = 2) \) over ℚ, ℝ (the ℚ case recalls Chapter 2's `thm-sqrt2-irrational`). §11 makes this a definition; until then "true" is read informally, and **every claim about derivability is proved by a derivation**, never by this reading.
- **`def-bounded-quantifier`** (minor): \( \forall x \in A\ \varphi \) abbreviates \( \forall x (x \in A \to \varphi) \); \( \exists x \in A\ \varphi \) abbreviates \( \exists x (x \in A \wedge \varphi) \); likewise \( \forall \varepsilon > 0 \), \( \exists n \ge N \). Explain **why** \( \to \) under \( \forall \) and \( \wedge \) under \( \exists \) (the other choice gives nonsense: \( \forall x (x \in A \wedge \varphi) \) says everything is in \( A \); \( \exists x (x \in A \to \varphi) \) is true whenever something lies outside \( A \)). **One domain per formula:** the book's logic has a single domain; when a sentence mixes kinds of object (\( \varepsilon \in \nR \), \( N, n \in \nN \)), the domain is the larger one (ℝ) and the smaller kind enters as a bound (\( \exists N \in \nN \), \( \forall n \in \nN \)); a sequence \( a_n \) is a function symbol \( a \) whose values off ℕ are never used. Say this once here; every analysis translation in the chapter follows it.
- **Quantifier order** (`exm-quantifier-order-sum`, the curriculum's example): \( \forall x \exists y\, (x + y = 0) \) true on ℝ, \( \exists y \forall x\, (x + y = 0) \) false; read each aloud as a game (the second player may see the first move). The dependence of \( y \) on \( x \) is the whole difference; §04–§06 will show the same dependence as a fact about boxes.
- **Examples** (reading, simplest first): \( \forall x\, (x^2 \ge 0) \) over ℝ; "there is no largest prime" read as \( \neg\exists p\, (\mathrm{Prime}(p) \wedge \forall q\, (\mathrm{Prime}(q) \to q \le p)) \); the degenerate bounded case \( \forall x \in \emptyset\ \varphi \) (true, informally; derived in §04) versus \( \exists x \in \emptyset\ \varphi \) (false).
- **Warnings.** "Any" is ambiguous in English ("if any \( x \) satisfies…" is \( \exists \); "any \( x \) satisfies…" is \( \forall \)). Hidden universal quantifiers: a theorem stated "if \( n \) is even then \( n^2 \) is even" means \( \forall n \) (Chapter 5 names this convention). An existential with \( \to \) inside is almost always a mistranslation.
- **Quick check.** Swap the quantifiers in "for every \( n \) there is a prime larger than \( n \)"; is the result true? (STYLE §10 swap check.)
- **What the rules sound like:** none yet; one sentence pointing to §03–§06.
- **Exercises.** A1: read five sentences aloud and say over which of ℕ, ℤ, ℝ each is true; spot the mistranslation among three bounded forms. B1: for each of four ordered-quantifier sentences over ℕ, ℤ or ℝ, decide true or false with a witness or a reason. B2 (example generation): a predicate \( R(x, y) \) on ℕ such that \( \forall x \exists y\, R \) is true and \( \exists y \forall x\, R \) is false; check both (e.g. \( R(x, y) \) = "\( y > x \)"; the solution lists the two properties any answer must have). C1: decide \( \exists x \forall y\, (x \le y) \) over ℕ, ℤ, \( [0, 1] \), \( (0, 1] \), with the witness or the reason. C2: rewrite "every element of \( A \) is positive" and "some element of \( A \) is positive" with the wrong connective under the quantifier, and give a set \( A \subseteq \nR \) (domain ℝ) for which each wrong form has the wrong truth value.

## 02-terms-formulas-substitution.md — Terms, Formulas, Free Variables and Substitution

*Migrates:* old §02 whole, with every S2 fix (the capture hook \( \exists y\, (y \ne x) \); "\( a \) is not a variable"; the exact `def-free-for`; \( \varphi[t/x] = \varphi \) proved in the text; the renaming justification; the new C1).

**The one new thing:** substitution \( \varphi[t/x] \), and when it is safe. (Terms, formulas and free variables are the minor definitions it needs, each introduced just before use.)

- **Hook.** "\( x > 2 \)" is not a statement (the open-sentence non-example of Chapter 1 §01, recalled); "\( \forall x\, (x^2 \ge 0) \)" is; "\( \forall x\, (x > y) \)" depends on \( y \). The difference is which variables are **free**. And "\( \exists y\, (y \ne x) \)" (true in ℕ for every \( x \)), read with \( y \) put for \( x \), becomes \( \exists y\, (y \ne y) \), false for a silly reason: **capture**. (Not \( \forall y\, (y \ne x) \): it is false for every \( x \) already, so capture changes nothing visible.) Every rule of §03–§07 writes \( \varphi[t/x] \), so this section is their grammar.
- **Definitions** (inductive, as in Chapter 3 §01; structural induction and recursion carry over — say so, cite `thm-structural-induction` and `thm-structural-recursion` for the pattern, and note the new clauses):
  - `def-term`: variables, constants, \( f(t_1, \dots, t_n) \).
  - `def-first-order-formula`: atomic \( R(t_1, \dots, t_n) \) and \( t_1 = t_2 \), \( \bot \), connectives, \( \forall x\, \varphi \), \( \exists x\, \varphi \). Parse trees with quantifier nodes.
  - `def-free-variable` (by recursion; a quantifier node binds its variable in its subtree), bound occurrence, scope. `def-sentence` (no free variables).
  - `def-substitution` \( \varphi[t/x] \): replace the **free** occurrences of \( x \). Say in one sentence that this is a different operation from Chapter 3's substitution \( \sigma \) of formulas for atoms (§04 there, `lem-substitution`, cited): here a **term** replaces a **variable**, and bound occurrences are left alone (S2 recheck #6). `def-free-for`: \( t \) is free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \) if no free occurrence of \( x \) in \( \varphi \) lies in the scope of a quantifier \( \forall y \) or \( \exists y \) with \( y \) a variable of \( t \) (informally: no variable of \( t \) becomes bound). In the text, with a two-line structural induction: if \( x \) is not free in \( \varphi \), then \( \varphi[t/x] = \varphi \) (the passage laws of §09 use this, so it may not be left to an exercise). Renaming bound variables (\( \forall y\, \varphi \) and \( \forall z\, \varphi[z/y] \) for \( z \) not occurring in \( \varphi \)) as the standard way out; stated and used. Its justification: for a renaming at the front, \( \forall y\, \varphi \dashv\vdash \forall z\, \varphi[z/y] \) is a three-line derivation each way once §04's rule exists (worked there; the \( \exists \) version in §06); renaming inside a formula is taken as a convention here, and its semantic justification is an instance of `lem-term-substitution` (optional §12; prose pointer only).
- **Worked examples.** Free and bound occurrences in \( \forall \varepsilon (\varepsilon > 0 \to \exists N \forall n (n \ge N \to \abs{a_n - L} < \varepsilon)) \): which variables are free? (Only \( L \); \( a \) is a function symbol, not a variable, so "free" does not apply to it, though the statement is about \( a \) too.) Substitutions that are fine and one that captures: \( (\exists y\, (y > x))[y + 1 / x] \) gives \( \exists y\, (y > y + 1) \): true becomes false. \( y + 1 \) **is** free for \( x \) in \( \forall z\, (x < z) \).
- **How substitution looks in a mathematical proof.** "Apply the hypothesis with \( \varepsilon/2 \) in place of \( \varepsilon \)" is a substitution; "apply it with \( n \) in place of \( N \)" inside \( \exists N \forall n \) would be a capture. Say so with the formula shown.
- **Warnings.** The same letter may occur free and bound in one formula (\( P(x) \wedge \forall x\, Q(x) \)); legal, but the book renames to avoid it. Capture changes meaning: exhibit it.
- **Quick checks.** Is \( x \) free in \( \forall y\, (x < y) \to \exists x\, (x = 0) \)? Where? Is \( y + 1 \) free for \( x \) in \( \forall z\, (x < z) \)?
- **Exercises.** A1: free/bound marking for four formulas; sentences or not. B1: compute five substitutions, two of which need renaming first. B2: define the set of free variables by recursion (the clauses), then check it on two formulas. C1: prove by structural induction that if \( t \) is free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \) and \( x \) is free in \( \varphi \), then the free variables of \( \varphi[t/x] \) are those of \( \varphi \) other than \( x \), together with those of \( t \); show by the capture example of the text that the hypothesis "free for" is needed. ## 03-universal-elimination.md — Using "For Every": Instantiation

**The one new thing:** \( \forall \)E. With it alone, universal statements can be **used** but not proved; the section ends on that stall, which motivates §04.

- **Motivation.** Every "by Theorem 3, applied to \( x = 2 \)" is this rule. The §01 hook argument is its first instance.
- **`def-rule-universal-elimination`** (minor, one rule). In the definition block:
  ```{=latex}
  % schema
  \begin{center}
  $\begin{nd}[schema]
    \have {i} {\forall x\, \varphi}
    \have {k} {\varphi[t/x]} \Ae{i}
  \end{nd}$
  \end{center}
  ```

  2. \( \forall x \forall y\, R(x, y) \vdash R(c, c) \) (3 lines: \( \forall \)E twice, the same term twice). Idea: one quantifier per step, outermost first; nothing forbids equal terms.

  4. \( \forall x (P(x) \to P(f(x))), P(c) \vdash P(f(f(c))) \) (6 lines: \( \forall \)E at \( c \), \( \to \)E, \( \forall \)E at \( f(c) \), \( \to \)E). - **Hook.** To prove \( x^2 - 2x + 1 \ge 0 \) for every real \( x \), we cannot check every \( x \). We take one we know **nothing** about and prove it for that one. The rule makes "nothing" precise. **The one new thing:** \( \exists \)E and its box (a flagged box **with** an assumption).

- **Hook.** To use "there is a \( k \) with \( n = 2k \)", give it a name and forget where it came from. Chapter 2's evenness arguments (`thm-square-even-implies-even`, cited) did this informally.
- **`def-rules-existential`.** In the definition block, the \( \exists \)E schema, then \( \exists \)I restated (recalled from `def-rule-existential-introduction`):
  ```{=latex}
  % schema
  \begin{center}
  $\begin{nd}[schema]
    \have {h} {\exists x\, \varphi}
    \open[x_0]
    \hypo {i} {\varphi[x_0/x]}
    \have {} {\vdots}
    \have {j} {\chi}
    \close
    \have {k} {\chi} \Ee{h,i-j}
  \end{nd}$
  \end{center}
  ```
  Side condition: \( x_0 \) does **not** appear in \( \chi \), in \( \exists x\, \varphi \), in any premise, or in any assumption of a box **still open at line \( k \)** (the box's own assumption is closed by then); \( \chi \) is the last line of the box itself.
  - **Clause-by-clause reading.** The box says "whatever the witness is, \( \chi \) follows"; so \( \chi \) must not mention it. "Not in a premise or open assumption": the name must be new to what we know, or we would be assuming something about the witness. "Not in \( \exists x\, \varphi \)" is **needed**: without it, \( \forall y \exists x\, R(x, y) \vdash \exists z\, R(z, z) \) (\( \forall \)E at \( x_0 \) gives \( \exists x\, R(x, x_0) \); box \( x_0 \) with assumption \( R(x_0, x_0) \); \( \exists \)I), false in ℤ with \( < \). It also guarantees that \( x_0 \) is free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \). The phrase "open at line \( k \)" matters: read inside the box, "open assumption" would include the box's own, \( \varphi[x_0/x] \), and the rule could never apply whenever \( x \) occurs free in \( \varphi \), which is every case that matters (S2 recheck #10: "could never apply" overstated it).
  - The rule's premise-directed use: **open the box early**, and make its last line the current goal.
- **Worked derivations** (`exm-existential-elimination-derivations`, each with an Idea):

## 07-equality-and-uniqueness.md — Equality and Unique Existence

*Migrates:* old §06 whole (`def-rules-equality`; `prp-equality-symmetric-transitive`; calculation chains; `def-unique-existence` with the S2 proviso; `prp-unique-existence-use`; worked examples; the corrected bound-position warning; quick check; exercises A1, B1–B3, C1 with its checked finite example, C2).
