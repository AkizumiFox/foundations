# Blueprint: Chapter 5 — From Derivations to Mathematical Prose

**Directory:** `src/ch05-from-derivations-to-prose/` (slug `from-derivations-to-prose`). **Part 0, the last of five logic chapters**, main line. It is the bridge from the formal calculus of Chapters 1–4 to the prose of the rest of the book. Its final section is the dispute over excluded middle (decision 3, spec below). Level reference: none directly (Huth & Ryan has no counterpart); the rhetoric is ours, and the education research behind §04 and §06 is `authoring/design/research-reader.md` A1, B4 and `curriculum.md` "Evidence".

**Purpose.**
- Mathematicians do not write Fitch boxes, yet every proof in this book **is** a derivation underneath. This chapter makes the correspondence exact and usable in both directions:
  - **reading:** recover the derivation (at least its skeleton) from a prose proof, and so check it;
  - **writing:** start from the derivation's skeleton and produce the prose a mathematician would write, knowing which steps may go silent and which never may.
- Tools: a **rhetoric dictionary** (every primitive and derived rule, the phrases that carry it, an analysis example), **four levels of abbreviation** of one proof (full derivation → skeleton → annotated prose → plain prose), the book's single **self-explanation training passage** (STYLE §11.1), and the **"what is not allowed" catalogue** with proof verification.
- It hands over to the rest of the book: from Chapter 6 on, proofs are plain prose (level 3), with Part 0's rule tags (level 2) allowed in Part 0 only (STYLE §10), and the catalogue is cited whenever a warning concerns one of its errors.
- It ends with excluded middle: the one rule (RAA) that separates classical from constructive mathematics, its history, and what it means for analysis. The main line stays classical.

**Signature moves** (`index.md`):
- **Hear the rule in the phrase.** "Let" is \( \forall \)I or an assumption; "choose" is \( \exists \)E; "take" is \( \exists \)I; "suppose, for contradiction" is RAA or \( \neg \)I.
- **Expand to check.** When a step is doubtful, rewrite that stretch one level lower.
- **Skeleton first.** Write the first and last sentence of every box before filling any.
- **Self-explain each line** by earlier lines, definitions and named results — never by paraphrase.
- **Two columns when checking:** *is the line true?* and *does it follow, and by which rule?*
- **Say what depends on what.** Every chosen object carries its dependencies, which are the boxes open when it was chosen.

## The rhetoric dictionary (specification for §01–§02)

Each row: **rule** → **phrases** (in the order a writer should prefer them) → **analysis example** (a sentence from a proof the reader has seen or will see in Part I, stated here in full, with the rule's premises visible) → **may it be silent in plain prose?** (feeds `def-silent-steps`) → **Lean** (one column, optional). Every row gets one worked `exm-rhetoric-*` in its section where the phrase is shown doing its job; rows grouped as in the labels list. The prose phrases must be our own choices; common phrases ("let", "suppose", "hence") are common property.

| Rule | Phrases | Analysis example (to be written out in full in the section) | Silent at level 3? |
|---|---|---|---|
| \( \wedge \)I | "…, and …"; "we show (a) … and (b) …"; "both … hold" | "\( s \) is an upper bound of \( A \), and no smaller number is" (sup, two clauses) | often, when both conjuncts are displayed |
| \( \wedge \)E | "in particular"; "so \( a > 0 \)"; silently using one clause of a hypothesis | "since \( x \in (0, 1) \), in particular \( x > 0 \)" | yes |
| \( \to \)I | "assume …; then …; hence if … then …"; "suppose …"; "if …, then …" as the frame of the whole proof | "Suppose \( \abs{x - 1} < 1 \). Then … so \( x > 0 \)." | never the assumption; the closing "hence" may be silent |
| \( \to \)E | "since …, …"; "by (hypothesis/lemma), …"; "hence" | "Since \( f \) is continuous at \( a \) and \( a_n \to a \), …" | the citation, never |
| \( \vee \)I | "…, so certainly \( P \) or \( Q \)"; "in particular \( x \ne 0 \) or …" | "\( x > 0 \), so \( x \ge 0 \)" (weakening a strict inequality is \( \vee \)I) | yes |
| \( \vee \)E | "we consider two cases"; "Case 1 … Case 2 …"; "in either case"; "otherwise" | the triangle inequality by signs | never the split; the "in either case" may be silent |
| \( \neg \)I | "suppose \( P \); … a contradiction; hence \( \neg P \)"; "\( P \) is impossible, since …" | "suppose some rational \( r \) has \( r^2 = 2 \) …" | never |

| \( \bot \)E | "this case cannot occur"; "vacuously"; "there is nothing to prove" | "if \( A = \emptyset \) there is nothing to prove" (vacuous \( \forall x \in \emptyset \), Chapter 4 §04) | yes, once the contradiction or the empty case is stated |

| \( \leftrightarrow \)E | "by definition"; "that is"; "i.e."; "equivalently" | "\( f \) is strictly increasing, that is, \( x < y \) implies \( f(x) < f(y) \)" | yes for definitional iff |

The §07 flag (STYLE §10) is added only for a genuinely non-constructive step such as this one, not for order trichotomy on ℝ, which Chapter 11 takes as an axiom |
| DS | "since \( x \ne 0 \), the first case is impossible, so …" | "\( x^2 = x \) so \( x = 0 \) or \( x = 1 \); since \( x \ne 0 \), \( x = 1 \)" | the rule's name yes; the fact \( x \ne 0 \) it uses, never |

| \( \forall \)E | "in particular, for \( x = \)…"; "applying … with \( \varepsilon/2 \) in place of \( \varepsilon \)" | "apply the definition of \( a_n \to L \) with \( \varepsilon/2 \)" | the instance must be named when it is not the obvious one |

| \( \exists \)E | "choose \( N \) such that"; "by hypothesis there is \( k \) with \( n = 2k \)"; "let \( x_0 \) be such a point" | "choose \( N \) (depending on \( \varepsilon \)) such that …" | never; the name must be fresh |
| =I, =E | "substituting"; "\( = \) … \( = \)" chains; "by (1)" | a calc chain for \( \abs{x^2 - 4} \) | yes, inside chains |
| \( \exists! \) | "there is exactly one"; "existence … uniqueness …"; "suppose \( y \) also …" | "\( 2x + 3 = 7 \) has exactly one real solution: \( x = 2 \) is one, and if \( 2y + 3 = 7 \) then \( y = 2 \)"; the uniqueness half alone in the uniqueness of a limit (§02) | never either half |
| induction (cites Chapter 3's `ax-induction`, `thm-strong-induction`) | "we argue by induction on \( n \)"; "base case"; "suppose the claim holds for \( n \)" | \( 0 < a^n \le 1 \) for \( 0 < a \le 1 \) and every \( n \in \nN \), base case \( P(0) \) (`exm-rhetoric-induction`) | never either the base case or the step (the induction hypothesis is stated) |
| cut / lemma (cites Chapter 3's `lem-weakening-cut`) | "we first show (Claim)"; "it suffices to show"; "we claim" | "it suffices to show \( \abs{a_n - L} < \varepsilon \) for \( n \ge N \)" | never the claim itself; "it suffices" may be dropped if the claim is displayed and then used |
| symmetry (not a rule: a derived pattern) | "without loss of generality"; "similarly"; "by symmetry" | "WLOG \( x \le y \)" as \( \vee \)E whose second box is the first with letters swapped | allowed only when the swap is literally exact |

## 02-quantifier-rhetoric.md — The Quantifier Rules in Prose

- **Hook.** "Let \( \varepsilon > 0 \). Choose \( N \) …. Then for \( n \ge N \) …": three sentences, three rules, and one dependence the prose records in a parenthesis.
- **Content.** Dictionary rows for \( \forall, \exists \), =, \( \exists! \), bounded quantifiers, induction, cut and "it suffices", WLOG and symmetry. The **dependency parenthesis**: "choose \( N \) (depending on \( \varepsilon \))" is the prose record of which \( \forall \)I boxes are open (Chapter 4 §10) — this is the rule behind STYLE §4's "say what each constant depends on". The hidden universal closure. "Any".
- **Worked examples**, each a short analysis proof annotated phrase by phrase: `exm-rhetoric-universal`, the limit of a constant sequence (\( \forall \)I on \( \varepsilon \) and on \( n \); any \( N \) works); `exm-rhetoric-existential`, \( 1/n \to 0 \) for the sequence \( (1/n)_{n \ge 1} \), so Chapter 4 §08's definition is read with \( N \) and \( n \) in \( \nZ_{>0} \) instead of \( \nN \), as `NOTATION.md` prescribes when the formula needs \( n \ge 1 \) (say so in one clause) (a cut first: "it suffices to find \( N \in \nZ_{>0} \) with \( 1/N < \varepsilon \), since \( n \ge N \) gives \( 0 < 1/n \le 1/N \)", the claim displayed and then used, row-89's phrase; then \( \exists \)I with \( N \in \nZ_{>0} \), \( N > 1/\varepsilon \), the dependency parenthesis, so \( n \ge N \) gives \( 0 < 1/n \le 1/N < \varepsilon \); school fact: an integer beyond any real, pointer to Chapter 11; it is positive because \( 1/\varepsilon > 0 \)); `exm-rhetoric-equality`, the uniqueness of a limit (two \( \exists \)E's with fresh \( N_1, N_2 \), the \( \varepsilon/2 \) choice, a calculation chain, then Chapter 4's `prp-arbitrarily-close-equal`, cited: the uniqueness half of \( \exists! \)); `exm-rhetoric-induction`: for a real \( a \) with \( 0 < a \le 1 \), \( 0 < a^n \le 1 \) for every \( n \in \nN \), by Chapter 3's `ax-induction` (cited) with \( P(n) \) the conjunction "\( 0 < a^n \) and \( a^n \le 1 \)" (base case \( P(0) \): \( a^0 = 1 \), school fact, and \( 0 < 1 \le 1 \); step: \( a^{n+1} = a^n a > 0 \) as a product of positives, using \( a^n > 0 \) from the induction hypothesis by \( \wedge \)E, and \( a^{n+1} = a^n a \le a^n \le 1 \), using \( a \le 1 \), \( a^n > 0 \) and \( a^n \le 1 \); the two halves joined by \( \wedge \)I). ## 03-levels-of-abbreviation.md — Four Levels of One Proof

- **Hook.** A derivation, a skeleton and a paragraph are the same proof written for different readers.
- **`def-levels-of-proof`** (minor definition; the chapter's organizing idea):
  - **Level 0 — full derivation.** Every line one rule, where a rule may be a derived rule used as one line (Chapter 2's `def-derived-rule`, cited; Chapter 4 §08's bounded-quantifier rules); arithmetic facts entered as premises.
  - **Level 1 — skeleton.** The boxes and the logical lines only; each mathematical stretch compressed into one line justified "arith", "by def", or a cited result.
  - **Level 2 — annotated prose.** Sentences, each tagged with its rule in brackets (the Part 0 rule tags of STYLE §10).
  - **Level 3 — plain prose.** What Part I onward writes.
- **`def-silent-steps`:** the convention, exactly the dictionary's last column (the two must agree row for row; the drafter checks this). At level 3 **may be silent:** \( \wedge \)I (when both conjuncts are displayed) and \( \wedge \)E; R; \( \vee \)I; \( \bot \)E once the contradiction or the empty case is stated; the name of DS; definitional \( \leftrightarrow \)E; the obvious \( \forall \)E; =I and =E inside chains; the phrase "it suffices" when the cut's claim is displayed and then used; and the **closing** lines of \( \to \)I ("hence \( P \to Q \)"), \( \forall \)I ("since \( x \) was arbitrary") and \( \vee \)E ("in either case"). **Never silent:** the openings of \( \to \)I, \( \neg \)I, RAA, \( \forall \)I and \( \exists \)E (with a fresh name), and the conclusions of \( \neg \)I and RAA ("hence \( \neg P \)", "hence \( P \)"); a \( \neg\neg \)E or LEM step, written as an explicit case split (as for \( \vee \)E) or an explicit removal of the double negation ("\( P \) must hold, since …"), with the §07 flag when the step is genuinely non-constructive; the case split of \( \vee \)E with the reason it exhausts; the clash of \( \neg \)E; the witness of \( \exists \)I with its dependencies; a non-obvious \( \forall \)E instance; every citation (so every \( \to \)E of a hypothesis or result); both directions of \( \leftrightarrow \)I and both halves of \( \exists! \); the base case and step of an induction; the announcement of a contrapositive; the negated form after De Morgan or quantifier negation; a cut's claim; a WLOG with the symmetry that licenses it.

## 07-excluded-middle.md — The Dispute over Excluded Middle

Specification: `decisions.md` item 3, binding. All results here are `.optional` (nothing later depends on them), including `prp-not-not-excluded-middle`; so B1's printed solution and the A1 answers may discuss it but must not cite it as a step (`check_optional.py`); later chapters may point here in prose when they flag a non-constructive step (STYLE §10).

- **Hook.** Chapter 2 §05 found that one rule, RAA, is the only classical rule, and that over the other rules it is interderivable with \( \neg\neg \)E and LEM (Chapter 2's `thm-classical-rules-interderivable`, cited). Chapter 3 §05's optional theorem showed that it cannot be derived from the others (prose pointer only). Some mathematicians refused it. Why, and what is lost without it?
- **One extra rule** (recall, no re-proof): LEM, \( \neg\neg \)E and RAA are one principle over the other rules; the **intuitionistic** system is the primitive rules of Chapters 1, 2 and 4 minus RAA (this is why \( \bot \)E was kept primitive — pay Chapter 2 §05's remark).
- **`prp-not-not-excluded-middle`** (`.optional`; **Try it first**; key proof): \( \vdash \neg\neg(P \vee \neg P) \) without RAA (8 lines, no \( \bot \)E either) — a \( \neg \)I box on \( \neg(P \vee \neg P) \), inside it a \( \neg \)I box on \( P \). Aftermath: nobody can *refute* excluded middle; the intuitionist declines to *assert* it. Remark (no proof): every classical propositional tautology becomes intuitionistically derivable after \( \neg\neg \) is put in front (Glivenko, 1929).
- **BHK reading.** A proof of \( P \wedge Q \) is a pair of proofs; of \( P \vee Q \), a proof of one **with a label saying which**; of \( P \to Q \), a method turning proofs of \( P \) into proofs of \( Q \); of \( \neg P \), a method turning proofs of \( P \) into proofs of \( \bot \) (Chapter 2 §02's remark that \( \neg P \) behaves like \( P \to \bot \)); \( \bot \) has no proof; of \( \exists x\, \varphi \), a witness with a proof; of \( \forall x\, \varphi \), a method for every \( x \). Read LEM under BHK: a uniform method deciding every statement — which nobody has.
- **`thm-irrational-power-rational`** (`.optional`): there are irrational \( a, b \) with \( a^b \) rational.
  - *Non-constructive proof:* LEM on "\( \sqrt 2^{\sqrt 2} \) is rational". Either it is (take \( a = b = \sqrt 2 \)) or not (take \( a = \sqrt 2^{\sqrt 2} \), \( b = \sqrt 2 \); \( a^b = 2 \)). Say exactly which line uses LEM, and that the proof does not tell us which case holds. (It is the second: by the Gelfond–Schneider theorem, proved independently by Gelfond and by Schneider in 1934, \( \sqrt 2^{\sqrt 2} \) is transcendental. Prose remark, no proof.)
  - *Constructive proof* (**Try it first**): \( a = \sqrt 2 \), \( b = \log_2 9 \), \( a^b = 3 \). Needs `prp-log2-9-irrational` (`.optional`): if \( \log_2 9 = p/q \) then \( 2^p = 9^q \), even = odd (uses \( p, q \ge 1 \), since \( \log_2 9 > 0 \)). Exponent laws for real exponents are school facts here; pointer to the Part I chapter that defines \( a^x \) (prose only; take its number from `book.yaml`).
- **History** (prose, dated, checked; every quotation either verified against a primary source or omitted — `TODO(verify)` until checked): Brouwer 1908 ("De onbetrouwbaarheid der logische principes", *Tijdschrift voor Wijsbegeerte*: the unreliability of the logical principles); Kolmogorov 1925 (the first double-negation translation); Hilbert's July 1927 Hamburg lecture "Die Grundlagen der Mathematik" (printed 1928; the telescope-and-boxer remark on excluded middle may be quoted only from van Heijenoort's *From Frege to Gödel* translation, checked against the page) and the Grundlagenstreit (including the 1928 *Mathematische Annalen* editorial board affair, in one sentence: Hilbert had Brouwer removed, and the board was dissolved and re-formed without him); Heyting 1930 ("Die formalen Regeln der intuitionistischen Logik", *Sitzungsberichte der Preußischen Akademie*) formalizes intuitionistic logic; Glivenko 1929; the Gödel–Gentzen double-negation translation, 1933 (Gödel's paper appeared in 1933; Gentzen found it independently and withdrew his paper of 1933 when Gödel's appeared): classical arithmetic is consistent if intuitionistic arithmetic is. (Not "Gentzen 1936": that is his consistency proof for arithmetic, a different paper.) Bishop 1967, *Foundations of Constructive Analysis*: constructive analysis without Brouwer's philosophy.
- **Consequences for analysis** (previews, stated informally; Part I proves the classical theorems and should point back here):
  - the supremum: constructively, a non-empty set of reals bounded above has a supremum iff it is **order-located** (upper located; often just "located"): for all \( x < y \), either \( y \) is an upper bound or some element exceeds \( x \) (Bishop's constructive least-upper-bound principle);
  - Specker's sequence (1949): a computable increasing bounded sequence of rationals whose limit is not a computable real;
  - the intermediate value theorem: for \( f \) continuous on \( [a, b] \) with \( f(a) < 0 < f(b) \), constructively only the **approximate** version (for every \( \varepsilon > 0 \) some \( x \in [a, b] \) has \( \abs{f(x)} < \varepsilon \)) holds in general; the exact version holds under extra hypotheses (for example \( f \) strictly increasing), which the prose need not give;
  - Bolzano–Weierstrass: the bisection step "one half contains infinitely many terms" is LEM on a statement nobody can decide in general.
- **Curry–Howard and Lean.** Propositions as types, proofs as programs; the BHK clauses as data types. In Lean, `Classical.em` is a theorem, derived from the axiom `Classical.choice` via Diaconescu's theorem (choice with function and propositional extensionality implies LEM). A definition that uses choice to produce data (for instance, branching on an undecided proposition through classical decidability) must be marked `noncomputable`; a proof that uses `Classical.em` needs no mark, since proofs carry no data. One `code` snippet at most.
- **The book's position.** The main line stays classical. The ordered-field axioms of Chapter 11 already include trichotomy, which fails constructively; so the classical commitment is made once, in the axioms, and a case split \( x \ge 0 \) or \( x < 0 \) is not flagged. Where a proof takes a genuinely non-constructive step of the kind discussed here (as in Bolzano–Weierstrass) or uses choice, one clause flags it (STYLE §10); nothing more.
- **Warnings.** Intuitionistic logic does not say LEM is false: no instance \( P \vee \neg P \) can be refuted, since \( \neg\neg(P \vee \neg P) \) is provable. (Brouwer's own analysis, with extra axioms about the continuum, does refute the *universal* statement "every real is 0 or not 0"; one clause at most.) "Constructive" is not "computable in practice".
