### 4.0 `index.md`

**Budget:** ≤ 1,000 words (expected ~990: story ~150, reading note ~90, quiz ≤ 200, What you need ~40, Time ~25, Minimum ~25, roadmap ~150, named moves ~120, Where this goes ~60, Summary ~170, Terms; Terms lines are short). 1. **Story (new, ~150 words).** The motivating question (C3): "The axioms of a partial order say that \( \le \) is reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive. Do they prove that any two elements are comparable, \( \forall x\, \forall y\, (x \le y \vee y \le x) \)? Every order a school student meets, on numbers, is comparable, so a search for a derivation looks hopeful; it stalls, and a stalled search proves nothing. Two dots settle it: a domain \( \{ a, b \} \) in which each element is \( \le \) itself and nothing else. All three axioms are true there and comparability is false, so, by a statement of Section 7.2, no derivation exists. This chapter makes 'true there' exact, turns such pictures into proofs of non-derivability, and then uses them on whole theories: in Section 7.4 the same question about groups, 'is every group commutative?', is answered by the six symmetries of a triangle." No figure in the index (the antichain is drawn in 7.4, Figure 7.4-A).
2. **How to read this chapter (new, ~90 words; bold lead-in; C1).** "First reading: Sections 7.1, 7.2 and 7.4, and Section 7.5 up to 'Isomorphic structures'. Second pass: the rest of Section 7.5.    **`def-isomorphism-of-structures`** `[Isomorphism of Structures]`: "Let \( \fM \), \( \fN \) be structures for the same symbols, with domains \( D \), \( E \). An **isomorphism** \( h \colon \fM \to \fN \) is a bijection \( h \colon D \to E \) such that \( h(c^{\fM}) = c^{\fN} \) for each constant; \( h(f^{\fM}(d_1, \dots, d_n)) = f^{\fN}(h(d_1), \dots, h(d_n)) \) for each function symbol; and \( (d_1, \dots, d_n) \in R^{\fM} \) if and only if \( (h(d_1), \dots, h(d_n)) \in R^{\fN} \) for each predicate symbol. An isomorphism \( \fM \to \fM \) is an **automorphism**. \( \fM \) and \( \fN \) are **isomorphic** if an isomorphism exists."
   Examples (~150 words): \( (\nN, <) \) and the even numbers with \( < \), by \( h(d) = 2d \) (bijective onto the evens; \( d < e \) iff \( 2d < 2e \)); Figure 7.5-I: \( \fM \) with \( D = \{ a, b, c \} \), \( R^{\fM} = \{ (a, b), (b, c), (a, c) \} \), and \( (\{ 1, 2, 3 \}, <) \), with the table \( a \mapsto 1 \), \( b \mapsto 2 \), \( c \mapsto 3 \) beside it; a non-example: \( d \mapsto d + 1 \) from \( (\nN, <) \) to itself preserves \( < \) and is not onto (nothing is sent to \( 0 \)).
   **`thm-isomorphism-invariance`** `[Isomorphic Structures Satisfy the Same Sentences]`: "Let \( h \colon \fM \to \fN \) be an isomorphism. For every formula \( \varphi \) and every assignment \( \ell \) in \( \fM \), \( \fM, \ell \models \varphi \) if and only if \( \fN, h \circ \ell \models \varphi \), where \( h \circ \ell \) gives each variable \( x \) the value \( h(\ell(x)) \). In particular \( \fM \models \sigma \) iff \( \fN \models \sigma \) for every sentence \( \sigma \)." Meaning (STYLE §12.12): "*In words: renaming the elements, consistently, changes the truth of nothing.*" Proof → fold **F7.5-a** `title="The proof: terms, then formulas"` (~450 words): terms first, \( h(t^{\fM, \ell}) = t^{\fN, h \circ \ell} \) by induction on \( t \) (variables by the definition of \( h \circ \ell \), constants and function symbols by the clauses of the definition); then structural induction on \( \varphi \), for all assignments at once (as in @lem-free-coincidence): atomic by the predicate clause and the term claim; equations since \( h \) is injective; connectives by (S4), (S5); \( \forall x\, \varphi \): \( \fM, \ell \models \forall x\, \varphi \) iff for all \( d \in D \), \( \fM, \ell[x \mapsto d] \models \varphi \), iff (hypothesis) for all \( d \), \( \fN, h \circ (\ell[x \mapsto d]) \models \varphi \); and \( h \circ (\ell[x \mapsto d]) = (h \circ \ell)[x \mapsto h(d)] \); as \( d \) runs over \( D \), \( h(d) \) runs over **all** of \( E \) because \( h \) is onto; so this is (S6) for \( \fN, h \circ \ell \). \( \exists \) likewise with (S7). The fold marks "onto" and "injective" in bold where used (A1 (e) asks).

5. **`## Numbers that are not numerals` (~620 words).** **`exm-non-standard-naturals`** `[A Model of Arithmetic with an Infinite Number]`: let \( T \) be the set of all sentences of \( 0, S, +, \cdot, < \) true in \( \nN \), add a new constant \( c \), and the axioms \( \underline{n} < c \) for each \( n \in \nN \), where \( \underline{n} \) is the numeral \( S(S(\cdots S(0) \cdots)) \) with \( n \) letters \( S \). 6. **`## Induction: a schema and an axiom` (~450 words).** Text: the **induction schema** of first-order arithmetic is one sentence for each formula \( \varphi \) with free variable \( x \): \( \bigl( \varphi[0/x] \wedge \forall x\, (\varphi \to \varphi[S(x)/x]) \bigr) \to \forall x\, \varphi \) (its other free variables, if any, bound by \( \forall \) in front). No contradiction: the schema speaks only about sets **defined by formulas**, and the standard elements are not such a set. The argument, in four sentences (main line, S2 round 1; it rests on no exercise): suppose a formula \( \varphi \) with free variable \( x \) defined the standard elements in \( \fM \). By @lem-term-substitution (Section 7.3; \( 0 \) is closed and \( S(x) \) is free for \( x \) in \( \varphi \)), \( \varphi[0/x] \) holds in \( \fM \) exactly when \( \varphi \) holds of \( 0^{\fM} \), and \( \varphi[S(x)/x] \) holds under \( \ell \) exactly when \( \varphi \) holds of \( S^{\fM}(\ell(x)) \) (S2 round 2: the optional section cites optional 7.3 here, by name). Then \( \varphi[0/x] \) holds in \( \fM \), since \( 0^{\fM} \) is standard, and \( \forall x\, (\varphi \to \varphi[S(x)/x]) \) holds, since the successor of a standard element is standard. The induction instance for \( \varphi \) is a sentence true in \( \nN \), so it is in \( T \) and true in \( \fM \); it gives \( \forall x\, \varphi \) in \( \fM \), so every element would be standard, against \( c^{\fM} \). (Exercise C2 reaches the same conclusion another way, by least elements.) The induction principle of Section 4.2 (@ax-induction) is stated for every property of natural numbers, which is the **second-order** form: it quantifies over all sets, not only the definable ones, and it does rule out non-standard elements. "First-order logic can state the schema, not the axiom; compactness is the price of its completeness."
7. **`## What can be decided` (~500 words).** Three statements, each in a `::: {.remark}` with a bold name, stated without proof: **Decidable: propositional logic.** "Chapter 4's search tree decides every propositional claim in finitely many steps (@prp-search-decides)." **Undecidable: predicate logic (Church and Turing, 1936).** "No mechanical procedure decides, for every first-order sentence, whether it is derivable." With completeness: "listing all derivations finds one when one exists; what no procedure can do is announce, in every case, that none exists." **Decidable: the monadic fragment.** "For sentences with only one-place predicate symbols and no constants, function symbols or \( = \), the bound of Section 7.2's remark makes the counter-model search finite." (S2: "constants" added so that the statement is exactly the case the remark's bound covers; the fragment stays decidable with constants and with \( = \), which this section does not claim.) Then **Incompleteness, named** (~120 words, a remark): "Gödel showed in 1931 that no consistent theory whose axioms a mechanical procedure can list, and which derives the basic facts of arithmetic, derives every sentence true in \( \nN \). Completeness says every sentence true in **all** models of a theory is derivable from it; incompleteness says that the sentences true in **the** intended model \( \nN \) are not all true in all models of any such theory. The two theorems do not conflict: the non-standard models are where they part." **Quick check (new):** "A sentence \( \sigma \) is true in \( \nN \) and false in some model of a theory \( T \) of arithmetic. Can \( T \vdash \sigma \)?" *Solution:* no: by the soundness statement, \( T \vdash \sigma \) would make \( \sigma \) true in every model of \( T \).
