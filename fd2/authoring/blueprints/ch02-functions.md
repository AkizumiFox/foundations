6. **`thm-cancellation`** `[Cancellation]`: "Let f: X → Y be a function. (a) f is injective if and only if, for every set W and all functions g, h: W → X, f∘g = f∘h implies g = h. (b) f is surjective if and only if, for every set Z and all functions g, h: Y → Z, g∘f = h∘f implies g = h." One sentence after the statement: "Injections cancel on the left, surjections on the right."
   - **`[Scratch work]`** (≤ 20 lines). The two (⇒) directions unwind @prp-function-equality and are routine. The (⇐) directions are the content: the hypothesis speaks of functions, the goal of points. For (a), a point of X **is** a function from a one-element set: x gives {0} → X, 0 ↦ x. Table:

     | Givens | Goal |
     |---|---|
     | cancellation on the left; x_1, x_2 ∈ X, f(x_1) = f(x_2) | x_1 = x_2 |
     | the hypothesis needs functions into X | g, h: {0} → X, g(0) ≔ x_1, h(0) ≔ x_2 |
     | (f∘g)(0) = f(x_1) = f(x_2) = (f∘h)(0) | f∘g = f∘h, so g = h: the gap closes at x_1 = g(0) = h(0) = x_2 |

     For (b), argue by contrapositive: if y_0 is missed by f, two functions on Y that differ only at y_0 cannot be told apart after f. Two values suffice: the constant 0 and the indicator of { y_0 } (@exm-first-functions (d)).
   - **Proof** (~230 words). (a) (⇒) Let f∘g = f∘h and w ∈ W. Then f(g(w)) = f(h(w)), so g(w) = h(w) by injectivity; g and h have the same domain and codomain, so g = h (@prp-function-equality). (⇐) As in the scratch work: g, h: {0} → X are functions (one input, one value each); f∘g and f∘h agree at the only point 0, so they are equal, hence g = h, and x_1 = g(0) = h(0) = x_2. (b) (⇒) Let g∘f = h∘f and y ∈ Y. By surjectivity y = f(x) for some x ∈ X [∃E], and g(y) = g(f(x)) = h(f(x)) = h(y); so g = h. (⇐) It suffices to prove the contrapositive; the step back to (⇐) is @fd1:prp-contrapositive-converse, which uses RAA. Suppose f is not surjective; negating the bounded quantifiers of @def-surjective (@fd1:prp-bounded-quantifier-negation, classical), there is y_0 ∈ Y that is not a value. Let Z ≔ {0, 1}, g: Y → Z the constant 0, and h ≔ 1_{{y_0}}: Y → Z. For x ∈ X, f(x) ≠ y_0, so h(f(x)) = 0 = g(f(x)): g∘f = h∘f. But h(y_0) = 1 ≠ 0 = g(y_0), so g ≠ h.

7. **`exm-method-cancel`** `[Method: cancel an injection or a surjection]`.
   - **Template.** To prove g = h, find a function f with f∘g = f∘h and f injective, or with g∘f = h∘f and f surjective. Compute the two composites, often by @prp-composition-associative; prove the injectivity or surjectivity of f; cancel (@thm-cancellation).
   - **When to use it.** When g and h are known through equations rather than formulas, or when the composites are easier to compute than g and h.
   - **The usual failure.** Cancelling a non-injective f on the left (the squaring example above); and cancelling on the wrong side. An injection does not cancel on the right: f: {0} → {0, 1}, f(0) = 0, and g = id_{{0, 1}}, h the constant 0, give g∘f = h∘f with g ≠ h.
8. **`exm-cancel-parity-map`** `[Cancelling the parity map]` (~200 words; S2 replaces the S1 example on one-sided inverses, which pre-empted 2.4 C1). "Let g, h: ℤ → ℤ. (a) Suppose g(2q + r) = h(2q + r) for all q ∈ ℤ and r ∈ {0, 1}. Show g = h by cancellation. (b) Suppose only g(2q) = h(2q) for all q ∈ ℤ. Must g = h?" *Solution:* (a) With φ of @exm-maps-from-algebra (c), the hypothesis says (g∘φ)(q, r) = (h∘φ)(q, r) at every point of ℤ × {0, 1}, so g∘φ = h∘φ (@prp-function-equality). φ is surjective (@exm-maps-from-algebra (c)); cancel it on the right (@thm-cancellation (b)): g = h. (b) No. Now the hypothesis is g∘ψ = h∘ψ with ψ(q) = 2q, and ψ is not surjective: 1 = 2q would make 1 even, and 1 is odd (@thm-even-or-odd, not both).
