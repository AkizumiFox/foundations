6. **`thm-cancellation`** `[Cancellation]`: "Let f: X → Y be a function. (a) f is injective if and only if, for every set W and all functions g, h: W → X, f∘g = f∘h implies g = h. (b) f is surjective if and only if, for every set Z and all functions g, h: Y → Z, g∘f = h∘f implies g = h." One sentence after the statement: "Injections cancel on the left, surjections on the right."
   - **`[Scratch work]`** (≤ 20 lines). The two (⇒) directions unwind @prp-function-equality and are routine. The (⇐) directions are the content: the hypothesis speaks of functions, the goal of points. For (a), a point of X **is** a function from a one-element set: x gives {0} → X, 0 ↦ x. Table:

     | Givens | Goal |
     |---|---|
     | cancellation on the left; x_1, x_2 ∈ X, f(x_1) = f(x_2) | x_1 = x_2 |
     | the hypothesis needs functions into X | g, h: {0} → X, g(0) ≔ x_1, h(0) ≔ x_2 |
     | (f∘g)(0) = f(x_1) = f(x_2) = (f∘h)(0) | f∘g = f∘h, so g = h: the gap closes at x_1 = g(0) = h(0) = x_2 |

     For (b), argue by contrapositive: if y_0 is missed by f, two functions on Y that differ only at y_0 cannot be told apart after f. Two values suffice: the constant 0 and the indicator of { y_0 } (@exm-first-functions (d)).
   - **Proof** (~230 words). (a) (⇒) Let f∘g = f∘h and w ∈ W. Then f(g(w)) = f(h(w)), so g(w) = h(w) by injectivity; g and h have the same domain and codomain, so g = h (@prp-function-equality). (⇐) As in the scratch work: g, h: {0} → X are functions (one input, one value each); f∘g and f∘h agree at the only point 0, so they are equal, hence g = h, and x_1 = g(0) = h(0) = x_2. (b) (⇒) Let g∘f = h∘f and y ∈ Y. By surjectivity y = f(x) for some x ∈ X [∃E], and g(y) = g(f(x)) = h(f(x)) = h(y); so g = h. (⇐) We prove the contrapositive (@fd1:prp-contrapositive; the way back is @fd1:prp-contrapositive-converse). Suppose f is not surjective, and let y_0 ∈ Y be a point that is not a value. Let Z ≔ {0, 1}, g: Y → Z the constant 0, and h ≔ 1_{{y_0}}: Y → Z. For x ∈ X, f(x) ≠ y_0, so h(f(x)) = 0 = g(f(x)): g∘f = h∘f. But h(y_0) = 1 ≠ 0 = g(y_0), so g ≠ h.
   - One sentence after the proof: "The proofs needed only W = {0} and Z = {0, 1}."
7. **`exm-method-cancel`** `[Method: cancel an injection or a surjection]`.
   - **Template.** To prove g = h, find a function f with f∘g = f∘h and f injective, or with g∘f = h∘f and f surjective. Compute the two composites, often by @prp-composition-associative; prove the injectivity or surjectivity of f; cancel (@thm-cancellation).
   - **When to use it.** When g and h are known through equations rather than formulas, as inverses are (Section 2.4); or when the composites are easier to compute than g and h.
   - **The usual failure.** Cancelling a non-injective f on the left (the squaring example above); and cancelling on the wrong side. An injection does not cancel on the right: f: {0} → {0, 1}, f(0) = 0, and g = id_{{0, 1}}, h the constant 0, give g∘f = h∘f with g ≠ h.
8. **`exm-cancel-one-sided-inverses`** `[Cancelling to finish an inverse]` (~200 words). "Let f: X → Y and g: Y → X. (a) If f∘g = id_Y and f is injective, then g∘f = id_X. (b) If g∘f = id_X and f is surjective, then f∘g = id_Y." *Solution:* (a) f∘(g∘f) = (f∘g)∘f = id_Y∘f = f = f∘id_X (@prp-composition-associative, both statements); cancel the injection f on the left: g∘f = id_X. (b) (f∘g)∘f = f∘(g∘f) = f∘id_X = f = id_Y∘f; cancel the surjection f on the right: f∘g = id_Y. Aftermath (one sentence): "Neither g nor f∘g was computed at a single point; the equations did the work, which is the box's 'when to use it'."

10. **`## Structure maps`** (new). Lead (~30 words): "Every subset, product and power set comes with maps of its own. They are the test cases of the two questions."
   - **`exm-structure-maps`** `[The maps that come with sets]`. "Let X, Y be sets and A ⊆ X. Decide injectivity and surjectivity of: (a) the inclusion map A → X, a ↦ a; (b) the projection p_1: X × Y → X, p_1(x, y) = x; (c) the diagonal map δ_X: X → X × X, δ_X(x) = (x, x); (d) the complement map c: P(X) → P(X), c(A) = X ∖ A."
