### 4.1 Section 3.6: Cardinal Arithmetic (`06-cardinal-arithmetic.md`)

**Budget:** main line ≤ 5,000 (expected ~4,880, §5; the S1 figure ~4,800 was a mis-addition of 5,800). One fold, F3.6-a (~300 words, the routine parts of `thm-cardinal-laws`). Six Quick checks, two warnings, one remark (the notation of other books), one `[Looking ahead]` remark.

**Opening and `## The plane and the line` (hook, ~200).**
1. Opening paragraph (~70): Section 2.5 counted finite sets, and Section 2.3 proved the laws of powers \( m^{n+k} = m^n m^k \) and \( (m^n)^k = m^{nk} \) by induction (@prp-power-laws). For finite sets these count functions: \( Y^X \) has \( m^n \) elements when \( Y \) has \( m \) and \( X \) has \( n \) (Section 1.5, before @exm-function-set-small). What is left of that arithmetic when the sets are infinite, where there are only bijections?
2. The hook (~130). "A square has two dimensions and a segment one. Surely the plane \( \nR \times \nR \) has more points than the line?" The injection \( x \mapsto (x, 0) \) gives \( \card{\nR} \le \card{\nR \times \nR} \). Second question: is \( \nN^{\nN} \), the sequences of natural numbers, bigger than \( \nR \)? A real number in \( [0, 1) \) is only a sequence of digits below 10, so \( \nN^{\nN} \) looks bigger. "Guess both answers before reading on." Then: "The plane is a product, \( \nN^{\nN} \) is a power, and the line turns out to be a power too: of \( 2 \)." No answer here.

**`## Adding sizes: disjoint unions` (~540).**
1. **Naive attempt** (~90): for finite disjoint sets, Section 2.5 added sizes by taking a union (@prp-disjoint-union-count). So define the sum as the union? With \( A = B = \{0\} \) we want two elements, and \( A \cup B = \{0\} \) has one. The cure: tag every element with the name of its set, so the copies cannot overlap.
2. *One-line summary:* "*A disjoint union keeps a labelled copy of each set, so nothing is counted twice.*"
3. **`def-disjoint-union`** `[Disjoint Union]`: "Let \( A \) and \( B \) be sets. Their **disjoint union** is
   \[ A \sqcup B \coloneqq (A \times \{0\}) \cup (B \times \{1\}) . \]
   The maps \( \iota_A \colon A \to A \sqcup B \), \( \iota_A(a) \coloneqq (a, 0) \), and \( \iota_B \colon B \to A \sqcup B \), \( \iota_B(b) \coloneqq (b, 1) \), are the **inclusions**."
4. Unwinding (~110), stated and proved inline: every element of \( A \sqcup B \) is \( (a, 0) \) with \( a \in A \) or \( (b, 1) \) with \( b \in B \), and not both, since \( (a, 0) = (b, 1) \) would give \( 0 = 1 \) (@def-ordered-pair). The inclusions are injective by the same property, with disjoint images covering \( A \sqcup B \). So a function on \( A \sqcup B \) may be defined by its values on the two tagged parts separately: the "two cases that cannot overlap" of the proof of @prp-union-two-countable.
5. **`exm-two-copies-of-naturals`** `[Two copies of ℕ]` (~120): "Show that \( \card{\nN \sqcup \nN} = \card{\nN} \)." *Solution:* this is the merging of two lists in the proof of @prp-union-two-countable, now as a bijection. \( e(n, 0) \coloneqq 2n \), \( e(n, 1) \coloneqq 2n + 1 \). The decoder \( d(m) \coloneqq (q, r) \), where \( m = 2q + r \) with \( q \in \nN \), \( r \in \{0, 1\} \), unique by the claim in that proof. Then \( d(e(n, r)) = (n, r) \) by the uniqueness, and \( e(d(m)) = 2q + r = m \); cite @thm-bijection-iff-inverse. One sentence: the first encoding of the section; the box comes with the next one.
6. **Quick check 1** (weird examples): "(a) With \( a \ne b \), list the elements of \( \{a, b\} \sqcup \{a\} \) and of \( \{a, b\} \cup \{a\} \). (b) What is \( \emptyset \sqcup B \)? (c) If \( A \cap B = \emptyset \), give a bijection \( A \cup B \to A \sqcup B \)." *Solution:* (a) \( (a, 0), (b, 0), (a, 1) \): three elements, against the two of \( \{a, b\} \). (b) \( B \times \{1\} \), and \( b \mapsto (b, 1) \) is a bijection \( B \to \emptyset \sqcup B \). (c) \( x \mapsto (x, 0) \) if \( x \in A \), \( x \mapsto (x, 1) \) if \( x \in B \); the cases do not overlap since \( A \cap B = \emptyset \); inverse \( (a, 0) \mapsto a \), \( (b, 1) \mapsto b \).
7. **Warning** `**No subtraction.**` (~40): "For numbers, \( m + k = n + k \) gives \( m = n \). For sets, \( \card{\{0\} \sqcup \nN} = \card{\emptyset \sqcup \nN} \) although \( \card{\{0\}} \ne \card{\emptyset} \) (Exercise B4 (a)). Every law below is proved, never borrowed from numbers." No @exm-hilbert-hotel citation; the maps are in B4 (a)'s solution.

**`## Multiplying, raising to powers, and comparing` (~600).**
1. Lead (~70): products \( A \times B \) (@def-cartesian-product) and sets of functions \( Y^X \) (Section 1.5) are the other two operations. For finite sets they have \( mn \) and \( m^n \) elements (Section 2.5); this is prose, not a citation of `@exr-finite-sets-c2` in a proof. The base is written on the line, \( Y \), and the exponent is the domain, \( X \).
2. **`prp-card-operations-well-defined`** `[Sums, Products and Powers Respect Size]`: "Let \( A, A', B, B' \) be sets with \( \card{A} = \card{A'} \) and \( \card{B} = \card{B'} \). Then
   (a) \( \card{A \sqcup B} = \card{A' \sqcup B'} \); (b) \( \card{A \times B} = \card{A' \times B'} \); (c) \( \card{A^B} = \card{A'^{B'}} \)."
   *Proof:* let \( \alpha \colon A \to A' \) and \( \beta \colon B \to B' \) be bijections, with inverses \( \alpha^{-1} \), \( \beta^{-1} \) (@thm-bijection-iff-inverse). (a) \( (a, 0) \mapsto (\alpha(a), 0) \) and \( (b, 1) \mapsto (\beta(b), 1) \); the same with \( \alpha^{-1}, \beta^{-1} \) is a two-sided inverse. (b) \( (a, b) \mapsto (\alpha(a), \beta(b)) \), with inverse \( (a', b') \mapsto (\alpha^{-1}(a'), \beta^{-1}(b')) \). (c) \( f \mapsto \alpha \circ f \circ \beta^{-1} \), a function \( B' \to A' \); the inverse is \( g \mapsto \alpha^{-1} \circ g \circ \beta \). The round trips follow from @prp-composition-associative and \( \alpha^{-1} \circ \alpha = \id_A \), and so on. The map in (a) is defined by the two-part rule of the unwinding.
3. **Remark** (backward only, ~50): "The proposition lets us replace either set by an equinumerous one without changing the size of the result. An answer computed from representatives must not depend on them: compare 'send \( p/q \) to \( p \)' in Section 1.5 (@exm-not-a-function), which fails exactly this test." (The forward sentence naming Section 4.2 is in the Looking-ahead remark at the end of the main line.)
4. **`prp-card-operations-monotone`** `[Monotonicity]`: "Let \( A, A', B, B' \) be sets with \( \card{A} \le \card{A'} \) and \( \card{B} \le \card{B'} \). Then
   (a) \( \card{A \sqcup B} \le \card{A' \sqcup B'} \); (b) \( \card{A \times B} \le \card{A' \times B'} \); (c) if \( A' \ne \emptyset \), then \( \card{A^B} \le \card{A'^{B'}} \)."
   *Idea* (two lines): carry the injections across, as in the previous proof; for powers, a function on \( B \) must be extended to all of \( B' \), and the extension needs a value to put on the points that \( B \) does not reach.
   *Proof:* let \( u \colon A \to A' \) and \( v \colon B \to B' \) be injections. (a), (b): the maps of the previous proof with \( u, v \) in place of \( \alpha, \beta \) are injective, by @def-ordered-pair. (c) Fix \( a_0 \in A' \). For \( f \in A^B \), define \( F(f) \colon B' \to A' \) by \( F(f)(b') \coloneqq u(f(b)) \) if \( b' = v(b) \) for some \( b \in B \), and \( F(f)(b') \coloneqq a_0 \) if \( b' \notin v(B) \). In the first case \( b \) is unique, since \( v \) is injective, so \( F(f) \) is a function. If \( F(f) = F(g) \), then for every \( b \in B \), \( u(f(b)) = F(f)(v(b)) = F(g)(v(b)) = u(g(b)) \), so \( f(b) = g(b) \), since \( u \) is injective. Hence \( f = g \) (@prp-function-equality).
5. **Non-example by minimal change** (~80): drop "\( A' \ne \emptyset \)" from (c). Take \( A = A' = \emptyset \), \( B = \emptyset \) and \( B' = \{0\} \). Then \( \card{B} \le \card{B'} \), but \( \emptyset^{\emptyset} \) has one element (the empty function, @prp-empty-function) and \( \emptyset^{\{0\}} = \emptyset \), so there is no injection from the first into the second. "The proof used \( a_0 \) exactly once, and here there is no \( a_0 \)." (If \( A' = \emptyset \), then \( A = \emptyset \), since an injection into \( \emptyset \) has empty domain.)
6. **Quick check 2:** "Where does the proof of (c) use that \( v \) is injective? Where does it use that \( u \) is injective?" *Solution:* \( v \) is used to make \( F(f) \) a function: the point \( b \) with \( v(b) = b' \) must be unique. \( u \) is used to make \( F \) injective: \( u(f(b)) = u(g(b)) \) must give \( f(b) = g(b) \).

**`## Encoding: the diagonals of ℕ × ℕ` (~740; method section 1).**
1. Lead (~70): Section 3.1 showed \( \nN \times \nN \) countable by an injection, and at the scratch work for @thm-product-countable set aside the list along the diagonals, because "writing a formula for the position of \( (m, n) \) and checking that every position is used takes real work". Section 3.4's Quick check got \( \card{\nN \times \nN} = \card{\nN} \) from Schröder–Bernstein without ever seeing a bijection. Here is the work, done.
2. Notation, inline: for \( s \in \nN \), the **triangular number** \( \tau_s \coloneqq \sum_{i=1}^{s} i \) (@def-sum-product; \( \tau_0 = 0 \)), so \( \tau_{s+1} = \tau_s + s + 1 \). It counts the points on the diagonals \( m + n = 0, \dots, s - 1 \).
3. **`exm-pairing-bijection`** `[The diagonal pairing of ℕ × ℕ with ℕ]`: "Show that \( \pi(m, n) \coloneqq \tau_{m+n} + n \) is a bijection \( \nN \times \nN \to \nN \)."
   - **`[Scratch work]`** (≤ 20 lines). What must the code record? Which diagonal the point is on, \( s = m + n \), and its place on that diagonal, \( n \in \{0, \dots, s\} \); from \( s \) and \( n \), \( m = s - n \) is read back. The diagonals before diagonal \( s \) hold \( 1 + 2 + \dots + s = \tau_s \) points, so the code counts them and adds the place. Small values: diagonal 0 gives 0; diagonal 1 gives 1, 2; diagonal 2 gives 3, 4, 5. The decoder must find, for each \( k \), the diagonal it falls on.

     | Givens | Goal |
     |---|---|
     | \( (m, n) \in \nN \times \nN \) | \( \pi(m, n) \in \nN \) |
     | \( k \in \nN \) | exactly one \( (m, n) \) with \( \tau_{m+n} + n = k \) |
     | gap | every \( k \in \nN \) is \( \tau_s + n \) with \( s, n \in \nN \), \( n \le s \), in exactly one way |

     "Existence by induction on \( k \): stepping from \( k \) to \( k + 1 \) moves one place along the diagonal, or, at its end, to the start of the next one. Uniqueness: the diagonal \( s \) fills the block \( \tau_s, \dots, \tau_s + s \), just below \( \tau_{s+1} \), so different diagonals give different numbers."
   - *Solution.* *Claim.* For every \( k \in \nN \) there are \( s, n \in \nN \) with \( n \le s \) and \( k = \tau_s + n \), and the pair \( (s, n) \) is unique. *Existence*, by @thm-induction from 0. \( 0 = \tau_0 + 0 \). If \( k = \tau_s + n \) with \( n \le s \): when \( n < s \), \( k + 1 = \tau_s + (n + 1) \) with \( n + 1 \le s \); when \( n = s \), \( k + 1 = \tau_s + s + 1 = \tau_{s+1} + 0 \). *Uniqueness.* First, \( s < s' \) gives \( \tau_{s+1} \le \tau_{s'} \), since \( \tau \) increases at each step (\( \tau_{j+1} = \tau_j + j + 1 > \tau_j \)), by induction on \( s' \) (@thm-induction from \( s + 1 \)). Let \( \tau_s + n = \tau_{s'} + n' \) with \( n \le s \), \( n' \le s' \), and suppose \( s \ne s' \); by symmetry \( s < s' \). Then \( \tau_s + n \le \tau_s + s < \tau_{s+1} \le \tau_{s'} \le \tau_{s'} + n' \), a contradiction. So \( s = s' \), and then \( n = n' \). *The bijection.* \( \pi(m, n) \in \nN \) (@lem-naturals-arithmetic). For \( k \in \nN \), write \( k = \tau_s + n \) by the claim and set \( \delta(k) \coloneqq (s - n, n) \), where \( s - n \in \nN \) as \( n \le s \). Then \( \pi(\delta(k)) = \tau_s + n = k \). And \( \delta(\pi(m, n)) = (m, n) \) by the uniqueness half, applied to \( k = \tau_{m+n} + n \) with \( s = m + n \ge n \). By @thm-bijection-iff-inverse, \( \pi \) is a bijection. Table: \( \pi(0, 0) = 0 \), \( \pi(1, 0) = 1 \), \( \pi(0, 1) = 2 \), \( \pi(2, 0) = 3 \), \( \pi(1, 1) = 4 \), \( \pi(0, 2) = 5 \); and \( \delta(11) = (3, 1) \), since \( \tau_4 = 10 \) and \( 11 = \tau_4 + 1 \).
   - Ledger: \( \tau_{s+1} = \tau_s + s + 1 \) from @def-sum-product; the order and cancellation steps are @lem-naturals-arithmetic / school arithmetic of \( \nN \).
4. **`exm-method-encode`** `[Method: build a bijection by encoding and decoding]`.
   - **Template.** (1) Say what an element of the source must record for nothing to be lost. (2) Choose a code: a rule sending each element to an element of the target, and check that it lands there. (3) Write the decoder: a rule reading an element of the source back from each element of the target, and check that it is a function, with one answer for each input. (4) Check both round trips and cite @thm-bijection-iff-inverse.
   - **When to use it.** The two sets carry the same information arranged differently: two numbers in one, a pair of sequences in one sequence, a function of two variables as a function to functions.
   - **The usual failure.** Checking only one round trip: \( \delta \circ \pi = \id \) alone shows only that \( \pi \) is injective (@thm-left-inverse-iff-injective). And a decoder that is not a function, with two readings of one code or none (`exm-interleaving-decimals`). If the decoder works only on part of the target, keep the injection and use @exm-method-injections-both-ways.
5. **Quick check 3:** "Compute \( \pi(3, 2) \) and \( \delta(47) \)." *Solution:* \( \pi(3, 2) = \tau_5 + 2 = 15 + 2 = 17 \). \( \tau_9 = 45 \le 47 < 55 = \tau_{10} \), so \( 47 = \tau_9 + 2 \) and \( \delta(47) = (7, 2) \); check: \( \pi(7, 2) = \tau_9 + 2 = 47 \).

**`## The laws of exponents, for all sets` (~800; method section 2).**
1. Lead (~50): for numbers, \( m^{n+k} = m^n m^k \) and \( (m^n)^k = m^{nk} \). Read through the sets these numbers count, each law says that two sets of functions are the same size; for sets we must find the bijection.
2. **`thm-cardinal-laws`** `[Laws of Cardinal Arithmetic]`: "For all sets \( A, B, C, X, Y, Z \):
   (a) \( \card{A \sqcup B} = \card{B \sqcup A} \), \( \card{(A \sqcup B) \sqcup C} = \card{A \sqcup (B \sqcup C)} \), \( \card{A \times B} = \card{B \times A} \) and \( \card{(A \times B) \times C} = \card{A \times (B \times C)} \);
   (b) \( \card{A \times (B \sqcup C)} = \card{(A \times B) \sqcup (A \times C)} \);
   (c) \( X^{\emptyset} \) has exactly one element, and \( \card{X^{\{0\}}} = \card{X} \);
   (d) \( \card{X^{Y \sqcup Z}} = \card{X^Y \times X^Z} \);
   (e) \( \card{(X \times Y)^Z} = \card{X^Z \times Y^Z} \);
   (f) \( \card{(X^Y)^Z} = \card{X^{Y \times Z}} \)."
3. **`[Scratch work]`** for (d) (≤ 20 lines). What is an element of each side? On the left, a function \( h \) defined on the tagged copies of \( Y \) and \( Z \). On the right, a pair \( (f, g) \) with \( f \colon Y \to X \) and \( g \colon Z \to X \). A function on two disjoint pieces is the same as a function on each piece. So cut \( h \) into its two pieces by composing with the inclusions, and glue a pair back together piece by piece.

   | Givens | Goal |
   |---|---|
   | \( h \in X^{Y \sqcup Z} \) | an element of \( X^Y \times X^Z \): \( (h \circ \iota_Y, h \circ \iota_Z) \) |
   | \( (f, g) \in X^Y \times X^Z \) | one \( h \) with \( h \circ \iota_Y = f \), \( h \circ \iota_Z = g \) |
   | gap | \( h(y, 0) \coloneqq f(y) \), \( h(z, 1) \coloneqq g(z) \) is a function: the parts do not overlap (`def-disjoint-union`, unwinding) |

4. **Proof** of (d) and (f) on the main line, (a)–(c) and (e) in the fold.
   - (d) \( \Phi(h) \coloneqq (h \circ \iota_Y, h \circ \iota_Z) \). \( \Psi(f, g) \coloneqq h \), where \( h(y, 0) = f(y) \) and \( h(z, 1) = g(z) \). This is a function on \( Y \sqcup Z \) because every element has exactly one of the two forms. \( \Phi(\Psi(f, g)) = (f, g) \): \( h(\iota_Y(y)) = h(y, 0) = f(y) \), and likewise for \( g \), by @prp-function-equality and @def-ordered-pair. \( \Psi(\Phi(h)) = h \): both agree at every \( (y, 0) \) and every \( (z, 1) \), which are all the points. By @thm-bijection-iff-inverse, \( \Phi \) is a bijection.
   - (f) For \( g \in (X^Y)^Z \), each \( g(z) \) is a function \( Y \to X \); let \( \Phi(g)(y, z) \coloneqq g(z)(y) \), a function \( Y \times Z \to X \). For \( h \in X^{Y \times Z} \) let \( \Psi(h)(z) \coloneqq (y \mapsto h(y, z)) \), an element of \( X^Y \) for each \( z \), so \( \Psi(h) \in (X^Y)^Z \). \( \Phi(\Psi(h))(y, z) = \Psi(h)(z)(y) = h(y, z) \). And \( \Psi(\Phi(g))(z) \) is the function \( y \mapsto g(z)(y) \), which is \( g(z) \) (@prp-function-equality, used twice: first in \( Y \), then in \( Z \)). By @thm-bijection-iff-inverse, \( \Phi \) is a bijection.
   - **Fold F3.6-a** (`::: {.second-reading title="The other laws"}`, ~300; holds no cited statement): the maps, with the inverse of each. (a) \( (a, 0) \mapsto (a, 1) \), \( (b, 1) \mapsto (b, 0) \); \( ((a, 0), 0) \mapsto (a, 0) \), \( ((b, 1), 0) \mapsto ((b, 0), 1) \), \( (c, 1) \mapsto ((c, 1), 1) \); \( (a, b) \mapsto (b, a) \); \( ((a, b), c) \mapsto (a, (b, c)) \). (b) \( (a, (b, 0)) \mapsto ((a, b), 0) \), \( (a, (c, 1)) \mapsto ((a, c), 1) \). (c) \( X^{\emptyset} = \{\text{the empty function}\} \) by @prp-empty-function; \( f \mapsto f(0) \) is a bijection \( X^{\{0\}} \to X \), with inverse \( x \mapsto \) the constant function \( 0 \mapsto x \). (e) \( h \mapsto (p_1 \circ h, p_2 \circ h) \), with \( p_1(x, y) = x \), \( p_2(x, y) = y \); inverse \( (f, g) \mapsto (z \mapsto (f(z), g(z))) \). Each round trip is a line of @prp-function-equality and @def-ordered-pair. (No exercise asks for these again; B2 asks for laws the text does not print.)
5. Aftermath (~70): (f) is called **currying**: a function of two variables becomes a function whose values are functions. For finite sets with \( m, n, k \) elements the theorem gives back \( m^{n+k} = m^n m^k \) and \( (m^n)^k = m^{nk} \) without induction; the induction of 2.3 has moved into the counts of Section 2.5. Nothing in the proof used finiteness.
6. **`exm-method-laws-by-bijection`** `[Method: prove a law of sizes by a bijection of sets]`.
   - **Template.** (1) Write out what an element of each side is: a tagged element, a pair, a function, a function with function values. (2) Find the map that only regroups: compose with the inclusions, pair the coordinates, or curry. (3) Write the reverse regrouping. (4) Check both round trips with @prp-function-equality and @def-ordered-pair, and cite @thm-bijection-iff-inverse.
   - **When to use it.** A law familiar from numbers, such as \( m^{n+k} = m^n m^k \), is claimed for sets of any size; or a size is to be computed by rewriting the set step by step.
   - **The usual failure.** Computing with the symbols, as in "\( \card{X}^{\card{Y} + \card{Z}} = \card{X}^{\card{Y}} \card{X}^{\card{Z}} \)", when no number \( \card{X} \) exists and the step is the law itself (N9). Also dropping the tags: with \( \cup \) in place of \( \sqcup \) the law fails (B4 (b)).
7. **Quick check 4:** "Let \( X = \{0, 1\} \), \( Y = \{a\} \), \( Z = \{b, c\} \). How many elements has each side of (d)? Let \( h(a, 0) = 1 \), \( h(b, 1) = 0 \), \( h(c, 1) = 1 \). What is \( \Phi(h) \)?" *Solution:* \( Y \sqcup Z \) has 3 elements, so \( 2^3 = 8 \); \( X^Y \times X^Z \) has \( 2 \cdot 4 = 8 \). \( \Phi(h) = (f, g) \) with \( f(a) = 1 \), \( g(b) = 0 \), \( g(c) = 1 \).

**`## Subsets are 0–1 functions` (~400).**
1. Lead (~50): "Section 1.5 stated, for every set, that subsets and 0–1 functions match, and gave both maps: send \( f \) to \( \{ x : f(x) = 1 \} \) and \( A \) to \( \ind_A \). Here are the two round trips, which make \( 2 \) a base like any other."
2. **`thm-power-set-two-power`** `[Subsets and 0–1 Functions]`: "For every set \( X \), the map \( A \mapsto \ind_A \) is a bijection \( \powerset{X} \to \{0, 1\}^X \), with inverse \( f \mapsto \{ x \in X : f(x) = 1 \} \). In particular \( \card{\powerset{X}} = \card{\{0, 1\}^X} \)."
   No Idea line (the maps are 1.5's; only the round trips are new).
   *Proof:* \( \ind_A \in \{0, 1\}^X \) (05:61). For \( A \subseteq X \), \( \{ x : \ind_A(x) = 1 \} = A \) by the definition of \( \ind_A \). For \( f \in \{0, 1\}^X \), with \( S \coloneqq \{ x : f(x) = 1 \} \), we get \( \ind_S(x) = 1 = f(x) \) for \( x \in S \), and for \( x \notin S \), \( f(x) \ne 1 \), so \( f(x) = 0 = \ind_S(x) \). So \( \ind_S = f \) (@prp-function-equality). Cite @thm-bijection-iff-inverse.
   Aftermath, one sentence: the earlier uses in 2.5 (after @prp-power-set-count), 3.3 (after @cor-powerset-naturals-uncountable) and 3.4 (Quick check after @prp-card-le-surjection) quoted this correspondence; it is now a theorem to cite.
3. **`cor-cantor-two-power`** `[No Set Is the Size of Its 0–1 Functions]`: "For every set \( X \), \( \card{X} < \card{\{0, 1\}^X} \)." *Proof:* \( x \mapsto \ind_{\{x\}} \) is injective, since \( \ind_{\{x\}} \) takes the value 1 at \( x \) only. A bijection \( X \to \{0, 1\}^X \), composed with the inverse of the bijection of @thm-power-set-two-power, would be a bijection \( X \to \powerset{X} \), and in particular a surjection, against @thm-cantor.
4. Aftermath (one sentence): so \( \nN \), \( \{0,1\}^{\nN} \), \( \{0,1\}^{\{0,1\}^{\nN}} \), … grow strictly at each step, and there is no largest size.

**`## The size of the line` (~680).**
1. Lead (~50): the hook asked about \( \nR \times \nR \); first \( \nR \) itself. A real number in \( [0, 1) \) is a digit sequence, a function \( \nZ_{>0} \to \{0, \dots, 9\} \). So \( \nR \) should be a power, and that power should be a power of 2.
2. **`thm-reals-power-set-naturals`** `[The Line Is a Power of Two]`: "\( \card{\nR} = \card{\{0, 1\}^{\nN}} = \card{\powerset{\nN}} \)."
3. *Idea* (two lines): injections both ways (@exm-method-injections-both-ways). Into the 0–1 sequences the digits are encoded and the laws do the bookkeeping; out of them, a 0–1 sequence is read as decimal digits 0 and 1, which contain no 9.
4. *Proof.* Let \( T \coloneqq \{0, 1, \dots, 9\} \) and \( D \coloneqq T^{\nZ_{>0}} \), the digit sequences (@def-decimal-expansion).
   - *\( \card{[0, 1)} \le \card{D} \)* (kept short; this is the step of the proof of @thm-reals-uncountable-decimal). Send \( x \) to its expansion not ending in all 9s: one exists by (D1) and only one by (D3), so the rule is a function (no choice); it is injective by the uniqueness in (D2).
   - *\( \card{D} \le \card{\{0, 1\}^{\nN}} \).* \( n \mapsto n + 1 \) is a bijection \( \nN \to \nZ_{>0} \), so \( \card{D} = \card{T^{\nN}} \) (@prp-card-operations-well-defined (c)). \( t \mapsto \ind_{\{t\}} \) injects \( T \) into \( \{0, 1\}^T \), so \( \card{T^{\nN}} \le \card{(\{0, 1\}^T)^{\nN}} \) (@prp-card-operations-monotone (c); \( \{0, 1\}^T \ne \emptyset \)), which is \( \card{\{0, 1\}^{T \times \nN}} \) (@thm-cardinal-laws (f)). *Idea* (one line, the faded encode step): a pair \( (t, n) \) is recorded by one number, by the method of @exm-method-encode. The code \( (t, n) \mapsto 10n + t \) is a bijection \( T \times \nN \to \nN \), with decoder quotient and remainder by 10 (@thm-division-algorithm, the quotient in \( \nN \) as in the claim in the proof of @prp-union-two-countable). So \( \card{\{0, 1\}^{T \times \nN}} = \card{\{0, 1\}^{\nN}} \) (@prp-card-operations-well-defined (c)). Composing gives an injection \( D \to \{0, 1\}^{\nN} \) (@thm-composition-injective-surjective (a)).
   - *\( \card{\{0, 1\}^{\nN}} \le \card{[0, 1)} \).* For \( b \in \{0, 1\}^{\nN} \), let \( e_{k+1} \coloneqq b(k) \) for \( k \in \nN \): a digit sequence with digits 0 and 1 only. By (D2) it is the expansion of exactly one \( y_b \in [0, 1] \), and \( y_b \le t_1 + \frac{1}{10} \le \frac{2}{10} \), so \( y_b \in [0, 1) \). If \( b \ne b' \), the two digit sequences differ; if \( y_b = y_{b'} \), they would be two different expansions of one number, and by (D3) one of them would end in all 9s, but neither contains a 9. So \( y_b \ne y_{b'} \).
   - *Conclusion.* By @prp-card-le-preorder (b) and @thm-schroder-bernstein, \( \card{[0, 1)} = \card{\{0, 1\}^{\nN}} \). By @exm-reals-open-interval, \( \card{[0, 1)} = \card{\nR} \). By @thm-power-set-two-power, \( \card{\{0, 1\}^{\nN}} = \card{\powerset{\nN}} \). Chain with @prp-equinumerous-properties.
5. Aftermath (~50, honest): "The first step is the one of Exercise C1 of Section 3.4. What is new is the middle step, where the laws replace C1's hand-built code \( 10k + d_k \), and the last, where digits 0 and 1 suffice because (D3) needs only that no 9 appears. Neither proof needed binary expansions of real numbers."
6. **Remark** `[Notation in other books]` (~100): "Many books treat sizes as objects, called **cardinal numbers**, and write \( \aleph_0 \) for the size of \( \nN \) and \( 2^{\aleph_0} \), or \( \mathfrak c \), for the size of \( \nR \). Making a size into an object needs more set theory than this book uses (Section 2.5, after @prp-equinumerous-properties). Here '\( \card{A} = 2^{\aleph_0} \)' can be read only as shorthand for \( \card{A} = \card{\{0, 1\}^{\nN}} \). Whether some subset of \( \nR \) is strictly bigger than \( \nN \) and strictly smaller than \( \nR \) is the **continuum hypothesis**; it is known to be neither provable nor refutable from the usual axioms of set theory, if those axioms are consistent." (S2a: check the exact place where 2.5 says that no object \( \card{A} \) is defined, NOTATION row `\card{S}`; cite it by label or section.)
7. **Quick check 5:** "Which step of the proof of @thm-reals-power-set-naturals uses the axiom of choice?" *Solution:* None. The expansion of \( x \) is chosen by a rule ("not ending in all 9s"), every other map is a formula, and @thm-schroder-bernstein was proved without choice (Section 3.4).

**`## Back to the plane` (~680).**
1. **Lead and the almost-correct proof** (`exm-interleaving-decimals` `[Interleaving digits]`, ~250). "The natural bijection \( [0, 1) \times [0, 1) \to [0, 1) \) interleaves digits: if \( x = 0.a_1 a_2 \ldots \) and \( y = 0.b_1 b_2 \ldots \), with the expansions not ending in all 9s, let \( F(x, y) \coloneqq 0.a_1 b_1 a_2 b_2 \ldots \); the inverse splits the digits of \( z \)." Is it a bijection? *Solution:* the interleaved sequence \( c \) has infinitely many places that are not 9 (those of \( a \)), so it does not end in all 9s, and by (D2) it names one number \( z \). *\( z < 1 \)*, directly: let \( n \) be the first place with \( c_n \le 8 \) (@thm-well-ordering); then \( t_n \le (1 - 10^{-(n-1)}) + 8 \cdot 10^{-n} = 1 - 2 \cdot 10^{-n} \) (@prp-geometric-sum), so \( z \le t_n + 10^{-n} \le 1 - 10^{-n} < 1 \). (This is the computation of 3.3's Exercise B3 for \( x = 1 \), redone, not cited.) *Injective:* if \( F(x, y) = F(x', y') = z \), both interleaved sequences are expansions of \( z \) and neither ends in all 9s, so by (D3) they are equal; then \( a = a' \), \( b = b' \), and \( x = x' \), \( y = y' \) by (D2). *Not onto:* \( z = \frac{1}{11} = 0.090909\ldots \) has no 0 tail and no 9 tail, so by (D3) this is its only expansion. A preimage would need \( b = 999\ldots \), but \( b \) was the expansion of \( y \) not ending in all 9s. (c) \( E(m, n, k) \coloneqq \pi(\pi(m, n), k) \), with decoder \( k' \mapsto (m, n, k) \), where \( \delta(k') = (j, k) \) and \( \delta(j) = (m, n) \). - **B4** `[B4: Prove or disprove]`. "Prove each true statement and refute each false one with a counterexample. Here \( A, B, C, X, Y, Y', Z \) are sets."
  - (a) If \( \card{A \sqcup C} = \card{B \sqcup C} \), then \( \card{A} = \card{B} \).
  - (b) \( \card{X^{Y \cup Z}} = \card{X^Y \times X^Z} \).

  - (e) If \( \card{A} < \card{B} \) and \( C \ne \emptyset \), then \( \card{A \times C} < \card{B \times C} \).

  - *Solution:* (a) False: \( A = \{0\} \), \( B = \emptyset \), \( C = \nN \) (the warning "No subtraction"). True when \( Y \cap Z = \emptyset \), by Quick check 1 (c) and (d) of the theorem. (c) False: \( X = \emptyset \), \( Y = \emptyset \), \( Y' = \{0\} \) (the non-example after @prp-card-operations-monotone). True when \( X \ne \emptyset \). (d) False. \( f \mapsto (f(0), f(1)) \) is a bijection \( \nN^{\{0,1\}} \to \nN \times \nN \) (inverse: the function \( 0 \mapsto m \), \( 1 \mapsto n \)), and \( \nN \times \nN \) is countable (@thm-product-countable), so \( \nN^{\{0,1\}} \) injects into \( \nN \) (@thm-composition-injective-surjective (a)). Moral: base and exponent do not commute. (e) False: \( A = \{0\} \), \( B = \{0, 1\} \), \( C = \nN \). \( \card{\{0\} \times \nN} = \card{\nN} = \card{\{0, 1\} \times \nN} \), the latter by @exm-two-copies-of-naturals, since \( \{0, 1\} \times \nN \) and \( \nN \sqcup \nN \) are the same size by \( (r, n) \mapsto (n, r) \). (f) False: \( x \mapsto \ind_{\{x\}} \) gives \( \card{X} \le \card{\{0, 1\}^X} \), so \( \card{\{0, 1\}^X} \le \card{X} \) would give equality by @thm-schroder-bernstein, against @cor-cantor-two-power.

- **C2** `[C2: Find the error]` (false conclusion). "Claim: \( \{0, 1\}^{\nN} \) is countable. 'Proof': Send \( b \) to the natural number \( N(b) \coloneqq \sum_k b(k) 2^k \).
