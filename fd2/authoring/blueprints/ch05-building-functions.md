### 4.1 Section 5.1: Restriction, Extension and Gluing

**Opening and hook `## A function in two pieces` (~350).** The proof of `thm-schroder-bernstein` defined \( h \) by one formula on \( C \) and another off \( C \), and said "each \( a \) falls under exactly one case, so \( h \) is a function". The absolute value is written \( x \) for \( x \ge 0 \) and \( -x \) for \( x \le 0 \): here \( 0 \) falls under **both** cases. Is that a function? And the rule "\( x \) for \( x \ge 0 \), \( x + 1 \) for \( x \le 0 \)"? Question for the reader before reading on: what must two formulas satisfy for "one on this piece, one on that" to define a function?

**`## Restriction and extension` (~900).**
1. **`def-restriction-extension`** `[Restriction, Extension, Inclusion]`: For \( f \colon X \to Y \) and \( A \subseteq X \), the restriction \( f|_A \colon A \to Y \), \( f|_A(a) \coloneqq f(a) \) (as in Section 1.5). For \( A \subseteq X \) and \( g \colon A \to Y \), an **extension** of \( g \) to \( X \) is a function \( f \colon X \to Y \) with \( f|_A = g \). The **inclusion map** \( \iota_A \colon A \to X \) is \( a \mapsto a \).
2. **Naive expectation, broken** (~150): an extension is not unique (\( g \colon (0, 1] \to \nR \), \( g(x) = 1/x \), extends to \( [0, 1] \) with any value at \( 0 \)) and need not keep a property: the bijection \( g \colon \{1, 2\} \to \{a, b\} \) has no injective extension \( \{1, 2, 3\} \to \{a, b\} \) (@cor-pigeonhole-finite-sets (a)).
3. **`prp-restriction-properties`** `[Restrictions]`. Let \( f \colon X \to Y \) and \( B \subseteq A \subseteq X \). (a) \( f|_A = f \circ \iota_A \). (b) \( (f|_A)|_B = f|_B \). (c) For \( g \colon Y \to Z \), \( (g \circ f)|_A = g \circ (f|_A) \). (d) If \( f \) is injective, so is \( f|_A \). (e) \( f|_A(B) = f(B) \). *Proof:* each by @prp-function-equality, comparing values; (d) by @def-injective.
4. **Quick check:** "Is a restriction of a surjection surjective?" *Solution:* No. \( f \colon \nR \to [0, \infty) \), \( x \mapsto x^2 \), is surjective; \( f|_{[1, 2]} \colon [1, 2] \to [0, \infty) \) has image \( [1, 4] \). The codomain is kept, so restricting can lose surjectivity; it can gain injectivity (\( f|_{[0,\infty)} \)).

**`## Gluing` (~1,300).**
1. **`thm-gluing`** `[Gluing Functions]`. Let \( (A_i)_{i \in I} \) be a family of subsets of \( X \) with \( \bigcup_{i \in I} A_i = X \), and let \( f_i \colon A_i \to Y \) be functions that **agree on overlaps**: \( f_i(x) = f_j(x) \) for all \( i, j \in I \) and \( x \in A_i \cap A_j \). Then there is exactly one \( f \colon X \to Y \) with \( f|_{A_i} = f_i \) for every \( i \in I \).
2. **`[Scratch work]`** (≤ 20 lines). A function is a graph with (Fn1), (Fn2) and a codomain (@def-function). The only graph in sight is the union of the given graphs \( G_i \).

   | Givens | Goal |
   |---|---|
   | graphs \( G_i \subseteq A_i \times Y \) with (Fn1), (Fn2) on \( A_i \); \( \bigcup A_i = X \); agreement on overlaps | \( f \colon X \to Y \) with \( f|_{A_i} = f_i \), and only one |
   | \( G \coloneqq \bigcup_{i} G_i \subseteq X \times Y \) | (Fn1) and (Fn2) for \( G \) on \( X \) |
   | \( x \in X \), so \( x \in A_i \) for some \( i \) | (Fn1): \( (x, f_i(x)) \in G_i \subseteq G \) |
   | \( (x, y) \in G_i \), \( (x, y') \in G_j \): \( y = f_i(x) \), \( y' = f_j(x) \), \( x \in A_i \cap A_j \) | (Fn2): \( y = y' \), and this is the one place agreement is used |

   Uniqueness: two such functions both equal \( f_i(x) \) at \( x \in A_i \).
3. **Proof** forwards (as the table), then restriction: \( f|_{A_i}(x) = f(x) = f_i(x) \) since \( (x, f_i(x)) \in G \). Uniqueness by @prp-function-equality. Aftermath (one sentence): the cover gives (Fn1), agreement gives (Fn2), and dropping either breaks the corresponding clause.
4. **`exm-method-gluing`** `[Method: define a function by gluing]`.
   - **Template.** (1) Name the pieces and check that they cover the domain. (2) For each two pieces, find the overlap and check that the formulas agree on it; disjoint pieces need nothing. (3) Conclude by @thm-gluing. (4) Prove further properties piece by piece **and across pieces**: for injectivity, a value from one piece against a value from another.
   - **When to use it.** Definitions by cases, piecewise formulas, a default value off a subset, constructions like Schröder–Bernstein's.
   - **The usual failure.** Checking the cover and forgetting the overlaps (a boundary point lies in two pieces); checking injectivity inside each piece only.
5. **`exm-piecewise-as-gluing`** `[Piecewise definitions are gluings]`. (a) \( \abs{\cdot} \): pieces \( [0, \infty) \), \( (-\infty, 0] \), overlap \( \{0\} \), values \( 0 \) and \( -0 = 0 \): glues. (b) "\( x \) on \( [0, \infty) \), \( x + 1 \) on \( (-\infty, 0] \)": overlap \( \{0\} \), values \( 0 \ne 1 \): no function. (c) Minimal change, \( (-\infty, 0) \): disjoint cover, glues to \( k \colon \nR \to \nR \); but \( k \) is not injective, across pieces: \( k(\tfrac12) = \tfrac12 = k(-\tfrac12) \).
6. **Back to Schröder–Bernstein** (~120): \( C \) and \( A \setminus C \) are a disjoint cover, so the definition of \( h \) is a gluing with nothing to check; Case 3 of its injectivity proof is step (4)'s cross-piece check. **Default values:** for \( g \colon A \to Y \), \( A \subseteq X \) and \( y_0 \in Y \), "\( g \) on \( A \), \( y_0 \) on \( X \setminus A \)" glues over a disjoint cover; the 'Repair' in the solution of Section 3.1's @exr-countable-sets-c2 (default value \( a_0 \)) used exactly this (prose pointer, not a reason).
7. **Quick check:** "Glue \( x^2 \) on \( (-\infty, 1] \) and \( 2x - 1 \) on \( [1, \infty) \). Does it glue? Is the result injective?" *Solution:* both give \( 1 \) at \( 1 \), so it glues; not injective, \( f(-1) = 1 = f(1) \).

**`## Indicator functions` (~1,500).**
1. **`def-indicator`** `[Indicator Function]`: for \( A \subseteq X \), \( \ind_A \colon X \to \{0, 1\} \), \( 1 \) on \( A \) and \( 0 \) on \( X \setminus A \), the function of @exm-first-functions (d), now labelled. It depends on \( X \), fixed throughout. It is a gluing of two constants over a disjoint cover.
2. **Re-reading 3.6** (~100): Section 3.6 proved \( \powerset{X} \approx \{0, 1\}^X \) (@thm-power-set-two-power) by "one on \( A \), zero off \( A \)", that is \( A \mapsto \ind_A \), with inverse \( f \mapsto f^{-1}(\{1\}) \). So \( \ind_A = \ind_B \) exactly when \( A = B \), the fact that lets a computation with indicators prove an equation of sets.
3. **Symmetric difference** (defined inline, bold): \( A \triangle B \coloneqq (A \setminus B) \cup (B \setminus A) \).
4. **`prp-indicator-arithmetic`** `[Set Operations as Arithmetic of Indicators]`. For \( A, B \subseteq X \) and every \( x \in X \), writing \( u = \ind_A(x) \), \( v = \ind_B(x) \): (a) \( \ind_{A \cap B}(x) = uv \); (b) \( \ind_{X \setminus A}(x) = 1 - u \); (c) \( \ind_{A \cup B}(x) = u + v - uv \); (d) \( \ind_{A \triangle B}(x) = u + v - 2uv \); (e) \( A \subseteq B \) iff \( \ind_A(x) \le \ind_B(x) \) for every \( x \). *Proof:* (a), (b), (d) by cases on \( x \in A \), \( x \in B \) (a four-row table); (c) from (a), (b) and De Morgan (@thm-set-algebra): \( 1 - (1 - u)(1 - v) = u + v - uv \); (e) by @def-subset, the only failing pattern being \( u = 1, v = 0 \).
5. **Named move "Compute with indicators"** (one paragraph, not a box): translate both sides to indicators, compute with \( u, v, w \in \{0, 1\} \) (using \( u^2 = u \)), translate back by injectivity of \( A \mapsto \ind_A \).
6. **`exm-symmetric-difference-associative`** `[Symmetric difference is associative]`. Show \( (A \triangle B) \triangle C = A \triangle (B \triangle C) \). *Solution:* with \( w = \ind_C(x) \) and (d) twice, \( \ind_{(A \triangle B) \triangle C}(x) = (u + v - 2uv) + w - 2(u + v - 2uv)w = u + v + w - 2uv - 2uw - 2vw + 4uvw \), symmetric in \( u, v, w \); the right side gives the same expression. Aftermath: the value is \( 1 \) exactly when an odd number of \( u, v, w \) are \( 1 \); element-chasing needs eight cases.
7. **Cantor, re-read** (two sentences, ~50, Russell → Cantor thread): the set \( D \) of @thm-cantor has \( \ind_D(x) = 1 - \ind_{g(x)}(x) \), the flipped diagonal of @thm-binary-sequences-uncountable (3.3), and 3.6's @cor-cantor-two-power already joined the two. Exercise C3 runs the argument for any \( X \) with indicators first.
8. **Quick checks:** (i) "Express \( \ind_{A \setminus B} \) by \( u, v \)." \( u(1 - v) = u - uv \). (ii) "For \( X = \{1, 2, 3, 4\} \), \( A = \{1, 2\} \), \( B = \{2, 3\} \), list the values of \( \ind_{A \triangle B} \)." \( (1, 0, 1, 0) \).

**Exercises (5.1).**
- **A1.** (a) The image of \( f|_{[-1, 2]} \) for \( f(x) = x^2 \). (b) Does \( g \colon \{1, 2\} \to \{a, b\} \), \( 1 \mapsto a \), \( 2 \mapsto b \), have an injective extension to \( \{1, 2, 3\} \) with codomain \( \{a, b\} \)? With codomain \( \{a, b, c\} \)? (c) Show that \( g \colon A \to Y \) has an extension to \( X \supseteq A \) if and only if \( A = X \) or \( Y \ne \emptyset \). (d) For \( X = \{1, 2, 3, 4\} \), write \( \ind_{\{1, 3\}} \) as a list of values, and name the subset whose indicator is \( (0, 1, 1, 0) \). *Solution:* (a) \( [0, 4] \). (b) No (@cor-pigeonhole-finite-sets (a)); yes, \( 3 \mapsto c \). (c) If \( A = X \), \( g \) itself. If \( y_0 \in Y \), glue \( g \) with the constant \( y_0 \) on \( X \setminus A \). Conversely, if \( A \ne X \) and \( Y = \emptyset \), a point of \( X \setminus A \) has no value (@prp-empty-function (b), or (Fn1) directly). (d) \( (1, 0, 1, 0) \); \( \{2, 3\} \).
- **B1** `[B1: Three pieces]`. \( f_1(x) = -x \) on \( (-\infty, -1] \), \( f_2(x) = x^2 \) on \( [-1, 1] \), \( f_3(x) = x \) on \( [1, \infty) \). Show they glue, find \( f(\nR) \) and \( f^{-1}(\{\tfrac14\}) \), and decide injectivity. *Solution:* overlaps \( \{-1\} \) (\( 1 = 1 \)), \( \{1\} \) (\( 1 = 1 \)), \( (-\infty, -1] \cap [1, \infty) = \emptyset \). \( f(\nR) = [1, \infty) \cup [0, 1] \cup [1, \infty) = [0, \infty) \). \( f^{-1}(\{\tfrac14\}) = \{-\tfrac12, \tfrac12\} \) (\( f_1, f_3 \) take values \( \ge 1 \)), so not injective.
- **B2** `[B2: Injective across pieces]`. Let \( f \colon X \to Y \), \( X = A \cup B \). Prove: \( f \) is injective iff \( f|_A \), \( f|_B \) are injective and \( f(A) \cap f(B) = f(A \cap B) \). *Solution:* (⇒) restrictions by `prp-restriction-properties` (d); \( \supseteq \) always (@def-image), \( \subseteq \) is @thm-image-intersection (b), direction (⇐), applied to \( A, B \). (⇐) Let \( f(x) = f(x') \); same piece: done. Say \( x \in A \), \( x' \in B \): \( y = f(x) \in f(A) \cap f(B) = f(A \cap B) \), so \( y = f(z) \), \( z \in A \cap B \); \( f|_A \) injective gives \( x = z \), \( f|_B \) gives \( x' = z \).
- **B3** `[B3: By indicators]`. Prove by computing with indicators: (a) \( A \setminus (B \cup C) = (A \setminus B) \cap (A \setminus C) \); (b) \( A \cap (B \triangle C) = (A \cap B) \triangle (A \cap C) \). *Solution:* (a) \( u(1 - (v + w - vw)) = u(1 - v)(1 - w) \) and \( u(1 - v) \cdot u(1 - w) = u^2(1 - v)(1 - w) \), equal as \( u^2 = u \). (b) \( u(v + w - 2vw) \) against \( uv + uw - 2u^2vw \), equal as \( u^2 = u \).
- **B4** `[B4: Prove or disprove]`. (a) If \( X = A \cup B \) with \( A \cap B = \emptyset \), \( f \colon A \to Y \), \( g \colon B \to Y \), there is exactly one \( h \colon X \to Y \) with \( h|_A = f \), \( h|_B = g \). (b) If \( X = A \cup B \) and \( f|_A \), \( f|_B \) are injective, \( f \colon X \to Y \) is injective. (c) If \( f|_A \colon A \to Y \) is surjective for some \( A \subseteq X \), then \( f \colon X \to Y \) is surjective. (d) \( h(x) = x \) for \( x \in \nQ \), \( h(x) = 2x \) for \( x \in \nR \setminus \nQ \) defines a bijection \( \nR \to \nR \). (e) Gluing \( x \) on \( [0, 1] \) and \( 2 - x \) on \( [1, 2] \) gives an injective function on \( [0, 2] \). *Solution:* (a) True: @thm-gluing, overlaps empty. (b) False: \( x^2 \), \( A = [0, \infty) \), \( B = (-\infty, 0] \), \( f(1) = f(-1) \). (c) True: \( Y = f|_A(A) = f(A) \subseteq f(X) \) (`prp-restriction-properties` (e)). (d) True: disjoint cover, glues. Injective across pieces: \( x \in \nQ \), \( x' \notin \nQ \), \( x = 2x' \) gives \( x' = x/2 \in \nQ \), impossible. Surjective: rational \( y = h(y) \); irrational \( y = h(y/2) \), \( y/2 \notin \nQ \) (else \( y = 2 \cdot (y/2) \in \nQ \)). Closure of \( \nQ \) under \( \cdot \) is a school fact (ledger). (e) False: it glues (\( 1 = 1 \)) but \( f(\tfrac12) = \tfrac12 = f(\tfrac32) \).
- **C1** `[C1: Counting extensions, used later]`. Let \( A \subseteq X \) and \( g \colon A \to Y \). Show that \( f \mapsto f|_{X \setminus A} \) is a bijection from the set of extensions of \( g \) to \( Y^{X \setminus A} \). *Solution:* inverse \( h \mapsto \) the gluing of \( g \) and \( h \) (disjoint cover). Round trips: the gluing restricts to \( h \) on \( X \setminus A \); an extension \( f \) and the gluing of \( g \) and \( f|_{X \setminus A} \) agree on both pieces, hence are equal by the uniqueness in @thm-gluing. (Used by the Same size thread in the index.)
- **C2** `[C2: Find the error]`. A proof of gluing reads: "Given \( x \in X \), pick \( i \) with \( x \in A_i \) and set \( f(x) \coloneqq f_i(x) \). Then \( f|_{A_i} = f_i \) for every \( i \)." Which hypothesis is never used? Where does the proof fail, and what check is missing? *Solution:* agreement on overlaps is unused. The rule reads a representative \( i \) of \( \{ i : x \in A_i \} \), and is a function only if the value does not depend on that choice: the check of @exm-method-well-definedness. Without it, \( f|_{A_j} = f_j \) fails at an \( x \in A_i \cap A_j \) where \( i \) was picked; @exm-piecewise-as-gluing (b) at \( x = 0 \) is a case. Marking: 1 for the unused hypothesis, 1 for the dependence on the choice, 1 for the counterexample.
- **C3** `[C3: Cantor through indicators]`. Let \( g \colon X \to \{0, 1\}^X \). Show that \( d(x) \coloneqq 1 - g(x)(x) \) is not a value of \( g \). Deduce @thm-cantor (use \( g(x) \coloneqq \ind_{f(x)} \)). *Solution:* if \( d = g(a) \), then \( d(a) = g(a)(a) \) and \( d(a) = 1 - g(a)(a) \), so \( 2g(a)(a) = 1 \), impossible for a value in \( \{0, 1\} \). For \( f \colon X \to \powerset{X} \), put \( g(x) \coloneqq \ind_{f(x)} \); then \( D \coloneqq d^{-1}(\{1\}) \) has \( \ind_D = d \), and \( D = f(a) \) would give \( d = g(a) \). So no \( f \) is onto. 3.6's @cor-cantor-two-power went the other way, from Cantor to 0–1 functions; here the 0–1 argument comes first.

### 4.2 Section 5.2: Fibers and the Canonical Factorization

**Hook `## Functions on the clock` (~300).** A function \( g \colon \nZ \to Y \) with \( g(a) = g(a') \) whenever \( 12 \mid a - a' \) (constant on the classes mod 12) "is" a function of the hour. (Drafter note: state this form, not \( g(a + 12) = g(a) \), which needs an induction in both directions on \( k \) to reach \( g(a + 12k) = g(a) \).) Section 4.2 built the clock \( \nZ/12 \); which functions on \( \nZ \) are functions on \( \nZ/12 \), and how? And in reverse: every function sorts its domain by value. Questions: (Q1) when does a function on \( X \) pass to \( X/{\sim} \)? (Q2) What does a function look like once its domain is sorted by value?

**`## The fibers of a function` (~700).**
1. Recall (one paragraph) the fiber \( f^{-1}(\{y\}) \) and the relation \( \sim_f \) of @exm-fibres-of-function.
2. **`prp-fibres-partition`** `[The Fibers of a Function]`. Let \( f \colon X \to Y \). (a) The fibers over the points of \( f(X) \) are the classes of \( \sim_f \); they partition \( X \). (b) \( y \mapsto f^{-1}(\{y\}) \) is a bijection \( f(X) \to X/{\sim_f} \); so \( \card{f(X)} = \card{X/{\sim_f}} \). *Proof:* (a) is @exm-fibres-of-function with @thm-classes-partition. (b) Onto: \( [a] = f^{-1}(\{f(a)\}) \). Injective: for \( y = f(a) \ne y' \), \( a \) lies in the first fiber and not the second.
   **Quick check** (after the proposition): "How many classes has \( \sim_f \) for \( f(x) = x^2 \) on \( \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\} \)?" Three: \( \{0\} \), \( \{-1, 1\} \), \( \{-2, 2\} \), one per value \( 0, 1, 4 \).
3. Same-size payoff (~80): for @exm-same-size-subsets, \( A \mapsto \) number of elements on \( \powerset{\{1, 2, 3\}} \) has four fibers and image \( \{0, 1, 2, 3\} \).
4. **Quick check:** "List the fibers of \( f \colon \{1, \dots, 6\} \to \{0, 1, 2\} \), \( n \mapsto \) remainder of \( n \) on division by \( 3 \)." \( \{3, 6\}, \{1, 4\}, \{2, 5\} \): three fibers, three values.

**`## Every function factors` (~1,200).**
1. **`thm-canonical-factorization`** `[Canonical Factorization]`. Let \( f \colon X \to Y \), \( q \colon X \to X/{\sim_f} \) the quotient map, \( \iota \colon f(X) \to Y \) the inclusion. There is exactly one function \( \bar f \colon X/{\sim_f} \to f(X) \) with \( f = \iota \circ \bar f \circ q \); it is \( \bar f([x]) = f(x) \), and it is a bijection.
2. **`[Scratch work]`** (≤ 20 lines). The only candidate is forced: \( \bar f([x]) \) must be \( f(x) \). That rule reads a representative, so the first check is well-definedness (@exm-method-well-definedness); the twist is that injectivity is the same line read backwards.

   | Givens | Goal |
   |---|---|
   | \( [x] = [x'] \iff f(x) = f(x') \) (@thm-classes-partition (b)) | \( \bar f \) a function, bijective, \( f = \iota \circ \bar f \circ q \), unique |
   | \( [x] = [x'] \) | \( f(x) = f(x') \): (Fn2), well defined |
   | \( f(x) = f(x') \) | \( [x] = [x'] \): injective, the same equivalence read right to left |
   | \( y \in f(X) \), \( y = f(a) \) | \( y = \bar f([a]) \): surjective |

   Uniqueness: \( q \) is onto, so a second \( \bar f' \) with \( \iota \circ \bar f' \circ q = f \) has \( \bar f'([x]) = f(x) \) at every class (\( \iota \) changes no value).
3. **Proof** forwards. Aftermath: every function is a surjection (\( q \)), then a bijection, then an injection (\( \iota \)); \( f \) is injective iff \( q \) is (classes are single points, 4.2's second reading), surjective iff \( \iota = \id_Y \).
4. **`exm-fraction-map-factorization`** `[Fractions as classes]`. \( f \colon \nZ \times \nZ_{>0} \to \nQ \), \( (p, q) \mapsto p/q \). Fiber relation: \( p/q = p'/q' \iff pq' = p'q \), the relation of 4.2's Exercise B1 (@exr-equivalence-relations-b1; one prose line, a pointer, not a reason; the equivalence is school arithmetic of fractions, ledger). The words go to what is new: \( q \), \( \bar f \), \( \iota \), and why the numerator map does not factor. \( f \) is onto, so \( \bar f \) is a bijection from the classes onto \( \nQ \): a rational number "is" a class of fractions. The numerator map \( (p, q) \mapsto p \) is not constant on classes, \( (1, 2) \sim_f (2, 4) \): this is why "send \( p/q \) to \( p \)" failed in @exm-not-a-function. No fd3 pointer here: 4.2's B1 already carries it.
5. **Quick check:** "Which of \( q \), \( \bar f \), \( \iota \) are injective, and which surjective?" \( q \) onto; \( \bar f \) both; \( \iota \) injective.

**`## Functions out of a quotient` (~1,300).**
1. **`thm-functions-out-of-quotient`** `[Functions out of a Quotient]`. Let \( \sim \) be an equivalence relation on \( X \), \( q \) its quotient map, \( g \colon X \to Z \). (a) There is a function \( \bar g \colon X/{\sim} \to Z \) with \( \bar g \circ q = g \) iff \( g \) is **constant on classes** (\( x \sim x' \Rightarrow g(x) = g(x') \)); it is then unique, \( \bar g([x]) = g(x) \). (b) \( \bar g \) is injective iff \( g(x) = g(x') \Rightarrow x \sim x' \). (c) \( \bar g \) is surjective iff \( g \) is. *Proof* (Idea: the canonical factorization's argument with \( \sim \) for \( \sim_f \)): (a) ⇒: \( x \sim x' \) gives \( q(x) = q(x') \), so \( g(x) = \bar g(q(x)) = \bar g(q(x')) = g(x') \); ⇐: well-definedness; uniqueness from \( q \) onto. (b) unwind with @thm-classes-partition (b). (c) \( \bar g(X/{\sim}) = g(X) \).
2. Remark: @thm-canonical-factorization is (a)–(c) for \( \sim_f \) and \( g = f \) with codomain \( f(X) \).
3. **`exm-method-factor-through-quotient`** `[Method: define a function on a quotient]`.
   - **Template.** (1) Write the function \( g \) on \( X \) that the rule computes from a representative. (2) Check that \( g \) is constant on classes (@exm-method-well-definedness). (3) Conclude by @thm-functions-out-of-quotient: \( \bar g \) exists, is unique and \( \bar g \circ q = g \). (4) Read injectivity as "equal values force equivalence", surjectivity as surjectivity of \( g \).
   - **When to use it.** Maps out of \( \nZ/n \), out of classes of fractions, out of any "same in some respect".
   - **The usual failure.** Proving (4)'s converse instead of (2); a \( g \) not defined on all of \( X \); running the check for a map **into** a quotient, which needs none (compose with \( q \)).
4. **`exm-clock-maps`** `[Maps out of the clock]` (hook payoff). (a) \( r \colon \nZ \to \{0, 1\} \), the remainder on division by \( 2 \), is constant on classes mod 12: \( a - a' = 12k = 2(6k) \), and by 4.2's remainder test the remainders agree. So "is the hour even?" is a function \( \nZ/12 \to \{0, 1\} \), onto, not injective (\( r(0) = r(2) \), \( 0 \not\equiv 2 \)). (b) The same \( r \) on \( \nZ/2 \) gives a bijection, by (b): equal remainders give \( a \equiv a' \pmod 2 \). (c) \( a \mapsto [a + 1]_{12} \) is constant on classes, giving the **tick** \( t \colon \nZ/12 \to \nZ/12 \), \( [a] \mapsto [a + 1] \), a bijection with inverse \( [a] \mapsto [a - 1] \) (used in 5.4).
5. **Second reading** (~80, a fold): every surjection is a quotient map up to renaming (\( \bar f \) for onto \( f \)); a right inverse of \( q \) (@thm-surjection-right-inverse, needing @ax-choice) picks one representative from each class.
6. **Quick check** (uses (b), (c) of the theorem): "Is \( [a]_{10} \mapsto [a]_5 \) well defined? Injective? Surjective?" Yes (\( 10 \mid a - a' \Rightarrow 5 \mid a - a' \)); no (\( [0]_{10} \ne [5]_{10} \), both go to \( [0]_5 \)); yes (\( g = q_5 \) is onto).

### 4.3 Section 5.3: Binary Operations

**Hook `## Brackets` (~300).** \( 2 + 3 + 4 \) needs no brackets, but \( (2 - 3) - 4 = -5 \) and \( 2 - (3 - 4) = 3 \), and \( 2^{(3^2)} = 512 \), \( (2^3)^2 = 64 \). The same questions arise for \( \cup \) on sets, \( \circ \) on functions and \( + \) on the clock. Which rules does an "arithmetic" obey, and what follows from each rule alone? A question for the reader: can a set with an operation have two different "zeros"?

**`## Operations` (~850).**
1. **Failure first** (~100): subtraction takes \( (2, 3) \in \nN \times \nN \) outside \( \nN \); division has no value at \( (1, 0) \). An operation must give **every** pair a value **in the set**: (Fn1) with codomain \( X \).
2. **`def-binary-operation`** `[Binary Operation]`: a function \( \ast \colon X \times X \to X \), written \( x \ast y \). \( A \subseteq X \) is **closed** under \( \ast \) if \( a \ast b \in A \) for \( a, b \in A \); then \( \ast \) restricted to \( A \times A \), codomain \( A \), is an operation on \( A \) (5.1).
3. **`exm-first-operations`** `[Operations]`: \( + \), \( \cdot \), \( - \) on \( \nZ \); \( \max \) on \( \nR \); \( \cup \), \( \cap \), \( \triangle \) on \( \powerset{X} \); \( \circ \) on \( X^X \); \( + \) and \( \cdot \) on \( \nZ/n \) (@prp-congruence-arithmetic); the average \( (x + y)/2 \) on \( \nR \). Division on \( \nR \setminus \{0\} \) is one; \( \nZ_{>0} \) is closed under \( + \) but not under \( - \).
4. **Quick check:** "Is \( (A, B) \mapsto A \setminus B \) an operation on \( \powerset{X} \)? Are the even integers closed under \( + \)? The odd integers?" Yes (\( A \setminus B \subseteq X \)); yes; no (\( 1 + 1 = 2 \)).

**`## Properties` (~800).**
1. **`def-operation-properties`** `[Associative, Commutative, Identity, Inverse]`. For \( \ast \) on \( X \): associative, commutative (for all \( x, y, z \)); \( e \) is a **left identity** if \( e \ast x = x \) for all \( x \), a **right identity** if \( x \ast e = x \) for all \( x \), an **identity** if both. With an identity \( e \): \( y \) is an **inverse** of \( x \) if \( x \ast y = e = y \ast x \); \( x \) is **invertible** if it has one.
2. Deciding a property is 4.1's method (@exm-method-relation-properties): a "for all" is proved, a failure needs one witness.
3. **`exm-subtraction-identity`** `[A right identity that is not an identity]`: on \( \nZ \), \( x - 0 = x \), so \( 0 \) is a right identity; \( 0 - 1 \ne 1 \), so it is not a left identity; and there is no left identity: \( e - x = x \) for all \( x \) gives \( e = 0 \) (at \( x = 0 \)) and \( e = 2 \) (at \( x = 1 \)). Subtraction is not associative (\( 2, 3, 4 \)).
4. **Quick check:** "Is the average on \( \nR \) associative? Commutative? Has it an identity?" No: \( (0 \ast 0) \ast 4 = 2 \ne 1 = 0 \ast (0 \ast 4) \); yes; none: \( (e + x)/2 = x \) for all \( x \) gives \( e = x \) for all \( x \).
5. **Quick check:** "\( \max \) on \( \nN \) and on \( \nZ \): associative, commutative, identity?" Both associative and commutative; on \( \nN \) the identity is \( 0 \); on \( \nZ \) none: an identity \( e \) needs \( \max(e, e - 1) = e - 1 \).

**`## One identity, one inverse` (~1,300).**
1. **`prp-operation-identity-unique`** `[An Identity Is Unique]`: if \( e \) is a left identity and \( e' \) a right identity of \( \ast \), then \( e = e' \). An operation has at most one identity.
2. **`[Scratch work]`** (≤ 15 lines): two candidates, so @exm-method-uniqueness. The givens are equations about products, the goal has none: find one product both givens simplify.

   | Givens | Goal |
   |---|---|
   | \( e \ast x = x \) for all \( x \); \( x \ast e' = x \) for all \( x \) | \( e = e' \) |
   | the givens speak of products; the goal of two elements | one product each given can simplify |
   | \( e \ast e' \): first given at \( x = e' \) gives \( e' \); second at \( x = e \) gives \( e \) | \( e = e \ast e' = e' \) |

3. **Proof** (two lines). Aftermath: neither associativity nor commutativity was used.
4. **`exm-method-two-ways`** `[Method: compute one expression in two ways]`.
   - **Template.** To prove \( a = b \) when \( a \) and \( b \) are known only through equations: (1) find one expression \( m \) that the equations about \( a \) reduce to \( a \) and those about \( b \) reduce to \( b \); (2) write \( a = \dots = m = \dots = b \), citing a hypothesis at each "=".
   - **When to use it.** Uniqueness of identities and inverses, cancellation, any goal equating two objects given by equations.
   - **The usual failure.** Assuming \( a = b \) in the middle (N9); using associativity or commutativity that was not given; a bracket silently moved.
5. **`prp-operation-inverse-unique`** `[Inverses Are Unique for an Associative Operation]`: \( \ast \) associative with identity \( e \); if \( y \ast x = e \) and \( x \ast z = e \), then \( y = z \). So \( x \) has at most one inverse, written \( x^{-1} \) (\( -x \) for \( + \)). *Idea* (citing the box): \( m = y \ast x \ast z \). *Proof:* \( y = y \ast e = y \ast (x \ast z) = (y \ast x) \ast z = e \ast z = z \).
6. **`exm-two-inverses`** `[Without associativity, two inverses]`: \( X = \{e, a, b\} \), \( e \) an identity, and \( a \ast a = a \ast b = b \ast a = b \ast b = e \) (a table). Commutative with identity; \( a \) has inverses \( a \) and \( b \); not associative: \( (a \ast a) \ast b = b \), \( a \ast (a \ast b) = a \).
7. **`prp-operation-inverse-rules`** `[Inverses of Products]`: \( \ast \) associative with identity \( e \). (a) \( e^{-1} = e \). (b) If \( x \) is invertible, so is \( x^{-1} \), with \( (x^{-1})^{-1} = x \). (c) If \( x, y \) are invertible, so is \( x \ast y \), with \( (x \ast y)^{-1} = y^{-1} \ast x^{-1} \). *Proof:* check the candidate on both sides; (c): \( (x \ast y) \ast (y^{-1} \ast x^{-1}) = x \ast (y \ast y^{-1}) \ast x^{-1} = e \), and the other side alike; uniqueness by `prp-operation-inverse-unique`. For \( \circ \) on \( S_X \) (5.4), (c) gives \( (g \circ f)^{-1} = f^{-1} \circ g^{-1} \): write "for \( \circ \) on \( S_X \), (c) is the statement of Exercise B2 of Section 1.6 (@exr-inverses-b2), now proved for every associative operation", a prose pointer, never a reason.
8. **Quick check:** "Which elements of \( \powerset{X} \) are invertible under \( \cup \)? under \( \triangle \)?" Identity \( \emptyset \) in both. Under \( \cup \): \( A \cup B = \emptyset \) forces \( A = \emptyset \), so only \( \emptyset \). Under \( \triangle \): \( A \triangle A = \emptyset \), every \( A \) is its own inverse.

**`## Operations on functions` (~1,100).**
1. **`prp-pointwise-operations`** `[Pointwise Operations]`: \( \ast \) on \( Y \), \( X \) a set; \( (f \ast g)(x) \coloneqq f(x) \ast g(x) \) is an operation on \( Y^X \). If \( \ast \) is associative (commutative), so is the pointwise operation; if \( e \) is an identity, the constant \( x \mapsto e \) is one; \( f \) is invertible iff every \( f(x) \) is, with inverse \( x \mapsto f(x)^{-1} \). *Proof:* @prp-function-equality value by value; for "only if", evaluate \( f \ast g = \text{const}_e = g \ast f \) at \( x \).
2. **Warning** `**Iterate or product?**` (binding rule 4).
3. Indicators again (~80): on \( \{0, 1\}^X \), pointwise \( \cdot \) is \( \cap \) (`prp-indicator-arithmetic` (a)); the identity, constant \( 1 \), is \( \ind_X \).
4. **`prp-composition-operation`** `[Composition on X^X]`: \( \circ \) is an associative operation on \( X^X \) (@prp-composition-associative) with identity \( \id_X \); \( f \) is invertible iff bijective, with inverse the inverse function (@thm-bijection-iff-inverse, @prp-inverse-unique); if \( X \) has two elements \( a \ne b \), \( \circ \) is not commutative: constants \( c_a \circ c_b = c_a \ne c_b = c_b \circ c_a \).
5. **Quick check:** "In \( \nR^\nR \), the inverse of \( f(x) = 2x + 1 \) under \( \circ \) and under pointwise \( + \)?" \( (x - 1)/2 \); \( x \mapsto -2x - 1 \).

**`## Operations on a quotient` (~500, clock payoff).**
1. **`prp-operation-on-quotient`** `[Operations on a Quotient]`: \( \ast \) on \( X \), \( \sim \) an equivalence relation with \( x \sim x' \), \( y \sim y' \Rightarrow x \ast y \sim x' \ast y' \). Then \( [x] \ast [y] \coloneqq [x \ast y] \) is a well-defined operation on \( X/{\sim} \), \( q(x \ast y) = q(x) \ast q(y) \), and associativity, commutativity, an identity \( [e] \), inverses \( [x^{-1}] \) pass to it. *Idea* (citing `exm-method-factor-through-quotient`): the rule reads two representatives; check independence one argument at a time; the laws hold for representatives, and every class has one.
2. Instance: @prp-congruence-arithmetic is the hypothesis for \( + \) and \( \cdot \) on \( \nZ \); so \( \nZ/n \) has identity \( [0] \) for \( + \), inverse \( [-a] \), and \( [1] \) for \( \cdot \). On the clock: \( [9] + [3] = [0] \), so \( -[9] = [3] \).

### 4.4 Section 5.4: Permutations and Iteration

**Hook `## Shuffling twelve cards` (~350).** Positions \( 0, \dots, 11 \). Cut the deck into three piles of four (\( 0\text{–}3 \), \( 4\text{–}7 \), \( 8\text{–}11 \)) and deal them back in turn: the new order is the cards from positions \( 0, 4, 8, 1, 5, 9, 2, 6, 10, 3, 7, 11 \). The card at position \( i \) moves to \( \sigma(i) = 3i \) for \( i \le 3 \), \( 3i - 11 \) for \( 4 \le i \le 7 \), \( 3i - 22 \) for \( 8 \le i \le 11 \) (a gluing of three pieces, 5.1). Shuffle five times by hand: the deck is back. Question: does every shuffle, repeated, restore the deck, and after how many?

**`## Permutations and iterates` (~1,050).**
1. **`def-permutation`** `[Permutation]`: a bijection \( X \to X \); \( S_X \subseteq X^X \) the set of them (NOTATION row). Two-line tables for finite \( X \), as tables.
2. **`prp-permutations-closed`** `[Permutations under Composition]`: \( S_X \) is closed under \( \circ \) (@thm-composition-injective-surjective), contains \( \id_X \), and \( \sigma^{-1} \in S_X \) for \( \sigma \in S_X \); so \( \circ \) is an associative operation on \( S_X \) with identity in which every element is invertible (`prp-composition-operation`). Looking ahead (the section's one pointer): *Algebraic Preliminaries*, Chapter 2 names a set with such an operation and studies \( S_X \) as its first example.
3. **`def-iterate`** `[Iterates]`: for \( f \colon X \to X \), @thm-recursion on the set \( X^X \), start \( \id_X \), step \( (n, h) \mapsto f \circ h \), gives \( f^0 = \id_X \), \( f^{n+1} = f \circ f^n \). For \( \sigma \in S_X \), \( \sigma^{-n} \coloneqq (\sigma^{-1})^n \).
4. **`prp-iterate-laws`** `[Laws of Iterates]`: for \( m, n \in \nN \): (a) \( f^{m+n} = f^m \circ f^n \); (b) \( (f^m)^n = f^{mn} \); (c) for \( \sigma \in S_X \), \( \sigma^n \in S_X \) and \( \sigma^{m+n} = \sigma^m \circ \sigma^n \) for all \( m, n \in \nZ \). *Proof:* (a) induction on \( m \): \( f^{(m+1)+n} = f \circ f^{m+n} = f \circ (f^m \circ f^n) = f^{m+1} \circ f^n \). (b) induction on \( n \) with (a). (c) first \( \sigma \circ \sigma^{-1} = \id \) carried to \( \sigma^n \circ \sigma^{-n} = \id = \sigma^{-n} \circ \sigma^n \) by induction, then cases on the signs of \( m, n \): the case work goes into a fold (F5.4-a).
5. **Quick check:** "For \( f(x) = x + 3 \) on \( \nR \), what is \( f^n \)? Prove it." \( x + 3n \); induction, \( f^{n+1}(x) = (x + 3n) + 3 \).

**`## Orbits` (~950).**
1. **Failure first** (~120): "\( y = f^n(x) \) for some \( n \in \nN \)" is reflexive and transitive but not symmetric: \( n \mapsto n + 1 \) on \( \nN \) reaches \( 1 \) from \( 0 \), never \( 0 \) from \( 1 \). For a permutation, negative \( n \) repair it.
2. **`def-orbit`** `[Orbit, Forward Orbit, Fixed Point]`: for \( \sigma \in S_X \), \( x \sim_\sigma y \) if \( y = \sigma^k(x) \) for some \( k \in \nZ \); the **orbit** \( O_\sigma(x) \coloneqq \{ \sigma^k(x) : k \in \nZ \} \). For any \( f \colon X \to X \), the **forward orbit** of \( x \) is \( \{ f^n(x) : n \in \nN \} \); for a permutation it is contained in \( O_\sigma(x) \), and on a finite set the two are equal (by `prp-orbit-cycle`, said there). A **fixed point** of \( f \) is an \( x \) with \( f(x) = x \) (the sense of 3.4; 1.6's informal "fixed point \( x_0 \)" becomes "chosen point" at S3, §6).
3. **`thm-orbits-partition`** `[The Orbits of a Permutation Partition the Set]`: \( \sim_\sigma \) is an equivalence relation; its class of \( x \) is \( O_\sigma(x) \); so the orbits partition \( X \) (@thm-classes-partition); \( x \) is fixed iff \( O_\sigma(x) = \{x\} \). *Proof:* (Eq1) \( k = 0 \); (Eq2) \( \sigma^{-k}(\sigma^k(x)) = x \); (Eq3) \( \sigma^l(\sigma^k(x)) = \sigma^{l+k}(x) \), all by `prp-iterate-laws` (c). Fixed: \( \sigma(x) = x \) gives \( \sigma^{-1}(x) = x \), then \( \sigma^k(x) = x \) by induction both ways.
4. **Quick check:** "Orbits of \( \sigma = \begin{pmatrix} 1&2&3&4&5 \\ 3&5&1&2&4 \end{pmatrix} \)?" \( \{1, 3\} \), \( \{2, 5, 4\} \).

**`## Following an orbit` (~1,050).**
1. **`prp-orbit-cycle`** `[Following an Orbit]`: \( X \) finite, \( \sigma \in S_X \), \( x \in X \). There is a least \( k \ge 1 \) with \( \sigma^k(x) = x \); \( x, \sigma(x), \dots, \sigma^{k-1}(x) \) are distinct; \( O_\sigma(x) \) is exactly these \( k \) elements; and \( \sigma^m(x) = x \) iff \( k \mid m \) (\( m \in \nZ \)). \( k \) is the **length** of the orbit.
2. **`[Scratch work]`** (≤ 20 lines):

   | Givens | Goal |
   |---|---|
   | \( X \) has \( n \) elements; \( \sigma \) bijective | some \( k \ge 1 \) with \( \sigma^k(x) = x \) |
   | the \( n + 1 \) points \( \sigma^0(x), \dots, \sigma^n(x) \) | two equal: \( m \mapsto \sigma^{m-1}(x) \) on \( \{1, \dots, n + 1\} \) is not injective (@cor-pigeonhole-finite-sets (a)) |
   | \( \sigma^i(x) = \sigma^j(x) \), \( i < j \le n \); \( \sigma^j = \sigma^i \circ \sigma^{j-i} \) | cancel \( \sigma^i \): it is injective (`prp-iterate-laws` (c)); \( k = j - i \) works |
   | a non-empty set of such \( k \) | a least one (@thm-well-ordering) |

3. **Proof:** existence as in the table; distinctness: \( \sigma^i(x) = \sigma^j(x) \) with \( 0 \le i < j < k \) gives \( \sigma^{j-i}(x) = x \), \( 0 < j - i < k \), against minimality. Write \( m = qk + r \), \( 0 \le r < k \) (@thm-division-algorithm); \( \sigma^{qk}(x) = x \) (induction on \( q \ge 0 \), inverse for \( q < 0 \)), so \( \sigma^m(x) = \sigma^r(x) \), which is \( x \) iff \( r = 0 \) (distinctness).
4. **`exm-method-follow-the-orbit`** `[Method: follow an orbit]`.
   - **Template.** (1) List the forward orbit \( x, f(x), f^2(x), \dots \). (2) On a finite set of \( n \) elements, the first \( n + 1 \) terms repeat (@cor-pigeonhole-finite-sets). (3) Take the first repeat (@thm-well-ordering). (4) Carry the repeat with the map's structure: cancel if injective, push forward with \( f^{l+1} = f \circ f^l \) always.
   - **When to use it.** Repeated application on a finite set: shuffles, the clock tick, maps on \( \nZ/n \).
   - **The usual failure.** "The first repeat" without proving a repeat exists; cancelling a map that is not injective, which forgets the tail before the cycle.
5. **`exm-three-pile-shuffle`** `[The three-pile shuffle]` (hook payoff): for \( i = 4p + r \) (\( 0 \le r < 4 \)), \( \sigma(i) = 3r + p \equiv 3i \pmod{11} \) for \( i \le 10 \), and \( \sigma(11) = 11 \). Orbits \( \{0\} \), \( \{11\} \), \( \{1, 3, 9, 5, 4\} \) (\( 27 \equiv 5 \), \( 15 \equiv 4 \), \( 12 \equiv 1 \)), \( \{2, 6, 7, 10, 8\} \) (\( 18 \equiv 7 \), \( 21 \equiv 10 \), \( 30 \equiv 8 \), \( 24 \equiv 2 \)); lengths \( 1, 1, 5, 5 \); so \( \sigma^5 = \id \) (\( 3^5 = 243 = 22 \cdot 11 + 1 \)) and no smaller power is (5 is prime and \( \sigma(1) \ne 1 \)).
6. **Quick check:** "Length of the orbit of \( [1] \) under the tick \( t \) of `exm-clock-maps`, and under \( [a] \mapsto [a + 3] \) on \( \nZ/12 \)?" \( 12 \); \( \{[1], [4], [7], [10]\} \), length \( 4 \).

**`## Cycles` (~850).**
1. **`def-permutation-cycle`** `[Cycle]`: for distinct \( x_1, \dots, x_k \in X \), \( k \ge 2 \), the cycle \( (x_1\ x_2\ \cdots\ x_k) \) sends \( x_j \mapsto x_{j+1} \) (\( j < k \)), \( x_k \mapsto x_1 \), and fixes every other point: a gluing over the disjoint cover \( \{x_1, \dots, x_k\} \), its complement (Idea citing `exm-method-gluing`). A cycle is a permutation: \( (x_k\ \cdots\ x_2\ x_1) \) is its inverse (check on the \( k \) entries and off them). Cycles are **disjoint** if their entry sets are.
2. **`thm-permutation-cycles`** `[Cycle Decomposition]`: \( X \) finite, \( \sigma \in S_X \), \( O_1, \dots, O_r \) its orbits with at least two elements, \( x_i \in O_i \), \( k_i \) the number of elements of \( O_i \) (so \( \card{O_i} = k_i \)), \( \gamma_i \coloneqq (x_i\ \sigma(x_i)\ \cdots\ \sigma^{k_i - 1}(x_i)) \). The \( \gamma_i \) are pairwise disjoint cycles, \( \gamma_i \circ \gamma_j = \gamma_j \circ \gamma_i \), and \( \sigma = \gamma_1 \circ \gamma_2 \circ \cdots \circ \gamma_r \), bracketed from the left (\( \sigma = \id_X \) if \( r = 0 \)). *Proof:* entries distinct by `prp-orbit-cycle`; disjoint by `thm-orbits-partition`. Define \( \delta^{(0)} \coloneqq \id_X \) and \( \delta^{(j)} \coloneqq \delta^{(j-1)} \circ \gamma_j \) by recursion (so \( \delta^{(r)} \) is the composite, bracketed once and for all), and prove by induction on \( j \): \( \delta^{(j)}(y) = \sigma(y) \) for \( y \in O_1 \cup \dots \cup O_j \), \( = y \) otherwise. The key value: for \( y = \sigma^m(x_i) \), \( m < k_i \), \( \gamma_i(y) = \sigma(y) \) (for \( m = k_i - 1 \), \( \gamma_i(y) = x_i = \sigma^{k_i}(x_i) = \sigma(y) \)); \( \gamma_j \), \( j \ne i \), fixes \( y \) and \( \sigma(y) \in O_i \). Closing line: a point outside \( O_1 \cup \dots \cup O_r \) has a one-element orbit, so it is fixed by \( \sigma \) (@thm-orbits-partition); hence \( \delta^{(r)} = \sigma \) by @prp-function-equality. Commuting: evaluate at a point of \( O_i \), of \( O_j \), and of neither; this check goes into a fold (F5.4-b, ~120). After the theorem, one sentence: "We write \( \gamma\delta \) for \( \gamma \circ \delta \) (right factor first), as in *Algebraic Preliminaries*."
3. Examples (~150): the three-pile shuffle is \( (1\ 3\ 9\ 5\ 4)(2\ 6\ 7\ 10\ 8) \); the tick of `exm-clock-maps` is the 12-cycle \( ([0]\ [1]\ \cdots\ [11]) \).
4. **`cor-permutation-power-identity`** `[Some Power Is the Identity]`: \( X \) finite, \( \sigma \in S_X \): there is \( N \ge 1 \) with \( \sigma^N = \id_X \); and \( \sigma^N = \id_X \) iff every orbit length divides \( N \). *Proof:* list \( X = \{x_1, \dots, x_n\} \) (a bijection from \( \{1, \dots, n\} \)), \( N_0 = 1 \), \( N_i = N_{i-1} k_{x_i} \) (recursion); \( k_{x_i} \mid N_i \mid N_n \) (@prp-divides-transitive, induction); then `prp-orbit-cycle`'s "iff". The least such \( N \) is the least common multiple of the lengths (prose, with @exm-divisibility-lcm for two lengths).
5. **Quick check:** "Decompose \( [a] \mapsto [5a] \) on \( \nZ/12 \)." \( 25 \equiv 1 \), so it is its own inverse: \( ([1]\ [5])([2]\ [10])([4]\ [8])([7]\ [11]) \), fixing \( [0], [3], [6], [9] \).

**`## Maps that are not permutations` (~550).**
1. **`prp-eventually-periodic`** `[Forward Orbits of a Self-Map of a Finite Set]`: \( X \) with \( n \) elements, \( f \colon X \to X \), \( x \in X \): there are \( m \ge 0 \), \( p \ge 1 \), \( m + p \le n \), with \( f^{l+p}(x) = f^l(x) \) for every \( l \ge m \). *Idea* (box, step (4) without cancelling). *Proof:* pigeonhole gives \( i < j \le n \) with \( f^i(x) = f^j(x) \); \( m = i \), \( p = j - i \); induction on \( l \ge m \): \( f^{l+1+p}(x) = f(f^{l+p}(x)) = f(f^l(x)) \).
