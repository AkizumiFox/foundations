# The Rationals

Chapter 2 built \( \nZ \), an ordered commutative ring in which \( 2x = 1 \) has no solution. This chapter supplies the missing quotients by the move that built \( \nZ \): a pair \( (p, q) \) with \( q > 0 \) records "\( p \) divided by \( q \)" without performing it, two pairs are identified when \( ps = rq \), and a rational number is a class of such pairs. Addition and multiplication are defined on representatives and checked to be well defined, and each field law becomes an identity in \( \nZ \), already proved. *Sets and Functions*, Section 3.1 took \( \nQ = \{ m/n : m \in \nZ, \ n \in \nZ_{>0} \} \) as a definition; here it is a theorem about the constructed set. The order comes from one choice: a class is positive when a representative has a positive numerator. The Archimedean property then follows from the construction, with no supremum anywhere.

The last section asks what \( \nQ \) still lacks. No rational squares to \( 2 \), and the set of rationals with square below \( 2 \) is bounded above but has no supremum in \( \nQ \). A sequence of rationals, \( 2, 3/2, 17/12, \dots \), closes in on the gap from above. Chapters 4 and 5 turn sequences of this kind into the real numbers.

## Roadmap

- **The Rationals as Classes of Fractions.** Pairs \( (p, q) \) with \( q > 0 \) identified by cross-multiplication, \( \nQ \) as their quotient set, a sum and a product checked on representatives (the second argument free by symmetry), rules that look like functions and are not, the proof that \( \nQ \) is a field, and the copy of \( \nZ \) inside it that turns the class \( [(p, q)] \) into the quotient \( p/q \).
- **The Rationals as an Ordered Field; the Archimedean Property.** The positive rationals as the classes with a positive numerator, and why negative denominators would break this; \( \nQ \) an ordered field with the comparison formula \( p/q \le r/s \iff ps \le rq \); reciprocals reversing order and the midpoint of two rationals; every rational below a natural number, proved from the construction, with its form \( 1/n < \varepsilon \); and the integer part \( \floor{x} \).
- **The Gaps in the Rationals.** No rational squares to \( 2 \), proved by descent on a least denominator rather than by parity; the set \( \{ x \in \nQ : x^2 < 2 \} \), bounded above with no supremum in \( \nQ \), each candidate refuted by an explicit rational; and the rational sequence \( 2, 3/2, 17/12, 577/408, \dots \) that closes in on the gap from above. The review exercises return to Chapter 2 and to *Sets and Functions* and *Logic and Proof*.

## Named moves

The chapter introduces no new method box; the first four moves below were taught earlier and are run here on new data, and the last is new to this chapter.

- **Check on representatives, one argument at a time** (@fd2:exm-method-well-definedness, with @thm-well-defined-on-classes). Change one representative with the other fixed; each change is one identity at the level below. A formula symmetric in its two inputs gets the second change free. (Section 3.1)
- **Reduce to the level below** (Section 2.3). Write a law about classes on representatives; the two sides become the same pair, or two related pairs, by an identity in \( \nZ \). (Section 3.1)
- **Order from a positive set** (@prp-order-from-positives). Say which elements are positive, check (P1) and (P2), and read off every rule for inequalities. (Section 3.2)
- **Descent on a least denominator** (@fd2:exm-method-smallest-counterexample). Take a counterexample with the least denominator and build one with a smaller denominator. (Section 3.3)
- **Refute a supremum candidate by an explicit rational.** Given a rational \( s \) claimed to be the supremum, produce from \( s \) an element of the set above it or an upper bound below it. (Section 3.3, the scratch work for @thm-rationals-gap-sqrt2)

## Threads

- **Classes of pairs.** Section 2.1 made integers from pairs of natural numbers, Section 3.1 makes rationals from pairs of integers, and Chapter 5 makes real numbers from classes of sequences of rationals, by the same route.
- **The integer part.** Section 3.2 defines \( \floor{x} \); for \( a \in \nZ \) and \( n \in \nZ_{>0} \), \( \floor{a/n} \) is the quotient of Section 2.5 (Exercise B3 of Section 3.2), and Section 4.2 builds the integer part in every Archimedean ordered field.
- **\( 1/n < \varepsilon \).** The corollary of Section 3.2 is the form of the Archimedean property that the limit arguments of Chapters 4 and 5 use, with \( \varepsilon \in \nQ_{>0} \).
- **The sequence \( 2, 3/2, 17/12, \dots \)** Section 3.3 computes it; Section 4.6 proves it Cauchy in \( \nQ \) with no limit in \( \nQ \); Chapter 5 opens with it, as the first sequence of rationals that should converge and does not in \( \nQ \).

## Summary

A rational number is a class of pairs \( (p, q) \in \nZ \times \nZ_{>0} \) under \( ps = rq \) (@def-rationals-as-classes), and the sum and product defined on representatives are well defined (@prp-rational-operations-well-defined). With them \( \nQ \) is a field (@thm-rationals-field) containing a copy of \( \nZ \), and every rational is \( m/n \) with \( m \in \nZ \) and \( n \in \nZ_{>0} \) (@prp-integers-embed-rationals). Declaring a class positive when its numerator is positive makes \( \nQ \) an ordered field whose order extends that of \( \nZ \) (@def-rational-order, @thm-rationals-ordered-field). Every rational lies below a natural number (@thm-rationals-archimedean), so for each \( \varepsilon \in \nQ_{>0} \) some \( 1/n \) is smaller (@cor-rationals-archimedean-epsilon), and every rational has an integer part (@prp-rational-integer-part). No rational squares to \( 2 \) (@thm-no-rational-square-two), and \( \{ x \in \nQ : x^2 < 2 \} \) is bounded above with no supremum in \( \nQ \) (@thm-rationals-gap-sqrt2).

What is open: \( \nQ \) is an Archimedean ordered field with gaps. Can the gaps be filled, and by what? Chapter 4 develops limits in an ordered field to say what "closing in on a gap" means, and Chapter 5 fills the gaps.

## Terms

- **denominator, numerator**: \( q \) and \( p \) in a representative \( (p, q) \) of a rational number (@def-rationals-as-classes).
- **integer part**: \( \floor{x} \), the one integer \( m \) with \( m \le x < m + 1 \) (@prp-rational-integer-part).
- **order on \( \nQ \)**: \( x < y \) when \( y - x \) is positive (@def-rational-order).
- **positive rational**: a class \( [(p, q)] \) with \( p > 0 \); the set \( P_{\nQ} = \nQ_{>0} \) (@def-rational-order).
- **rational number**: a class \( [(p, q)] \) of pairs in \( \nZ \times \nZ_{>0} \) under \( ps = rq \) (@def-rationals-as-classes).
