**Purpose.**
- F3.10 built one complete ordered field. Real Analysis assumes one. This chapter proves that the two agree: **any two complete ordered fields are isomorphic, by exactly one order-preserving field isomorphism**, so "the" real numbers are well defined and no theorem depends on the construction.
- The tool is one idea: in an Archimedean ordered field an element is determined by **the rationals below it**. An order-preserving map must send that set to the same set, and in a complete field the target element is its supremum.
- Show what each hypothesis buys with two non-examples: ℝ(x) (not Archimedean, hence not complete) and ℚ(√2) (Archimedean, not complete, and with an automorphism that does not preserve order).
- Close the Foundations construction of ℝ and hand over to Real Analysis: assume the axioms, or build a model; this chapter is why the two choices give the same mathematics.
- The reader leaves able to (i) prove two elements of an Archimedean field equal by comparing rationals below them; (ii) build an order-preserving map as a supremum and check a field identity for it by the rationals-below test; (iii) say which hypothesis (Archimedean, complete, order-preserving) each non-example breaks.

**Signature moves** (`index.md`, "Named moves"):
- **Rationals separate.** To show \( a = b \) in an Archimedean ordered field, show that the same rationals lie below each (a rational strictly between them would otherwise exist).
- **Construct a point as a supremum** (fd3's first use was in F3.10; here it builds square roots and the map \( \varphi \)). Faded triple below.
- **Uniqueness gives inverses.** If the only order-preserving field map \( F \to F \) is the identity, then two maps \( F \to L \to F \) compose to the identity without computing anything.

## 01-completeness-and-archimedes.md — What completeness gives at once

- **Standing setting and convention.** \( F \) is an ordered field (`@def-ordered-ring-and-field`); "complete" is F3.10's `@def-complete-ordered-field`, recalled, not redefined. Write \( q \) for \( \iota_F(q) \) inside proofs once the injectivity and order-preservation of \( \iota_F \) (`@thm-rationals-embed-ordered-field`) are recalled in one sentence.
- **`def-cof-order-preserving-map`.** For ordered fields \( F \), \( L \): a map \( \chi \colon F \to L \) is an *order-preserving field map* if \( \chi(a + b) = \chi(a) + \chi(b) \), \( \chi(ab) = \chi(a)\chi(b) \), \( \chi(1) = 1 \), and \( a < b \Rightarrow \chi(a) < \chi(b) \) for all \( a, b \in F \). An *order-preserving field isomorphism* is a bijective one; an *automorphism* has \( L = F \). Remark: strict increase gives injectivity, and in a total order also \( \chi(a) < \chi(b) \Rightarrow a < b \) (contrapositive with trichotomy, `@prp-contrapositive`).
- **`prp-cof-map-fixes-rationals`.** Hypotheses: \( F \), \( L \) ordered fields; \( \chi \colon F \to L \) additive, multiplicative, \( \chi(1) = 1 \) (no order hypothesis). Then \( \chi \circ \iota_F = \iota_L \). Proof (cite F3.9): \( \chi \circ \iota_F \colon \nQ \to L \) is additive, multiplicative and sends \( 1 \) to \( 1 \), so it is \( \iota_L \) by the uniqueness clause of `@thm-rationals-embed-ordered-field`.
- **`thm-cof-complete-archimedean`.** Hypothesis: \( F \) complete ordered field. Then \( F \) is Archimedean (`@def-archimedean-ordered-field`). Proof as in the ordering item (§01 A1(c) gives it shuffled; the text proves it in full first).
- **Density (recalled from F3.9, no new label).** In an Archimedean ordered field, between \( x < y \) lies some \( \iota_F(q) \), \( q \in \nQ \) (`@thm-rationals-dense-ordered-field`). Cited, not reproved; §02 and §04 (for ℚ(√2)) use it.
- **`def-cof-rationals-below`** \( \nQ_F(x) \). **`exm-cof-rationals-below-sqrt2`:** in the Cauchy model, \( \nQ_{\nR}(\sqrt 2) = \{ q : q < 0 \} \cup \{ q \ge 0 : q^2 < 2 \} \) (after `prp-cof-square-roots`); \( \nQ_F(\iota_F(r)) = \{ q : q < r \} \).
- **`lem-cof-rationals-separate`.** Hypothesis: \( F \) Archimedean. For \( x, y \in F \): \( x \le y \iff \nQ_F(x) \subseteq \nQ_F(y) \); hence \( x = y \iff \nQ_F(x) = \nQ_F(y) \). Route: \( \Rightarrow \) by transitivity; \( \Leftarrow \) by contrapositive: if \( y < x \), density (cite `@thm-rationals-dense-ordered-field`) gives \( q \in \nQ_F(x) \setminus \nQ_F(y) \).
- **`prp-cof-square-roots`** (faded triple, full version). Hypotheses: \( F \) complete, \( a \in F \), \( a \ge 0 \). Then there is exactly one \( b \ge 0 \) with \( b^2 = a \). Existence: \( S = \{ y \ge 0 : y^2 < a \} \cup \{0\} \), bounded above by \( a + 1 \); \( s = \sup S \). (The case \( a = 0 \) is \( b = 0 \); let \( a > 0 \), so \( s > 0 \).) Rule out \( s^2 < a \) with \( h = \min\{1, (a - s^2)/(2(2s + 1))\} \) (then \( (s + h)^2 \le s^2 + h(2s + 1) < a \)); rule out \( s^2 > a \) with \( h = (s^2 - a)/(2s) \) (then \( s - h \) is an upper bound). Uniqueness: \( b^2 = c^2 \), \( b, c \ge 0 \) gives \( (b - c)(b + c) = 0 \). Constants derived backwards in the Idea, placed first in the proof.
- **Warning.** Completeness is about subsets, not sequences: F3.10 proved the Cauchy model complete via Cauchy sequences, but the definition used here is the supremum one (`@def-complete-ordered-field`). One sentence: F3.10's `@thm-cauchy-archimedean-lub` gives "Archimedean and Cauchy-complete ⇒ complete"; this chapter needs only the supremum form.
- **Check.** Find \( \nQ_F(x) \) for \( x = \iota_F(3/2) \) and say whether \( 3/2 \) belongs to it (no: strict inequality).

## 02-measuring-by-rationals.md — Building the map from rationals below

- **Idea first.** If \( \varphi \colon F \to L \) is order-preserving and fixes ℚ, then \( q < x \iff \varphi(q) < \varphi(x) \iff q < \varphi(x) \): \( \varphi(x) \) has exactly the rationals below that \( x \) has. In a complete \( L \) there is a natural candidate, the supremum of those rationals.
- **`thm-cof-embedding-into-complete`** (key proof 1; try it first). Hypotheses: \( F \) an Archimedean ordered field, \( L \) a complete ordered field. Then there is **exactly one** order-preserving field map \( \varphi \colon F \to L \), namely
  \( \varphi(x) \coloneqq \sup_L \{ \iota_L(q) : q \in \nQ_F(x) \} \),
  and it satisfies (★) \( \nQ_L(\varphi(x)) = \nQ_F(x) \) for every \( x \in F \).
  Proof route, as numbered claims:
  1. *Defined.* \( \nQ_F(x) \) is nonempty and bounded above in ℚ: Archimedes in \( F \) gives \( n \) with \( -n < x < n \); so \( \iota_L(n) \) bounds the set in \( L \) (\( \iota_L \) order-preserving). Completeness of \( L \).
  2. *(★).* If \( q \in \nQ_F(x) \): density in \( F \) gives \( r \) with \( q < r < x \), so \( \iota_L(q) < \iota_L(r) \le \varphi(x) \). If \( \iota_L(q) < \varphi(x) \): \( \iota_L(q) \) is not an upper bound, so some \( r \in \nQ_F(x) \) has \( q < r \), hence \( q \in \nQ_F(x) \).
  3. *Fixes ℚ.* \( \nQ_L(\varphi(\iota_F(r))) = \{ q < r \} = \nQ_L(\iota_L(r)) \); `lem-cof-rationals-separate` in \( L \) (Archimedean by `thm-cof-complete-archimedean`).
  4. *Strictly increasing.* \( x < y \): rationals \( r < s \) with \( x < r < s < y \) (density twice); (★) gives \( \varphi(x) \le \iota_L(r) < \iota_L(s) \le \varphi(y) \).
  5. *Additive* by `lem-cof-below-sum` and (★): \( \nQ_L(\varphi(x + y)) = \nQ_F(x + y) = \{ q : \exists r \in \nQ_F(x),\ q - r \in \nQ_F(y) \} = \nQ_L(\varphi(x) + \varphi(y)) \); then rationals separate in \( L \).
  6. *Multiplicative:* for \( x, y > 0 \) by `lem-cof-below-product` the same way (note \( \varphi(x) > 0 \) by step 4 and step 3); \( \varphi(-x) = -\varphi(x) \) from 5; the sign cases follow; \( \varphi(1) = 1 \) by 3.
  7. *Uniqueness.* Any order-preserving field map \( \chi \) fixes ℚ (`prp-cof-map-fixes-rationals`) and reflects order, so \( \nQ_L(\chi(x)) = \nQ_F(x) = \nQ_L(\varphi(x)) \); rationals separate in \( L \).
- **`lem-cof-below-sum`.** Hypotheses: \( F \) Archimedean, \( a, b \in F \), \( q \in \nQ \). Then \( q < a + b \iff \) there is \( r \in \nQ \) with \( r < a \) and \( q - r < b \). (\( \Leftarrow \) add; \( \Rightarrow \) density in \( (q - b, a) \).)

## 03-the-uniqueness-theorem.md — Two complete ordered fields, one isomorphism

- **`thm-cof-uniqueness`** (key proof 2). Hypotheses: \( F \), \( L \) complete ordered fields. Then there is exactly one order-preserving field isomorphism \( F \to L \). Proof: \( \varphi \colon F \to L \) and \( \psi \colon L \to F \) from `thm-cof-embedding-into-complete` (each source is Archimedean by `thm-cof-complete-archimedean`). \( \psi \circ \varphi \) is an order-preserving field map \( F \to F \); so is \( \mathrm{id}_F \); by the uniqueness clause (with \( L \coloneqq F \)) they are equal. Likewise \( \varphi \circ \psi = \mathrm{id}_L \). So \( \varphi \) is a bijection (`@thm-bijection-iff-inverse`). Any other isomorphism is an order-preserving field map, so equals \( \varphi \).
- **`cor-cof-isomorphic-to-cauchy-reals`.** Every complete ordered field is isomorphic to F3.10's \( \nR \), by a unique order-preserving field isomorphism. Write \( F \cong L \) (`NOTATION.md`). Remark, proved in place: on any set of ordered fields, \( \cong \) is an equivalence relation (`@def-equivalence-relation`): \( \mathrm{id}_F \) is an order-preserving field isomorphism; the inverse of one (`@thm-bijection-iff-inverse`) is again additive, multiplicative and order-preserving (\( \chi(\chi^{-1}(u) + \chi^{-1}(v)) = u + v \) gives \( \chi^{-1}(u + v) = \chi^{-1}(u) + \chi^{-1}(v) \), likewise for products, and \( \chi^{-1} \) is strictly increasing because \( \chi \) reflects order); a composite of two is one. Nothing later uses symmetry or transitivity.
- **`exm-cof-two-models`.** Prose: the cut model of F3.3 (optional, prose pointer only) is complete, hence \( \cong \nR \); "\( \sqrt 2 \) as a Cauchy class" and "\( \sqrt 2 \) as a cut" correspond under \( \varphi \), and by (★) the cut of \( x \) is essentially \( \nQ_F(x) \): the Dedekind cut is the rationals-below set made into the definition.
- **`cor-cof-order-automorphism-identity`.** The only order-preserving field automorphism of a complete ordered field (in particular of \( \nR \)) is the identity. One line from step 7.
- **`thm-cof-field-automorphism-identity`** (key proof 3; try it first). Hypotheses: \( F \) complete ordered field, \( \sigma \colon F \to F \) additive, multiplicative, \( \sigma(1) = 1 \) (no order hypothesis, no bijectivity). Then \( \sigma = \mathrm{id}_F \). Proof: for \( a > 0 \), `prp-cof-square-roots` gives \( b \ne 0 \) with \( b^2 = a \); \( \sigma(b) \ne 0 \) since \( \sigma(b)\sigma(b^{-1}) = 1 \); so \( \sigma(a) = \sigma(b)^2 > 0 \) (F3.2 `@prp-ordered-ring-rules` (e): nonzero squares are positive). ## 04-two-non-examples.md — ℝ(x) and ℚ(√2)

- **`lem-cof-leading-term-sign`.** Hypotheses: \( d \in \nN \), \( a_0, \dots, a_d \in \nR \), \( a_d \ne 0 \), \( p(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{d} a_i t^i \) (powers \( t^i \) by F3.9's `@def-naturals-in-ordered-field`; finite sums in an ordered field by the ledger line on finite sums). Then for every \( t > T \coloneqq 1 + \sum_{i < d} \abs{a_i}/\abs{a_d} \), \( p(t) \ne 0 \) and \( p(t) \) has the sign of \( a_d \). Route, two cases. If \( d = 0 \), then \( p(t) = a_0 \) for every \( t \) and the claim is immediate (\( T = 1 \), the sum over \( i < 0 \) being empty). If \( d \ge 1 \), then for \( t > T \) (so \( t \ge 1 \)): \( \abs{\sum_{i<d} a_i t^i} \le (\sum_{i<d} \abs{a_i}) t^{d-1} \) (triangle inequality by induction on the number of terms from F3.9's `@prp-absolute-value-ordered-field` (e), and \( t^i \le t^{d-1} \) for \( i \le d - 1 \) and \( t \ge 1 \), from the power laws of the ledger line) \( < \abs{a_d} t^d \), the strict inequality because \( t > \sum_{i<d} \abs{a_i}/\abs{a_d} \); so \( p(t) = a_d t^d + \sum_{i<d} a_i t^i \) has the sign of \( a_d t^d \), that is of \( a_d \).
- **`lem-cof-polynomial-closure`.** Call \( t \mapsto \sum_{i \le d} a_i t^i \) (\( a_i \in \nR \), \( d \in \nN \)) a *polynomial function* with coefficient list \( (a_0, \dots, a_d) \); it has *top degree* \( d \) when \( a_d \ne 0 \). Hypotheses: \( p(t) = \sum_{i \le d} a_i t^i \) and \( r(t) = \sum_{j \le e} b_j t^j \) with **arbitrary** coefficient lists \( (a_0, \dots, a_d) \), \( (b_0, \dots, b_e) \) (zero entries, and the zero list, allowed). Then:
  - (a) \( p + r \) is the polynomial function with coefficients \( a_k + b_k \) (pad the shorter list with zeros).
  - (b) \( p \cdot r \) is a polynomial function with a coefficient list \( (c_0, \dots, c_{d+e}) \) whose last entry is \( c_{d+e} = a_d b_e \). If moreover \( a_d \ne 0 \) and \( b_e \ne 0 \), then \( a_d b_e \ne 0 \), so \( p \cdot r \) has top degree \( d + e \) and top coefficient \( a_d b_e \). (No formula for the other \( c_k \) is stated; nothing uses one.)
  - (c) *Identity principle:* if a polynomial function is \( 0 \) at all large \( t \), then every coefficient in its list is \( 0 \).
  Route: (a) by regrouping a finite sum termwise (ledger line on finite sums). (b) by induction on \( e \) (`@ax-induction`), so that no double sums need regrouping: \( t \cdot p(t) \) is the polynomial function with the list shifted by one (power laws, \( t \cdot t^i = t^{i+1} \)), and \( c \cdot p \) is one with list \( (c a_i) \) (distributivity over a finite sum, by induction on the number of terms); for \( e = 0 \) this is \( b_0 \cdot p \), list \( (b_0 a_0, \dots, b_0 a_d) \), last entry \( a_d b_0 \); for \( e \ge 1 \) write \( r(t) = b_0 + t \cdot r'(t) \) with \( r' \) the polynomial function of list \( (b_1, \dots, b_e) \) (length \( e \)), so by the induction hypothesis \( p \cdot r' \) has a list of length \( d + e \) with last entry \( a_d b_e \); shifting by \( t \) gives length \( d + e + 1 \), and adding \( b_0 \cdot p \) by (a) (its list has length \( d + 1 \le d + e \), padded with zeros) leaves the last entry \( a_d b_e \). All of this goes through for arbitrary lists (the zero list times anything is a list of zeros). Only the final sentence of (b) uses that a field has no zero divisors. (c) is the contrapositive of `lem-cof-leading-term-sign`: a list with a nonzero entry, cut at its last nonzero entry (dropping zero terms does not change the function), gives a function that is nonzero at all \( t > T \). Consequence used in `def-cof-rational-functions`: a product of two lists that each have a nonzero coefficient again has one (cut each at its last nonzero entry, then (b)).
- **`def-cof-rational-functions`** \( \nR(x) \). (Prose: F3.9 showed a school-level non-Archimedean field ℚ(t) as an illustration that nothing depends on; ℝ(x) here is built without polynomial algebra and is what this chapter uses.) A rational function is a pair of polynomial functions \( p/q \), \( q \) with a nonzero coefficient, read as the function \( t \mapsto p(t)/q(t) \) on \( t > T_q \). Two are equal in \( \nR(x) \) when they agree at all sufficiently large \( t \) (an equivalence relation, `@def-equivalence-relation`; \( \nR(x) \) is the quotient, `@def-quotient-set`). Sum and product pointwise at large \( t \), with representatives \( (p_1/q_1) + (p_2/q_2) \coloneqq (p_1 q_2 + p_2 q_1)/(q_1 q_2) \) and \( (p_1/q_1)(p_2/q_2) \coloneqq (p_1 p_2)/(q_1 q_2) \): these are pairs of polynomial functions by `lem-cof-polynomial-closure` (a), (b), and \( q_1 q_2 \) has a nonzero coefficient by (b); they agree with the pointwise sum and product at every \( t > \max\{T_{q_1}, T_{q_2}, T_{q_1 q_2}\} \); \( f > 0 \) when \( f(t) > 0 \) for all large \( t \). \( x \) denotes the class of \( t \mapsto t \).
- **`prp-cof-rational-functions-ordered`.** \( \nR(x) \) is an ordered field. Route: each field identity holds at every large \( t \), because it holds in \( \nR \); a nonzero class has \( p \) with a nonzero coefficient, so \( q/p \) is its inverse (`lem-cof-leading-term-sign` for \( p \)); trichotomy: by the lemma, \( p \) and \( q \) have eventually constant sign; positives closed under \( + \), \( \cdot \) (take the larger threshold). Well-definedness of the operations in one paragraph (the F3.2 pattern), the axiom checks as a table, two written out.
- **`prp-cof-rational-functions-not-archimedean`.** \( x - \iota(n) > 0 \) for every \( n \), so \( \iota(\nN) \) is bounded above by \( x \); by `thm-cof-complete-archimedean`, \( \nR(x) \) is not complete. Directly: an upper bound \( s \) of \( \iota(\nN) \) gives the smaller upper bound \( s - 1 \), so there is no supremum (exit check C1).
- **`exm-cof-infinitesimal`.** \( 0 < 1/x < \iota(1/n) \) for all \( n \ge 1 \). So in \( \nR(x) \) the sequence \( (\iota(1/(n+1)))_{n \in \nN} \) does **not** tend to \( 0 \) in the sense of `@def-sequence-limit-ordered-field` (take \( \varepsilon = 1/x \), since \( \iota(1/(n+1)) > 1/x \) for every \( n \in \nN \); it is exactly the sequence of F3.9's `@prp-reciprocals-null-iff-archimedean`), and "\( 0 \le a < 1/n \) for all \( n \) forces \( a = 0 \)" fails. A theorem about sequences in an ordered field that a reader thinks is "obvious" is usually Archimedes in disguise.
- **`exm-cof-x-not-square`** (proved in the text; the closing table cites it). \( x > 0 \) in \( \nR(x) \), but \( x \) is not a square in \( \nR(x) \). Proof: suppose \( x = (p/q)^2 \); then \( t \cdot q(t)^2 = p(t)^2 \) for all large \( t \), so by `lem-cof-polynomial-closure` (a), (b) the polynomial function \( t \cdot q^2 - p^2 \) has a coefficient list, which is all zero by (c); so the two sides have the same coefficient lists. Cut \( q \) at its last nonzero entry \( b_e \) (it has one). If \( p \) is the zero list, then \( t \cdot q^2 \) has top coefficient \( b_e^2 \ne 0 \) by (b), against (c). Otherwise cut \( p \) at its last nonzero entry \( a_d \): by (b) the left side has top degree \( 2e + 1 \), odd, and the right side \( 2d \), even, so their difference has the nonzero coefficient \( \pm b_e^2 \) or \( \pm a_d^2 \) at index \( \max\{2d, 2e + 1\} \), against (c). The contrast with \( \nR \), where every positive element is a square (`prp-cof-square-roots`), is the contrast the closing table records.
- **`def-cof-sqrt2-field`.** \( \nQ(\sqrt 2) \coloneqq \{ a + b\sqrt 2 : a, b \in \nQ \} \subseteq \nR \), with \( \sqrt 2 \) from `prp-cof-square-roots` in F3.10's \( \nR \).
- **`prp-cof-sqrt2-field-archimedean`.** It is a subfield of \( \nR \) (inverse \( (a - b\sqrt 2)/(a^2 - 2b^2) \), denominator nonzero by `@thm-no-rational-square-two`; representation unique by the same theorem), ordered by restriction. Archimedean: \( \nR \) is complete (`@thm-reals-complete-ordered-field`), hence Archimedean (`thm-cof-complete-archimedean`). For \( b \in \nQ(\sqrt 2) \) there is \( n \in \nN \) with \( b < \iota_{\nR}(n) \), and \( \iota_{\nR}(n) = n \cdot 1 \) lies in \( \nQ(\sqrt 2) \) as the same element, with the order restricted, so \( \nQ(\sqrt 2) \) is Archimedean (`@def-archimedean-ordered-field`).
- **`prp-cof-sqrt2-field-incomplete`.** \( \nQ(\sqrt 2) \) is not complete. Route: \( (a + b\sqrt 2)^2 = 3 \) forces \( 2ab = 0 \) and \( a^2 + 2b^2 = 3 \), so \( 3 \) or \( 6 \) is a rational square (\( b = 0 \): \( a^2 = 3 \); \( a = 0 \): \( (2b)^2 = 6 \)); so `prp-cof-square-roots` fails for \( a = 3 \). That neither 3 nor 6 is a rational square is **proved in the text**, by the descent of F3.2's `@thm-no-rational-square-two` (least denominator by `@prp-integer-least-element`): take \( p, q \in \nZ_{>0} \) as in F3.2 with \( p^2 = nq^2 \) and \( q \) least; \( k^2 < n < (k + 1)^2 \) gives \( k < p/q < k + 1 \); put \( p' \coloneqq nq - kp \), \( q' \coloneqq p - kq \), so \( 0 < q' < q \) and \( p' > 0 \) (\( k \cdot p/q < (p/q)^2 = n \), so \( p/q < n/k \)), and \( p'^2 - nq'^2 = (k^2 - n)(p^2 - nq^2) = 0 \) (\( n = 3, k = 1 \); \( n = 6, k = 2 \); sidecar checks the identity). No proof rests on an exercise and no countability argument is used.
- **`exm-cof-sqrt2-conjugation`.** \( a + b\sqrt 2 \mapsto a - b\sqrt 2 \) is a field automorphism of \( \nQ(\sqrt 2) \), not the identity, not order-preserving (\( \sqrt 2 > 0 \mapsto -\sqrt 2 < 0 \)). The line of `thm-cof-field-automorphism-identity` that fails: \( \sqrt 2 \) is positive but not a square in \( \nQ(\sqrt 2) \): if \( c \in \nQ(\sqrt 2) \) had \( c^2 = \sqrt 2 \), then \( \sigma(c)^2 = \sigma(\sqrt 2) = -\sqrt 2 < 0 \) for the conjugation \( \sigma \), but squares in the ordered field \( \nR \) are \( \ge 0 \) (F3.2 `@prp-ordered-ring-rules` (e)). So the step "\( \sigma(a) = \sigma(b)^2 > 0 \)" cannot start. Also: by §02 there is exactly one order-preserving field map \( \nQ(\sqrt 2) \to \nR \) (the inclusion), and conjugation followed by inclusion is a second, non-order-preserving one.
- **`exm-cof-shift-automorphism`.** \( \sigma(p/q) \coloneqq p(\cdot + 1)/q(\cdot + 1) \) defines an order-preserving field automorphism of \( \nR(x) \) with \( \sigma(x) = x + 1 \ne x \). Route: (i) if \( p \) is a polynomial function, so is \( t \mapsto p(t + 1) \), by induction on the length of the coefficient list (`@ax-induction`): \( p(t) = a_0 + t \cdot p'(t) \) gives \( p(t + 1) = a_0 + (1 + t) \cdot p'(t + 1) \), a polynomial function by the induction hypothesis and `lem-cof-polynomial-closure` (a), (b); and if \( p \) has top degree \( d \) with top coefficient \( a_d \), so does \( p(\cdot + 1) \) (same induction, last entries by (b)); (ii) hence \( q(\cdot + 1) \) has a nonzero coefficient when \( q \) does (alternatively: by `lem-cof-leading-term-sign` \( q(t + 1) \ne 0 \) for all large \( t \), so its list is not all zero); (iii) \( \sigma \) is well defined and additive and multiplicative with \( \sigma(1) = 1 \), because equality at all large \( t \), sums and products are pointwise at large \( t \) and the shift \( t \mapsto t + 1 \) keeps "all large \( t \)"; (iv) \( \sigma \) preserves order, since \( f(t) > 0 \) for all large \( t \) gives \( f(t + 1) > 0 \) for all large \( t \); (v) the shift by \( -1 \) is an inverse, so \( \sigma \) is bijective (`@thm-bijection-iff-inverse`); \( \sigma(x) = x + 1 \ne x \) since \( 1 \ne 0 \). **Remark:** `cor-cof-order-automorphism-identity` needs completeness (in fact Archimedes, through `lem-cof-rationals-separate`); preserving order alone does not force the identity. Not used in any proof.

## 05-axioms-or-model.md — Assume the axioms or build a model

- **Prose section, short.** Two honest ways to start analysis: (A) *assume* there is a complete ordered field and prove theorems from the axioms (Real Analysis, an1, chapter 1, does this); (B) *build* one inside set theory (F3.1 → F3.2 → F3.10). (B) shows (A)'s axioms describe something that exists, relative to the ZF fragment of F3.1. `thm-cof-uniqueness` shows (A)'s axioms describe **one** thing. Any two models are linked by exactly one order-preserving field isomorphism, which carries every element, and with it every subset, sequence and function built from elements, across. So any statement phrased only in terms of \( + \), \( \cdot \), \( < \) (and subsets, sequences and functions built from elements) that is true in one complete ordered field is true in every one, whether or not the axioms prove it. (That theorems proved from the axioms hold in every model needs no uniqueness: it is true of any axioms, including the ordered-field axioms alone, whose models ℚ, ℚ(√2) and ℝ(x) differ, §04.) Statements about how the elements are built as sets are not carried across. So "which construction is ℝ?" has no mathematical content; "is \( \sqrt 2 \) a set of rationals?" depends on the model and is never asked again.
- **What Real Analysis takes from here.** A table: an1's assumed axioms against the fd3 result that justifies each (field and order: F3.2's definitions, F3.10's model; supremum property: F3.10, `@def-complete-ordered-field` and `@thm-reals-complete-ordered-field`; uniqueness: this chapter). an1 re-proves Archimedes, density and roots from its axioms; these are the overlaps listed below (plan edges an1 `revisits` F3.9, F3.10, F3.4).
- **Remark (no proof).** The supremum property speaks about all subsets; that is why it pins ℝ down. Properties stated only about elements (such as "every positive element is a square and every odd-degree polynomial has a root") do not: there are non-Archimedean fields with them. Pointer to the set-theory volume ST (prose).
