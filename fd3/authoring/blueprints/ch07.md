**Purpose.**
- F3.10 built ℝ from Cauchy sequences of rationals and proved it a complete ordered field; F3.4 proved any two complete ordered fields are the same. This chapter walks **the other road**, Dedekind's: a real number is the set of rationals below it.
- On this road the least upper bound property is almost free (a union of cuts is a cut), and multiplication is the hard part. On the Cauchy road it was the reverse. Seeing both is the point.
- It ends by proving that the cuts form a complete ordered field, so F3.4 gives exactly one order-preserving field isomorphism to F3.10's ℝ, with an explicit formula. **The two roads give the same ℝ.**
- The reader leaves able to (i) check the three cut conditions for a set given by a formula; (ii) run "walk up by ε" to find rationals on both sides of a cut that are close together; (iii) say which properties of ℝ each construction gets cheaply, and why.

**Signature moves** (`index.md`, "Named moves"):
- **Walk up by ε.** From a point inside a cut, add ε until you leave; the last step inside and the first outside are ε apart (`lem-cut-step`). It is how every "⊇" in this chapter is proved. (Faded triple.)
- **A cut is a downset with an open top.** Inside is closed downward, outside is closed upward, and every inside point is below every outside point (`lem-cut-separation`). Most "is it a cut?" checks are this sentence.
- **Reduce to the positive case.** Define and prove for positive cuts, then extend by signs once (`lem-cut-sign-rules`), never case by case per axiom.

## index.md

- **Title:** Constructing ℝ by Dedekind Cuts.
- **Story.** F3.10 built a real number as a class of Cauchy sequences: many rational approximations, identified when they agree in the limit. Dedekind's idea, from 1872, is shorter to state: a real number is the set of all rationals **below** it. The gap in ℚ at √2 splits ℚ into the rationals whose square is below 2 (or which are negative) and the rest; Dedekind takes the lower piece **as** √2. On this road the least upper bound property is almost a one-liner and multiplication is a page of cases, the opposite of the Cauchy road. The chapter ends by showing the two roads arrive at the same place. Motivating question: *if two people build ℝ in different ways, in what sense do they have the same numbers?*
- **What you need:** F3.2 (ℚ as an ordered field, Archimedean), F3.9 (ordered fields), and for §06 only, F3.10 and F3.4. Optional: nothing later depends on it.
- **Time:** from the B1 formula once drafted; expect about 15 hours core, 25 full.
- **Roadmap:** one sentence per section, §01–§06.
- **Named moves:** the three above.

## 01-cuts.md — Cuts

- **Hook.** In ℚ, the set \( \{ p : p \le 0 \text{ or } p^2 < 2 \} \) has no supremum (F3.2's gap: `@thm-rationals-gap-sqrt2` proves this for \( A = \{ x \in \nQ : x^2 < 2 \} \); our set is \( A \cup \{ p : p \le 0 \} \) and has the same upper bounds, because it contains A and each of its elements outside A is \( \le 0 < 1 \in A \)). It is the "set of rationals below √2", and it exists. Take it as the number.
- **`def-dedekind-cut`** (major: full explain template; slogan *a real number is the set of rationals below it*): \( \alpha \subseteq \nQ \) is a **cut** if (C1) \( \alpha \ne \emptyset \) and \( \alpha \ne \nQ \); (C2) if \( p \in \alpha \), \( q \in \nQ \) and \( q < p \), then \( q \in \alpha \); (C3) α has no largest element: for every \( p \in \alpha \) some \( p' \in \alpha \) has \( p' > p \). \( \nR_{\mathrm D} \) is the set of all cuts, a subset of \( \powerset{\nQ} \) (`@def-power-set`). In symbols too (STYLE §10), and the negation of each condition (`@prp-quantifier-negation`).
- **`def-rational-cut`:** for \( q \in \nQ \), \( D_q \coloneqq \{ r \in \nQ : r < q \} \). It is a cut: (C1) \( q - 1 \in D_q \), \( q \notin D_q \); (C2) transitivity of <; (C3) for \( r < q \), \( (r + q)/2 \) is in \( D_q \) and larger.
- **`exm-cut-sqrt2`:** \( \sigma \coloneqq \{ p \in \nQ : p \le 0 \text{ or } p^2 < 2 \} \) is a cut. (C1) \( 0 \in \sigma \), \( 2 \notin \sigma \). (C2) if \( q < p \in \sigma \): \( q \le 0 \), or \( 0 < q < p \) so \( q^2 < p^2 < 2 \) ([OF-RULES]). ## 02-order-and-completeness.md — Order by Inclusion, and Least Upper Bounds by Unions

- **`def-cut-order`:** \( \alpha \le \beta \) iff \( \alpha \subseteq \beta \); \( \alpha < \beta \) iff \( \alpha \subsetneq \beta \). Proof: the six lines of B2. Remark: ⊆ on \( \powerset{\nQ} \) is not total; the cut conditions are what make it total here.
- **`thm-dedekind-lub`** (key proof; *Try it first*): let \( A \subseteq \nR_{\mathrm D} \) be non-empty and bounded above, that is, some cut β has \( \alpha \le \beta \) for every \( \alpha \in A \) (`@def-order-bounds`). (C2): \( q < p \in \gamma \) gives \( p \in \alpha \) for some \( \alpha \in A \), so \( q \in \alpha \subseteq \gamma \). (C3): \( p \in \alpha \in A \) gives \( p' \in \alpha \), \( p' > p \). ## 03-addition.md — Addition and Negatives

- **`def-cut-sum`:** \( \alpha + \beta \coloneqq \{ p + q : p \in \alpha, q \in \beta \} \), the set sum of NOTATION.
- **`thm-cut-sum`:** for cuts α, β, \( \alpha + \beta \) is a cut; \( + \) is commutative and associative on \( \nR_{\mathrm D} \). Proof: (C1) \( a \notin \alpha \), \( b \notin \beta \) give \( p + q < a + b \) for all \( p \in \alpha \), \( q \in \beta \) (`lem-cut-separation`), so \( a + b \notin \alpha + \beta \); (C2) \( r < p + q \) gives \( r = (r - q) + q \) with \( r - q < p \); (C3) from α's. Commutativity and associativity are set equalities inherited from ℚ.
- **`prp-cut-sum-zero`:** \( \alpha + D_0 = \alpha \). ⊆: \( p + q < p \) for \( q < 0 \). ⊇: for \( p \in \alpha \) take \( p' \in \alpha \) with \( p' > p \) (C3); \( p = p' + (p - p') \) with \( p - p' \in D_0 \). Point out that (C3) is used, and that §03 C2's flawed proof skips it.
- ***Before reading on*** **prompt.** Find β with \( D_1 + \beta = D_0 \); then a recipe from α.

For σ it happens to work, which is why the error survives a test on √2. (And the set \( \{ -p : p \in \alpha \} \) is closed upward, not downward: not a cut at all.) Each set in set-builder form, never written \( -\alpha \).
- **`def-cut-negative`:** \( -\alpha \coloneqq \{ p \in \nQ : -p - r \notin \alpha \text{ for some rational } r > 0 \} \): "the negatives of the outside, pushed down by a positive margin". It is a cut: (C1) for \( b \notin \alpha \), \( -b - 1 \in -\alpha \); for \( a \in \alpha \), \( -a \notin -\alpha \) since \( a - r \in \alpha \); (C2) the complement of α is closed upward (`lem-cut-separation`); (C3) \( p \in -\alpha \) with margin \( r \) gives \( p + r/2 \in -\alpha \) with margin \( r/2 \). One line: \( -D_q = D_{-q} \).
- **`thm-cut-additive-inverse`** (key proof): for every cut α, \( \alpha + (-\alpha) = D_0 \). ⊆: for \( p \in \alpha \) and \( t \in -\alpha \) with margin \( r \), \( -t - r \notin \alpha \), so \( p < -t - r \) and \( p + t < -r < 0 \). ⊇ (**walk up by ε**, worked in full, every step named): let \( s \in \nQ \), \( s < 0 \), and \( \varepsilon \coloneqq -s/2 > 0 \). Fix \( a \in \alpha \); `lem-cut-step` gives \( p \in \alpha \) (\( p = a + n\varepsilon \)) with \( p + \varepsilon \notin \alpha \). Put \( t \coloneqq s - p \). Then \( -t - \varepsilon = p - s - \varepsilon = p + \varepsilon \notin \alpha \), so \( t \in -\alpha \) with margin ε, and \( p + t = s \). Self-explanation: why \( -s/2 \) and not \( -s \) (with margin \( -s \), \( -t - (-s) = p \in \alpha \): the margin must be strictly less than the gap). Aftermath: the proof treats rational and irrational α alike; the margin r in `def-cut-negative` is exactly what absorbs the rational case.
- **`thm-cut-ordered-group`:** \( (\nR_{\mathrm D}, +, \le) \) is an ordered abelian group: `thm-cut-sum`, `prp-cut-sum-zero`, `thm-cut-additive-inverse`, and translation in both forms: \( \alpha \le \beta \Rightarrow \alpha + \gamma \le \beta + \gamma \) (immediate from ⊆), and \( \alpha < \beta \Rightarrow \alpha + \gamma < \beta + \gamma \) (if \( \alpha + \gamma = \beta + \gamma \), add \( -\gamma \): \( \alpha = (\alpha + \gamma) + (-\gamma) = (\beta + \gamma) + (-\gamma) = \beta \), by associativity, `thm-cut-additive-inverse` and `prp-cut-sum-zero`). Consequences used in §04, proved as in any abelian group (one line each): inverses are unique, \( -(-\alpha) = \alpha \), \( \alpha > D_0 \iff -\alpha < D_0 \).

## 04-multiplication.md — Multiplication

- **Lead-in.** The naive set \( \{ pq : p \in \alpha, q \in \beta \} \) is useless. For \( \alpha = \beta = D_1 \) (indeed whenever both cuts contain 0) it is all of ℚ, because negatives times negatives are unbounded above. For \( \alpha = \beta = D_{-1} \) it is \( \{ x \in \nQ : x > 1 \} \), which is not even a cut. So define for positive cuts first, then extend by signs.
- **`def-cut-product-positive`:** for \( \alpha, \beta > D_0 \) (equivalently \( 0 \in \alpha \), \( 0 \in \beta \), by `prp-rational-in-cut`), \( \alpha\beta \coloneqq \{ p \in \nQ : p \le rs \text{ for some } r \in \alpha, s \in \beta \text{ with } r > 0, s > 0 \} \).
- **`prp-cut-product-positive`:** for \( \alpha, \beta, \gamma > D_0 \): αβ is a cut and \( \alpha\beta > D_0 \); \( \alpha\beta = \beta\alpha \); \( (\alpha\beta)\gamma = \alpha(\beta\gamma) \) (both are \( \{ p : p \le rst \} \) over positive \( r \in \alpha, s \in \beta, t \in \gamma \)); \( \alpha D_1 = \alpha \) (⊇: for \( 0 < p \in \alpha \), take \( p' \in \alpha \), \( p' > p \), and \( p = p' \cdot (p/p') \)). Cut: (C1) with \( a \notin \alpha \), \( b \notin \beta \), \( rs < ab \); (C2) built in; (C3) \( p \le rs < r's \) for \( r' \in \alpha \), \( r' > r \).
- **`lem-cut-multiplicative-step`:** let \( \alpha > D_0 \) and \( \mu \in \nQ \), \( \mu > 1 \). Then some \( r \in \alpha \) with \( r > 0 \) has \( \mu r \notin \alpha \). Proof: (C3) gives \( a \in \alpha \), \( a > 0 \); `lem-cut-step` with \( \varepsilon \coloneqq (\mu - 1)a \) gives \( r = a + n\varepsilon \ge a \) with \( r + \varepsilon \notin \alpha \); \( \mu r = r + (\mu - 1)r \ge r + \varepsilon \), so \( \mu r \notin \alpha \) (`lem-cut-separation`).
- **`lem-cut-distributive-positive`:** for \( \alpha, \beta, \gamma > D_0 \), \( \alpha(\beta + \gamma) = \alpha\beta + \alpha\gamma \). ⊆: \( p \le ru \), \( u = s + t \); raise \( s, t \) to positive elements \( \tilde s \ge s \), \( \tilde t \ge t \) of β, γ (both contain \( 0 \), and (C3)), so \( p \le r\tilde s + r\tilde t \in \alpha\beta + \alpha\gamma \), a cut, closed downward. ⊇: \( x \le r_1 s \), \( y \le r_2 t \); with \( r \coloneqq \max(r_1, r_2) \in \alpha \), \( x + y \le r(s + t) \).
- **`def-cut-product`:** \( \abs{\alpha} \coloneqq \alpha \) if \( \alpha \ge D_0 \), else \( -\alpha \); \( \operatorname{sgn} \alpha \coloneqq 1, 0, -1 \) as \( \alpha >, =, < D_0 \) (`thm-cut-order-total`). For cuts α, β: \( \alpha\beta \coloneqq D_0 \) if either is \( D_0 \); \( \abs{\alpha}\abs{\beta} \) if their signs agree; \( -(\abs{\alpha}\abs{\beta}) \) if they differ. It agrees with `def-cut-product-positive` on positive cuts.
- **`lem-cut-sign-rules`:** for all cuts α, β: \( \alpha = \operatorname{sgn}(\alpha)\abs{\alpha} \) (reading \( 1 \cdot \xi = \xi \), \( 0 \cdot \xi = D_0 \), \( (-1) \cdot \xi = -\xi \)); \( \abs{\alpha\beta} = \abs{\alpha}\abs{\beta} \); \( \operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\beta) = \operatorname{sgn}\alpha \operatorname{sgn}\beta \); \( (-\alpha)\beta = -(\alpha\beta) = \alpha(-\beta) \). Proof by the definition's cases, using \( \abs{\alpha}\abs{\beta} > D_0 \) for non-zero α, β and the group facts of `thm-cut-ordered-group`. Then **commutativity and associativity for all cuts** follow in two lines each: both sides have absolute value \( \abs{\alpha}\abs{\beta}\abs{\gamma} \) and sign \( \operatorname{sgn}\alpha\operatorname{sgn}\beta\operatorname{sgn}\gamma \).
- **`lem-cut-distributive`:** for all cuts α, β, γ, \( \alpha(\beta + \gamma) = \alpha\beta + \alpha\gamma \). Proof: for \( \alpha > D_0 \), write \( f(\xi) \coloneqq \alpha\xi \); f is additive on cuts \( \ge D_0 \) (`lem-cut-distributive-positive`, zero cases trivial) and \( f(-\xi) = -f(\xi) \) (`lem-cut-sign-rules`). Cases: β, γ both \( \le D_0 \): negate. \( \beta \ge D_0 \ge \gamma \) with \( \beta + \gamma \ge D_0 \): \( \beta = (\beta + \gamma) + (-\gamma) \), both \( \ge D_0 \), so \( f(\beta) = f(\beta + \gamma) - f(\gamma) \). ## 05-rationals-inside.md — ℚ Inside the Cuts, and a Complete Ordered Field

- **`thm-rationals-embed-cuts`:** the map \( q \mapsto D_q \), \( \nQ \to \nR_{\mathrm D} \), is injective and satisfies, for all \( p, q \in \nQ \): \( p < q \iff D_p < D_q \); \( D_p + D_q = D_{p+q} \); \( -D_q = D_{-q} \); \( D_p D_q = D_{pq} \); \( D_1 \) is the one of `thm-cuts-ordered-field`. So it is \( \iota_{\nR_{\mathrm D}} \) of [Q-EMB] (that map is unique). Proofs: sum ⊇: \( x < p + q \), \( \delta \coloneqq (p + q - x)/2 \), \( x = (p - \delta) + (q - \delta) \); product for \( p, q > 0 \), ⊇: for \( 0 < x < pq \), \( \lambda \coloneqq (1 + x/(pq))/2 \), \( r \coloneqq \lambda p \), \( s \coloneqq x/(\lambda p) < q \), \( rs = x \); other signs by `lem-cut-sign-rules`.
- **`prp-cut-union-of-rationals`:** every cut is the supremum of the rational cuts below it: \( \alpha = \bigcup \{ D_q : q \in \alpha \} = \sup \{ D_q : q \in \alpha \} \) (by (C3) and `thm-dedekind-lub`).
- **`thm-cut-rationals-dense`** (*Try it first*): for cuts \( \alpha < \beta \) there is \( q \in \nQ \) with \( \alpha < D_q < \beta \). Proof: take \( p \in \beta \setminus \alpha \), \( p' \in \beta \) with \( p' > p \) (C3), \( q \coloneqq (p + p')/2 \); then \( \alpha \subseteq D_p \subsetneq D_q \subsetneq D_{p'} \subseteq \beta \) (`prp-rational-in-cut`).
- **`prp-cuts-archimedean`:** for every cut α there is \( n \in \nN \) with \( \alpha < D_n \). (\( b \notin \alpha \), [Q-ARCH] gives \( n > b \), so \( \alpha \subseteq D_b \subsetneq D_n \).) So \( \nR_{\mathrm D} \) is Archimedean, directly; F3.4 also gets it from completeness.
- **`prp-cut-sqrt2-square`:** \( \sigma > D_0 \) and \( \sigma\sigma = D_2 \); consequently \( \sigma \ne D_q \) for every \( q \in \nQ \). ⊆: for positive \( r, s \in \sigma \), \( (rs)^2 < 4 \), so \( rs < 2 \). ⊇ for \( x \in \nQ \): \( x \le 0 \) trivially; \( 0 < x < 1 \) with \( r = 1 \); \( 1 \le x < 2 \): `lem-cut-multiplicative-step` on σ with \( \mu \coloneqq 1 + (2/x - 1)/3 \) (\( \mu^2 < 2/x \) because \( 2/x \le 2 \); sidecar checks it) gives \( 0 < r \in \sigma \) with \( (\mu r)^2 \ge 2 \), so \( r^2 \ge 2/\mu^2 > x \); then \( s \coloneqq x/r \) has \( 0 < s < r \), so \( s \in \sigma \), and \( rs = x \). Consequence: \( \sigma = D_q \) forces \( q > 0 \) and \( D_{q^2} = D_2 \) (`thm-rationals-embed-cuts`), so \( q^2 = 2 \), against `@thm-no-rational-square-two`. One remark: this is the second proof of §01 C1, with no Newton step.
- **`thm-cuts-complete-field`:** \( \nR_{\mathrm D} \) is a complete ordered field in the sense of [COF]. Proof: `thm-cuts-ordered-field`; its order is ⊆, so suprema in the sense of [COF] are those of `thm-dedekind-lub`.
- **Warning.** "Dense" does not mean every cut is rational; σ is not.

## 06-two-roads.md — The Two Roads Give the Same ℝ

- **`thm-dedekind-same-reals`** (key proof). Let \( \nR_{\mathrm C} \) be F3.10's model and \( \iota \coloneqq \iota_{\nR_{\mathrm C}} \) ([Q-EMB]). Then: (a) there is exactly one order-preserving field isomorphism \( \Psi \colon \nR_{\mathrm C} \to \nR_{\mathrm D} \); (b) \( \Psi(\iota(q)) = D_q \) for every \( q \in \nQ \); (c) \( \Psi(x) = \nQ_{\nR_{\mathrm C}}(x) = \{ q \in \nQ : \iota(q) < x \} \) for every \( x \in \nR_{\mathrm C} \) ([Q-BELOW]). Proof. (a) \( \nR_{\mathrm C} \) is a complete ordered field ([RC]) and so is \( \nR_{\mathrm D} \) (`thm-cuts-complete-field`); apply [UNIQ]. (b) [Q-EMB] with \( \chi \coloneqq \Psi \) gives \( \Psi \circ \iota = \iota_{\nR_{\mathrm D}} \), and \( \iota_{\nR_{\mathrm D}}(q) = D_q \) (`thm-rationals-embed-cuts`). (c) \( q \in \Psi(x) \iff D_q < \Psi(x) \) (`prp-rational-in-cut`) \( \iff \Psi(\iota(q)) < \Psi(x) \) (b) \( \iff \iota(q) < x \) (Ψ is an order-preserving bijection between totally ordered sets, so it also reflects <: one line). - **Aftermath (prose).** (c) says the cut attached to a Cauchy real is "the rationals below it": Dedekind's definition read off from the other model. In the converse direction, \( \Psi^{-1}(\alpha) = \sup_{\nR_{\mathrm C}} \{ \iota(q) : q \in \alpha \} \) (by [EMB-C] with \( F \coloneqq \nR_{\mathrm D} \), Archimedean by [EMB-C]'s second clause and `thm-cuts-complete-field`, and \( L \coloneqq \nR_{\mathrm C} \): \( \Psi^{-1} \) is an order-preserving field map, so it is that theorem's unique φ, and \( \nQ_{\nR_{\mathrm D}}(\alpha) = \{ q : D_q < \alpha \} = \alpha \) by `thm-rationals-embed-cuts` and `prp-rational-in-cut`; C2(b) below proves the same fact again, directly, for any Archimedean F). - **`exm-two-roads-sqrt2`:** let \( x \in \nR_{\mathrm C} \) with \( x > 0 \) and \( x^2 = \iota(2) \) (F3.10's `@exm-sqrt2-class`, tag [SQRT2-C]: its \( r = [(x_n)] \) has \( r > 0 \) and \( r^2 = \kappa(2) \), and \( \kappa = \iota \) by F3.10 `@thm-rational-embedding` (d), [RC]).
