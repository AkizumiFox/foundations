**Purpose.**
- F3.9 ended with the Newton sequence, Cauchy in ℚ with no rational limit (`@thm-sqrt2-cauchy-no-rational-limit`). This chapter builds the number it aims at. **Slogan:** *a real number is a Cauchy sequence of rationals, up to a null difference.*
- Define \( \nR \coloneqq \mathcal{C}/{\sim} \) with fd2's quotient sets; define \( + \), \( \cdot \), \( < \) on representatives and prove each well defined; prove ℝ is an ordered field containing a copy of ℚ, Archimedean, with ℚ dense.
- Prove both forms of completeness: every Cauchy sequence in ℝ converges (with **no use of the axiom of choice**), and every non-empty subset bounded above has a supremum (from Cauchy completeness and Archimedes, by bisection). Conclude: ℝ is a **complete ordered field**.
- **Nothing about ℝ is assumed.** Until §04 every ε, δ, bound and threshold is rational; real ε appear only once ℝ exists, and §04 shows rational ones suffice.
- Prose pointers forward only: F3.4 (uniqueness), F3.3 (optional, cuts), Real Analysis (its axioms now have a model), F4.1 (ℂ on this ℝ), an2 (completion of a metric space).
- The reader leaves able to (i) prove an operation on classes well defined; (ii) pass between "eventually ≥ δ" for sequences and "> 0" for classes, and say why "termwise >" is not enough; (iii) build a rational approximant of a real number by a rule, not a choice; (iv) name the line where Archimedes enters the bisection proof.

**Signature moves** (`index.md`, "Named moves").
- **Representatives, then independence.** Define on representatives; show the output class survives replacing each input by an equivalent one. (Main move: the faded triple.)
- **A term approximates its class.** For \( a \in \mathcal{C} \), the rationals \( a_n \) converge in ℝ to \( [a] \).
- **ε/3 through an approximant.** Compare two far-apart objects through rational approximants of each.
- **Bisect and keep the invariant.** Halve an interval by a rule that keeps "right end is an upper bound, left end is not".

## 01-cauchy-sequences-of-rationals.md — Sequences That Should Converge

- **Hook.** Two rational sequences aiming at the same hole: F3.2's Newton sequence \( (x_n) \) (`@exm-newton-rationals-sqrt2`; \( x_0 = 2 \), \( x_{n+1} = x_n/2 + 1/x_n \)) and the decimal truncations \( d_k \coloneqq m_k/10^k \), \( m_k \) the largest natural number with \( m_k^2 \le 2 \cdot (10^k)^2 \) (no \( 10^{2k} \) is written: the text uses no exponent law in ℚ). Existence: `@prp-integer-least-element` applied to \( S_k \coloneqq \{ m \in \nZ : m \ge 0,\ m^2 > 2 \cdot (10^k)^2 \} \), bounded below by \( 0 \) and non-empty, since it contains \( 2 \cdot 10^k \): \( (2 \cdot 10^k)^2 = 4 (10^k)^2 > 2 (10^k)^2 \) because \( (10^k)^2 \ge 1 > 0 \) (from \( 10^k \ge 1 \), below); its least element \( m^\ast \) is \( \ge 1 \) (\( 0 \notin S_k \)), and \( m_k \coloneqq m^\ast - 1 \) is the largest natural number outside \( S_k \) (every \( m \ge m^\ast \) has \( m^2 \ge (m^\ast)^2 \), `@prp-ordered-ring-rules` (g)). Here \( 10^k \) is the power in ℚ (`@def-naturals-in-ordered-field`, \( F = \nQ \): \( 10^0 = 1 \), \( 10^{k+1} = 10^k \cdot 10 \)), not fd2's power over school ℝ; it lies in ℤ by `@ax-induction` (\( 10^0 = 1 \); ℤ is closed under products in ℚ, `@prp-integers-embed-rationals`). The text writes \( 1/10^k \) throughout, never \( 10^{-k} \) (no negative exponents are defined). Different sequences, one intended number.
- **`def-rational-cauchy-sequences`:** \( \mathcal{C} \) is the set of sequences \( a \colon \nN \to \nQ \) (`@def-sequence-ordered-field`, \( F = \nQ \)) that are Cauchy in ℚ (`@def-cauchy-sequence-ordered-field`), written out with \( \varepsilon \in \nQ_{>0} \). "Null" is `@def-null-sequence-ordered-field` with \( F = \nQ \).
- **The toolkit from F3.9, with \( F = \nQ \)** (one boxed list, no new label, each item with its `@ref`): constants and null sequences are in \( \mathcal{C} \), and \( \mathcal{C} \) is closed under \( + \), \( - \), \( \cdot \) (`@thm-cauchy-algebra-ordered-field`); sums, negatives and constant multiples of null sequences are null (`@thm-limit-laws-ordered-field` (a), (b)); null times bounded is null (`@prp-null-times-bounded-ordered-field`); members of \( \mathcal{C} \) are bounded (`@prp-cauchy-bounded-ordered-field`); changing finitely many terms changes nothing (`@prp-tail-change-ordered-field`); a sequence squeezed between \( 0 \) and a null sequence is null (`@prp-limit-preserves-order-ordered-field` (c)).
- **`def-cauchy-equivalent`:** for \( a, b \in \mathcal{C} \), \( a \sim b \) iff \( a - b \) is null. Slogan: *two Cauchy sequences are equivalent when their difference dies out.*
- **`prp-cauchy-equivalent-relation`:** \( \sim \) is an equivalence relation on \( \mathcal{C} \) (`@def-equivalence-relation`). Reflexive (the zero sequence); symmetric (\( b - a = -(a - b) \)); transitive (\( a - c = (a - b) + (b - c) \), the sum rule `@thm-limit-laws-ordered-field` (a)). ## 03-ordering-the-reals.md — Positive Classes and the Order

- **Hook: the naive order fails.** `exm-strict-termwise-order-fails`: \( a_n = 1/(n+1) > 0 = b_n \) for every \( n \), yet \( [a] = [b] \); so "\( [a] < [b] \) iff \( a_n < b_n \) eventually" is not even well defined.
- **`def-positive-cauchy-sequence`:** \( a \in \mathcal{C} \) is positive if \( \exists \delta \in \nQ_{>0}\ \exists N \in \nN\ \forall n \ge N\ a_n \ge \delta \). Slogan: *eventually above a fixed positive rational.* Contrast in symbols with \( \exists N\ \forall n \ge N\ a_n > 0 \), the quantifier-order point (STYLE §10).
- **`prp-positivity-well-defined`:** if \( a \) is positive and \( a' \sim a \), then \( a' \) is positive (`@prp-sign-stable-ordered-field`, \( F = \nQ \)). So \( P \coloneqq \{ [a] : a \text{ positive} \} \) is meaningful: \( x \in P \) iff every representative of \( x \) is positive.
- **`thm-reals-positive-cone`.** (i) For every \( x \in \nR \), exactly one of \( x \in P \), \( x = 0_{\nR} \), \( -x \in P \). (ii) \( x, y \in P \Rightarrow x + y \in P \) and \( xy \in P \). Route: (i) "at least one" by `@thm-cauchy-not-null-sign` when \( a \) is not null; "at most one": a positive sequence is not null, and no sequence is eventually \( \ge \delta \) and eventually \( \le -\delta' \). - **`def-reals-order`:** \( x < y \) iff \( y - x \in P \).
- **`thm-reals-ordered-field`:** \( (\nR, +, \cdot, <) \) is an ordered field (`@def-ordered-ring-and-field`). (O1) from (i) for \( y - x \); (O2) from \( z - x = (z - y) + (y - x) \); (O3) from \( (y + z) - (x + z) = y - x \); (O4) from (ii). One line each. - **`lem-termwise-order`.** If \( a, b \in \mathcal{C} \) and \( a_n \le b_n \) for every \( n \ge N_0 \), then \( [a] \le [b] \). By ¬I (point back to `@def-rule-raa` for why it is not RAA): if \( [b] < [a] \), then \( a - b \) is positive (`prp-positivity-well-defined`), so \( a_n - b_n \ge \delta > 0 \) at \( n = \max(N, N_0) \). **Warning** directly after: the strict version fails (`exm-strict-termwise-order-fails`).

## 04-rationals-inside-the-reals.md — ℚ Inside ℝ: Archimedes and Density

- **`def-rational-embedding`:** \( \kappa(q) \coloneqq [(q)_n] \).
- **`thm-rational-embedding`.** For \( q, r \in \nQ \): (a) \( \kappa(q + r) = \kappa(q) + \kappa(r) \), \( \kappa(qr) = \kappa(q)\kappa(r) \), \( \kappa(0) = 0_{\nR} \), \( \kappa(1) = 1_{\nR} \); (b) \( q < r \iff \kappa(q) < \kappa(r) \), hence κ is injective; (c) \( \kappa(\abs{q}) = \abs{\kappa(q)} \), \( \kappa(-q) = -\kappa(q) \), \( \kappa(1/q) = \kappa(q)^{-1} \) for \( q \ne 0 \); (d) κ is the embedding \( \iota_{\nR} \) of `@thm-rationals-embed-ordered-field` for \( F = \nR \) (by (a) and its uniqueness clause); in particular \( \kappa(n) = n \cdot 1_{\nR} \) (`@def-naturals-in-ordered-field`) for \( n \in \nN \). Route: (b) \( (\Rightarrow) \) with \( \delta = r - q \), \( (\Leftarrow) \) by trichotomy in ℚ.
- **`thm-constructed-reals-archimedean`:** ℝ is Archimedean (`@def-archimedean-ordered-field`). \( x = [a] \); \( \abs{a_k} \le M \in \nQ_{>0} \) (`@prp-cauchy-bounded-ordered-field`); `@thm-rationals-archimedean` gives \( n \in \nN \) with \( M < n \); then \( (n + 1) - a_k \ge 1 \) for all \( k \), so \( (n + 1) \cdot 1_{\nR} - x = \kappa(n + 1) - x \in P \) (by (d)).
- **Integer part in ℝ** (no new label; recall F3.9): by `thm-constructed-reals-archimedean`, `@prp-integer-part-ordered-field` applies with \( F = \nR \): for every \( x \in \nR \) there is **exactly one** \( m \in \nZ \) with \( \kappa(m) \le x < \kappa(m + 1) \), called the integer part of \( x \). §05 uses the uniqueness. One sentence comparing F3.2's rational version (`@prp-rational-integer-part`).
- **`thm-constructed-rationals-dense`** (*Try it first*). If \( x, y \in \nR \) and \( x < y \), some \( q \in \nQ \) has \( x < \kappa(q) < y \).

  - Route: \( x = [a] \), \( y = [b] \); \( b - a \) represents \( y - x \in P \), so (`prp-positivity-well-defined`) \( b_n - a_n \ge \delta \) for \( n \ge N_1 \); \( N \ge N_1 \) with \( \abs{a_m - a_n}, \abs{b_m - b_n} < \delta/4 \) for \( m, n \ge N \); \( q \coloneqq (a_N + b_N)/2 \). For \( n \ge N \): \( q - a_n = (a_N - a_n) + (b_N - a_N)/2 > \delta/4 \), and \( b_n - q > \delta/4 \). So \( \kappa(q) - x \) and \( y - \kappa(q) \) are positive.

One sentence (cite F3.9): since ℝ is Archimedean, `@thm-rationals-dense-ordered-field` with \( F = \nR \) gives the same conclusion; the direct proof is kept because it shows density without Archimedes, and it carries the try-it-first. Every later use of density in fd3 (F3.4) cites F3.9's general theorem, not this one.

## 05-completeness.md — Every Cauchy Sequence of Reals Converges

- **Hook.** We built ℝ to fill the holes in ℚ. Could ℝ have holes of its own, Cauchy sequences of *reals* crowding around nothing? Then the construction would have to be repeated for ever. It need not be.
- **`thm-constructed-reals-cauchy-complete`** (key proof; *Try it first*). Every Cauchy sequence \( (x_k)_{k \in \nN} \) in ℝ (`@def-cauchy-sequence-ordered-field`, \( F = \nR \)) converges in ℝ.
  - *Idea:* the attempt "pick a representative of each \( x_k \) and take a diagonal term" fails (C2). Instead replace \( x_k \) by a rational \( q_k \) within \( 1/(k+1) \) of it, chosen by a rule; the \( q_k \) form a member of \( \mathcal{C} \) (ε/3 through the approximants), hence a real number \( x \); and \( x_k \) is near \( q_k \), which is near \( x \).
  - **The approximant, without choice.** \( m_k \coloneqq \) **the** integer part of \( (k+1) x_k \) (`@prp-integer-part-ordered-field` with \( F = \nR \), §04), \( q_k \coloneqq m_k/(k+1) \). Uniqueness makes \( k \mapsto q_k \) a function given by a rule (`@def-function`): no use of `@ax-choice`. Then \( q_k \le x_k < q_k + 1/(k+1) \) (`thm-rational-embedding`, dividing by \( k + 1 > 0 \)).
  - Proof. (1) \( (q_k) \in \mathcal{C} \): for \( \varepsilon \in \nQ_{>0} \), \( K_1 \) with \( 1/(K_1 + 1) < \varepsilon/3 \) (`@prp-archimedean-reciprocal` in ℚ), \( K_2 \) with \( \abs{x_j - x_k} < \varepsilon/3 \) for \( j, k \ge K_2 \) (the Cauchy property in ℝ at \( \kappa(\varepsilon/3) \)); for \( j, k \ge \max(K_1, K_2) \), the triangle inequality in ℝ (`@prp-absolute-value-ordered-field` (e)) gives \( \abs{q_j - q_k} < \varepsilon \) in ℝ, hence in ℚ (`thm-rational-embedding` (b), (c)). (2) \( x \coloneqq [(q_k)] \). Fix \( n \ge 1 \) (`cor-rational-epsilon-suffices` (b)): `thm-terms-converge-to-class` gives \( K_3 \) with \( \abs{q_k - x} < 1/(2n) \) for \( k \ge K_3 \); and \( \abs{x_k - q_k} < 1/(k+1) \le 1/(2n) \) for \( k \ge 2n - 1 \). So \( \abs{x_k - x} < 1/n \) for \( k \ge \max(K_3, 2n - 1) \).
- **`exm-representatives-need-choice`** (after the proof). "For each \( k \) pick \( a^{(k)} \in x_k \)" picks one element from each of infinitely many classes with no rule: an instance of `@ax-choice`, which the proof above does not use.
- **Warning: the uncontrolled diagonal.** \( a^{(k)}_k \) need not be near \( x_k \): a representative may start with any finite list of rationals (`exm-finite-change-equivalent`).

## 06-the-least-upper-bound-property.md — ℝ Is a Complete Ordered Field

- **`def-complete-ordered-field`:** an ordered field F (`@def-ordered-ring-and-field`) is **complete** if every non-empty subset of F that is bounded above (`@def-order-bounds`, for the total order \( \le \) of F, `@def-partial-order`) has a supremum in F (`@def-order-supremum`). Terminology fixed here: "every Cauchy sequence converges" is **Cauchy-complete**.
- **`lem-power-two-bound`:** in any ordered field F, \( (2 \cdot 1_F)^k \ge (k + 1) \cdot 1_F \) for every \( k \in \nN \), the power being F3.9's \( x^k \) in F (`@def-naturals-in-ordered-field`, recursion with \( X = F \)); stated inside F only, with no power in ℕ and no use of fd2's power, which is defined over school ℝ. Proof by `@ax-induction` with base \( P(0) \): \( 1_F \ge 1_F \); step: \( (2 \cdot 1_F)^{k+1} = (2 \cdot 1_F)^k (2 \cdot 1_F) \ge ((k + 1) \cdot 1_F)(2 \cdot 1_F) \) (the defining equation; \( 2 \cdot 1_F > 0 \), `@prp-ordered-ring-rules`), and \( ((k + 1) \cdot 1_F)(2 \cdot 1_F) = (k + 1) \cdot 1_F + (k + 1) \cdot 1_F \ge (k + 1) \cdot 1_F + 1_F = (k + 2) \cdot 1_F \), using \( 2 \cdot 1_F = 1_F + 1_F \) and distributivity in F, \( (k + 1) \cdot 1_F \ge 1_F \) (`@prp-naturals-in-ordered-field` (b)) and the sum law (a). The induction is the lemma's own proof. ℕ-arithmetic used, ledgered "F3.1 (promised): (ℕ1), (ℕ3)": \( 1 + 1 = 2 \), \( (k + 1) + 1 = k + 2 \) and \( 1 \le k + 1 \). Below, \( 2^k \) means \( (2 \cdot 1_F)^k \).
- **`thm-cauchy-archimedean-lub`** (key proof). Let F be an ordered field that is Archimedean (`@def-archimedean-ordered-field`) and Cauchy-complete. Then F is complete.
  - Route (bisection). \( A \ne \emptyset \) bounded above; fix \( a_0 \in A \) and an upper bound \( M \) (two \( \exists \)E, tagged: not choice). \( l_0 \coloneqq a_0 - 1 \), \( u_0 \coloneqq M \), \( D \coloneqq u_0 - l_0 > 0 \). By `@thm-recursion`, with step \( g(l, u) \coloneqq (l, c) \) if the midpoint \( c \coloneqq (l + u)/2 \) (`@prp-ordered-field-halves` (b)) is an upper bound of \( A \), else \( (c, u) \): a function by cases, no choice. Invariants by induction: \( u_k \) is an upper bound, \( l_k \) is not; \( u_k - l_k = D/2^k \); \( l_k \le l_{k+1} \), \( u_{k+1} \le u_k \); \( l_k < u_k \) (some \( a \in A \) has \( l_k < a \le u_k \)).
  - \( (u_k) \) is Cauchy: for \( j \ge k \), \( 0 \le u_k - u_j \le u_k - l_k = D/2^k \). Given \( \varepsilon \in F_{>0} \), **Archimedes** gives \( n \in \nN \) with \( D/\varepsilon < n \cdot 1_F \); for \( k \ge n \), `lem-power-two-bound` gives \( D/2^k < \varepsilon \). So \( u_k \to s \in F \), and \( l_k = u_k - D/2^k \to s \) (`@thm-limit-laws-ordered-field`).
  - \( s \) is an upper bound: for \( a \in A \), \( u_k \ge a \) for all \( k \), so \( s \ge a \) (`@prp-limit-preserves-order-ordered-field` (b)). \( s \) is least: if an upper bound \( b \) had \( b < s \), some \( l_k > b \) (\( l_k \to s \), \( \varepsilon = s - b \)); \( l_k \) is not an upper bound, so some \( a \in A \) exceeds \( l_k > b \): contradiction; trichotomy gives \( s \le b \) for every upper bound \( b \).
- **`thm-reals-complete-ordered-field`** (the chapter's result). ℝ with the operations of `def-reals-operations` and the order of `def-reals-order` is a complete ordered field; \( \kappa \colon \nQ \to \nR \) is injective, preserves \( + \), \( \cdot \), \( < \), and has dense image. Proof: `thm-reals-ordered-field`, `thm-rational-embedding`, `thm-constructed-rationals-dense`, `thm-constructed-reals-archimedean`, `thm-constructed-reals-cauchy-complete`, `thm-cauchy-archimedean-lub`.
- **Warning (false conclusion).** "Every Cauchy-complete ordered field is complete." Run the bisection proof without Archimedes and find the unjustified line (\( D/\varepsilon < n \cdot 1_F \)). Prose remark, explicitly marked **"not proved here"** in the text: there are Cauchy-complete ordered fields that are not Archimedean (for instance formal Laurent series in a positive infinitesimal \( t \)); Real Analysis returns to this when it compares forms of completeness. **Nothing rests on it**: no proof, exercise or solution in fd3 uses the claim, and it gets no label.
